## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Cooperation learning; trust repair; sustained cooperation in adolescence; effects of partner adaptivity and social context on cooperation strategies
- **study_focus_short:** Cooperation learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from a partner's reciprocation behavior to sustain or repair cooperation over repeated interactions   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (trustee / partner)     - Source content (social): action/policy (reciprocation or defection decisions)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (partner)     - Target content (social): action/policy (expected reciprocation behavior / trustworthiness)
- **task_description:** Adolescents (ages 8-18) played a child-friendly iterative trust game ("dollar game") across 20 rounds with the same partner. As investors, they chose how many coins (0-5) to share; trustees could reciprocate or defect. Participants played under five conditions: fixed computer partners (30% and 70% reciprocation), a real peer partner, and adaptive computer algorithms under social (told it was peer) vs. non-social (told it was computer) contexts.
- **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Single agent (participant as investor), dyadic (trustee partner — real peer or computer algorithm); multi-condition (fixed, adaptive non-social, adaptive social, real social)
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Coin endowments (1-5 coins), binary/continuous cooperation decisions, partner reciprocation feedback, child-friendly game interface
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural (in-lab, two sites: Yale and George Washington University)
- **main_result:** - Adolescents more generous with adaptive vs fixed partners (beta = 0.44, SE = 0.16, p = 0.008) - Adolescents more generous in social vs non-social condition (beta = 0.45, SE = 0.12, p < 0.001) - Adolescents repaired trust more with adaptive partners (generosity after defecting: beta = 0.50, SE = 0.20, p = 0.01) - Adolescents reinitiated cooperation more in social vs non-social condition (freq: beta = 0.14, SE = 0.03, p < 0.001; generosity: beta = 0.65, SE = 0.15, p = 0.001) - Sustained cooperation more with adaptive vs fixed partners (time x adaptivity: beta = 0.04, SE = 0.01, p = 0.001) - T4t full model won in social condition; t4t fixed model won in fixed and non-social conditions - Quadratic age effect on confidence parameter in social condition (beta = 0.0003, SE = 0.005, p = 0.03)
- **effect_size:** - Generosity adaptive vs fixed: beta = 0.44 - Generosity social vs non-social: beta = 0.45 - Trust repair (adaptive): beta = 0.50 - Trust repair frequency (social): beta = 0.14 - Trust repair generosity (social): beta = 0.65 - Sustained cooperation (time x adaptivity): beta = 0.04 - Age^2 on confidence parameter: beta = 0.0003

- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Social condition involves deception (told playing with peer, actually computer); real peer condition only played once (always first); two different research sites with slight procedural differences; adaptive algorithms not based on real human trustee behavior; small sample given wide age range (8-18); participants in real social condition played different roles (investor vs trustee) which was not controlled for; no model or parameter recovery reported for this study
