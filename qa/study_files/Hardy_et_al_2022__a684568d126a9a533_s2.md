## Quality
- **sample_size:** N=1000 (across 5 conditions, 8 networks total, 10 generations each); Amazon Mechanical Turk; US-based.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Less idealized than Experiment 1 (no resampling allowed, evidence censored by predecessor's choices, all information social) but still highly controlled. Binary part-selection task is simplified relative to real-world cultural transmission. Evidence correlated within generation by design.
- **eligibility_flag:** Same as Study 1 -- borderline. Social context is the transmission mechanism, not the learning target. Learning content is non-social (spaceship part quality). Model has no free parameters.
- **concerns:** - Same as Study 1: no free parameters, no individual-level fitting. - In Condition 3 (high evidence strength), naive copying outperformed social sampling, suggesting the model may not capture behavior when evidence is strong. - Evidence was censored: participants only observed outcomes for parts their predecessor included, introducing an information bottleneck not fully addressed by the model. - Supplement referenced but not accessible.
- **limitations_reported:** Same as Study 1 (general discussion covers both experiments): "We tested our model on a simple binary-choice decision-making task where the transmission structure was explicitly designed to match our model assumptions"; "These simplifications limit our ability to draw general conclusions about information accumulation in natural populations"; "People's sampling, and exploration strategies may depend on the task and are likely considerably more complex in naturalistic domains and networks"; "People may not always transmit their true beliefs.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; task simplicity; artificial transmission structure; limited generalizability; idealized assumptions about belief transmission; information censoring not modeled.
