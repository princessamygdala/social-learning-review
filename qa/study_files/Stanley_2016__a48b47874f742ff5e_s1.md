## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 26 (from 30 scanned; 4 excluded: 1 excessive head motion, 3 poor behavioral performance; all female; median age = 23.5 years, range 19-37)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 19–37
- **ecological_validity:** Participants observed real people's real decisions with actual monetary consequences, enhancing ecological validity. However, the social condition was "minimally social" by design — distinguished from non-social only by a face and knowledge of viewing real individuals' behavior. The task involved simple binary outcomes (share/keep) lacking the abstraction and complexity of real-world social behavior. No direct interaction between participant and Gifters.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - The computational model is an ideal Bayesian observer used as a benchmark, not a fitted model — there is no formal model fitting or model comparison. The parametric regressors (SPE, predictability) are derived directly from participant estimates, not from a fitted model. - All participants were female (to match Gifter gender), limiting generalizability. - Only 1 model tested (Bayesian ideal observer as benchmark), no formal model comparison. - SPE and predictability regressors derived from participant behavior rather than model-generated predictions, which conflates participant noise with model predictions. - Right caudate SPE finding only trending (p = 0.058 SVC).
- **limitations_reported:** First, the social condition was minimally social, distinguished from non-social learning only by the presence of a face and participant knowledge that they were viewing behavior of real individuals, requiring only simple stimulus-outcome associativity with little abstraction or complexity found in real-world social behavior; Second, no regions reflected predictability specifically for Gifters and not Lotteries, and no person-specific learning signals were found in TPJ, possibly because participants were not required to actively mentalize; Third, the authors note that future work should incorporate abstract nature of real-world social behaviors into more quantitative computational models.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; task simplicity; limited generalizability (all-female sample); minimal social manipulation; no active mentalizing requirement; limited computational modeling (ideal observer only)
