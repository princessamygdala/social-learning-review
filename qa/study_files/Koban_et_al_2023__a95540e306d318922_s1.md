## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social approval learning; biased social learning of self-perception in social anxiety disorder. How positive versus negative social feedback alters self-evaluation and state self-esteem, and how these processes differ in SAD versus healthy controls.
- **study_focus_short:** Social approval learning · biased social learning of self-perception in social anxiety disorder
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from social evaluative feedback about one's own self-perception and self-esteem   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (judges/confederates)     - Source content (social): outcome (evaluative feedback on speech performance — positive vs. negative mismatch relative to self-evaluation)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): self     - Target content (social): state (self-evaluation of performance; state self-esteem)
- **task_description:** Participants prepared and delivered a 3-minute speech in an fMRI scanner, believing two judges would evaluate them. Across 52 trials, they rated their own performance on evaluative statements, received (manipulated) judge feedback, and rated their state self-esteem; approximately 20 minutes later, they re-rated themselves on the same statements without feedback.
- **task_paradigm:** Stereotype learning task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (2 confederate judges)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** confederate
- **stimuli:** Evaluative text statements about speech performance (e.g., "appeared confident," "seemed nervous"), visual analogue scale ratings, judge feedback displayed as a second cursor on the same VAS
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Group x valence interaction on social influence bias: SAD updated self-evaluation more from negative than positive feedback compared to HC (t(37.7) = 2.52, p = 0.016, Cohen's d = 0.77) - SAD negative feedback influence > HC negative feedback influence (t(35.4) = 2.15, p = 0.039, Cohen's d = 0.65) - Group x valence interaction on affective updating learning rates (t(25.0) = 2.75, p = 0.011, Cohen's d = 0.84) - HC positive updating bias: difference between alpha_SelfPos vs alpha_SelfNeg (t(22) = 3.91, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.82) - SAD initial self-esteem lower than HC (t(35.0) = 2.40, p = 0.022, Cohen's d = 0.73) - Correlation between affective updating bias and social influence bias (r = 0.54, p = 0.00014; partial r = 0.46, p = 0.0017 controlling for group) - FPN response to feedback: HC > SAD (t(29.9) = 2.23, p = 0.034, Cohen's d = 0.79) - FPN modulation by valence: HC > SAD (t(29.9) = 2.11, p = 0.043, Cohen's d = 0.75) - FPN response correlated with learning bias (Spearman's r = 0.42, p = 0.017) - FPN valence modulation correlated with positive learning (Spearman's r = 0.46, p = 0.008)

## Implementation level
- **fmri_model_type:** model-based fMRI (parametric regressors) + multilevel mediation analysis
- **contrast:** - Path a: Brain activity related to positive vs. negative feedback mismatch (parametric modulator for delta-Eval) - Path b: Brain activity related to changes in self-evaluation (T1 to T2), controlling for Path a - Path ab (mediation): Brain activity mediating effect of feedback mismatch on self-evaluation changes - Conjunction of Paths a, b, and ab - Correlation with individual learning bias (positive - negative) - Group difference (HC > SAD) in overall feedback response - Group difference (HC > SAD) in valence-modulated feedback response (positive > negative delta-Eval) - FPN mask response to feedback (HC > SAD) - FPN mask modulation by valence (HC > SAD)

Individual learning bias correlated with AI/FO/vlPFC, dmPFC/aMCC, dlPFC, striatum, brainstem. FPN showed greater overall response and greater positive-valence modulation in HC vs SAD. SAD showed hypoactivation in precuneus and frontoparietal areas.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 44 (21 SAD, 23 HC); fMRI subsample N = 32 (16 SAD, 16 HC); ages 18-40, M = ~23.6; predominantly white
- **population_category:** clinical
- **population_age_range:** 18–40
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. The speech task creates a naturalistic social-evaluative stressor, but feedback was manipulated (not real judge evaluations). Participants were briefly introduced to one confederate judge to increase believability; debriefing showed most participants believed the feedback was real. However, the lab setting and scripted feedback limit ecological validity compared to real-world social evaluation.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Peak MNI coordinates are not reported in the main text or supplement — only whole-brain maps are shown in figures. This limits reproducibility. - The feedback was manipulated (not genuine), which may affect the ecological validity of the social learning process. - Relatively small fMRI sample (N = 32) for a between-group comparison. - Two different scanners used (Siemens Trio and Prisma) — though authors note scanner covariate did not meaningfully alter results. - Mean fitted parameter values for alpha_SelfPos and alpha_SelfNeg not reported numerically (only shown in bar graphs). - Data analysis plan was not preregistered.
- **limitations_reported:** Sample was relatively small and predominantly white, findings should be replicated in larger and more diverse samples; the speech and feedback paradigm was a robust stressor especially for socially anxious individuals and acute threat generally reduces reward and increases punishment sensitivity, so it remains an open question whether acute stress evocation contributed to or enhanced group differences in self-related learning biases; future research should test whether learning biases are stronger for evaluative statements individuals are most concerned about and for negative compared to positive statements
- **limitations_categorized:** sample size; limited generalizability (demographic homogeneity); potential stress confound; task simplicity (uniform treatment of positive/negative items)

- **other_notes:** This paper builds on Koban et al. (2017, Emotion) which used the same paradigm behaviorally. The current paper adds fMRI data and a new sample. The social influence analysis (beta weights for positive/negative feedback on T2 self-evaluation) is a separate GLM-based analysis, not part of the RL model per se — the RL model specifically models trial-by-trial self-esteem updating. The mediation analysis is a key methodological contribution, linking feedback mismatch to brain activity to behavioral self-evaluation changes. The paper proposes a neuroscientific model (Fig. 5) integrating VS/vmPFC for positive feedback, AI/FO/aMCC for negative feedback, and FPN for valence-biased attentional/regulatory processing of social prediction errors.
