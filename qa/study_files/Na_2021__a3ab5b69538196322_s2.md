## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Aberrant social controllability computation in relation to delusional traits; illusion of control and belief-behavior disconnection in high- vs. low-delusion individuals
- **study_focus_short:** Aberrant social controllability computation in relation to delusional traits
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning how one's own actions influence future offers from social partners (social controllability) and relationship of this computation to delusional ideation   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (multiple partners from a team)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (monetary offer amounts; action-outcome contingency)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (partners' future behavior)     - Target content (social): action/policy (how partners' offers change in response to one's accept/reject decisions — i.e., one's own influence/controllability over others)
- **task_description:** Online participants played a modified ultimatum game as responder with two teams of 30 virtual partners each (Controllable vs. Uncontrollable conditions). After the task, they rated perceived controllability. Participants also completed the Peters Delusions Inventory (PDI) and ICAR cognitive ability measure. High- and low-PDI groups (±1 SD) were compared on expected influence and belief-behavior disconnection.
- **task_paradigm:** Ultimatum game
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (30 virtual partners per team; 2 teams)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Monetary split offers ($1–$9 out of $20), partner avatars with names, team assignments, self-reported controllability rating (0–100%)
- **method:** online (behavioural + computational)
- **main_result:** - High PDI group showed higher expected influence than low PDI group under Uncontrollable condition (mean_high = 1.08, mean_low = 0.76, t(249) = 2.32, P < 0.05) but not Controllable (mean_high = 1.14, mean_low = 1.20, t(249) = −0.49, P = 0.62) - Significant condition × group interaction on expected influence (F(1, 245) = 4.55, P < 0.05) - High PDI group reported higher self-reported controllability under Uncontrollable (mean_high = 29.37, mean_low = 19.53, t(249) = 2.91, P < 0.01) but not Controllable - Significant condition × group interaction on self-reported controllability (F(1, 245) = 5.55, P < 0.05) - Belief-behavior disconnection: Under Controllable, low PDI group showed strong δ–self-report correlation (r = 0.47, P < 0.0001) but high PDI group did not (r = 0.15, P = 0.09); difference significant (z = −2.73, P < 0.01) - Robust regression: significant interaction of group × δ on self-reported controllability under Controllable (b = −9.87, P < 0.05) but not Uncontrollable (b = 3.12, P = 0.34)
