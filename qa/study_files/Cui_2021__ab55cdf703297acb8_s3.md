## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social learning adaptation to environmental volatility in BPD; dissociating learning deficits from interpersonal hypersensitivity (betrayal aversion) as causes of social dysfunction.
- **study_focus_short:** Social learning adaptation to environmental volatility in BPD
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from a partner's sharing/keeping behavior during a repeated Trust Game about the partner's trustworthiness under varying volatility   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (human partner, or computer in nonsocial condition)       - Content (social): action/policy (partner's share/keep decisions)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (partner)       - Content (social): state (mental state; trustworthiness)
- **task_description:** Participants played a modified repeated Trust Game online as investors, deciding each round whether to invest $6 (quadrupled to $24, then partner shares/keeps) or keep it. They played with three human partners and three computers separately across six 45-round blocks with different reversal rates (low/medium/high volatility).
- **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (3 human partners + 3 computers, each played in separate 45-round blocks)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** unclear
- **stimuli:** Monetary endowments ($6), blurred face/computer images, share/keep outcomes, counterfactual feedback
- **method:** online (behavioural)
- **main_result:** - High-MSI group invested more often (F=8.67, p=.004) and had lower accuracy (F=16.93, p<.001) - LMM: significant 3-way interaction condition x volatility x group on learning rate (t=-2.82, p=.005) - High-MSI group showed diminished learning adaptation to volatility in social condition (interaction volatility x group: t=-4.72, p<.001) but not nonsocial (t=-1.27, p=.20) - Overall slower learning in high-MSI group (main effect group: t=-3.80, p<.001) - Betrayal aversion comparable between groups (F=1.31, p=.25) - High-MSI showed reduced loss aversion across domains (F=10.67, p=.001) - Gender interaction: effects driven primarily by males (volatility x group in males: t=-5.12, p<.001; females: ns)
