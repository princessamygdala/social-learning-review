## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Norm learning / social norm adaptation — how the midbrain dopamine system (SN/VTA) computes prediction error signals related to social norms and their uncertainty during an ultimatum game.
- **study_focus_short:** Norm learning / social norm adaptation
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from others' offer behavior about shared social norms (fairness expectations)   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (anonymous proposers)     - Source content (social): outcomes (monetary offer splits)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): social structure (shared social norm / fairness expectation)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; normative expectation about what proposers ought to offer)
- **task_description:** Participants played the role of Responder in a modified ultimatum game across 60 trials with ostensibly different anonymous Proposers. Offers were algorithmically generated from truncated Gaussian distributions that shifted from low/high to medium offers mid-task to induce norm adaptation, and participants chose to accept or reject each split.
- **task_paradigm:** Ultimatum game
- **players:** Single agent (participant as Responder), multi-target (60 ostensibly different Proposers; actually computer-generated)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Monetary offer splits (out of $20 endowment), emotion rating scale (1–9 emoticons)
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI (high-resolution cardiac-gated, tailored for midbrain)
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - H2M group rejected significantly more and were less happy with medium offers than L2M group (bootstrapping P < 0.05), consistent with norm conditioning effect   - Bayesian observer model fit significantly better than random choice model (bootstrapping P < 0.001) and shuffled null distribution (bootstrapping P < 0.001); mean negative log-likelihood = 7.62 (SD = 9.21)   - SN/VTA coded for positive norm PE at time of response (left VTA: Z = 3.88; right VTA: Z = 3.75) — posPE > negPE significant   - SN/VTA coded for negative norm PE at time of response (left VTA: Z = −3.82; right VTA: Z = −2.91)   - SN/VTA coded for variance PE at time of response (left VTA: Z = 3.20; left SN: Z = 3.40) and at time of offer (left VTA: Z = 3.29)   - Anterior insula/IFG coded for variance PE (right: Z = 2.91, 276 voxels; left: Z = 3.50, 89 voxels)   - No effect sizes reported as Cohen's d, r, or similar standardized metrics; results reported as Z-statistics from GLM parametric regressors

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Bayesian observer model with norm-dependent utility (envy α, guilt β, temperature τ); norm and variance updated trial-by-trial via Bayesian updating. Formula: PE = offer − norm; posPE = max(offer − norm, 0); negPE = min(offer − norm, 0); variance PE = observed variance − expected variance. Choice modeled via Fehr-Schmidt inequity aversion with Bayesian norm updating.
- **model_family:** Bayesian
- **model_class:** PE learning / Belief updating
- **all_models_tested:** [{"name": "Bayesian observer model (Fehr-Schmidt utility + Bayesian norm updating)", "family": "Bayesian belief updating", "n_params": 3, "metric": "negative log-likelihood"}] - Note: Only one model was tested against a random choice baseline and shuffled null distribution. No formal model comparison among multiple computational models.
- **model_mb_mf:** Bayesian
- **model_params:** - α (envy): sensitivity to disadvantageous norm violations (negative norm PE); range [0, 10]; mean = 2.26 (SD = 3.16) [S] - β (guilt): sensitivity to advantageous norm violations (positive norm PE); range [0, 1]; mean = 0.34 (SD = 0.44) [S] - τ (temperature): choice stochasticity; range [0, 10]; mean = 1.61 (SD = 2.43)
- **social_param:** α (envy) — sensitivity to disadvantageous social norm violations (offers below internal norm); β (guilt) — sensitivity to advantageous social norm violations (offers above internal norm). Both parameters are inherently social as they reflect aversion to deviations from a social fairness norm.
- **social_param_name:** α
- **social_param_value:** 2.26
- **social_param_sd:** 3.16
- **social_param_range:** 0–10
- **model_comparison_metric:** Negative log-likelihood (compared against random choice model and permuted null distribution via bootstrapping)
- **how_model_fit:** individual-level-fit (parameters optimized per participant)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 50 recruited (30 female); 11 excluded (2 acquisition problems, 6 excessive head movement, 3 alignment problems); final N = 39 (20 L2M, 19 H2M). Mean age = 27.5 (SD = 10.3; range 19–58).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 19–58
- **ecological_validity:** Participants believed they were playing with real human proposers (none reported suspicion), enhancing social validity. However, offers were computer-generated, interaction was one-directional (responder only), and the task is a stylized economic game with limited naturalistic social interaction.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Coordinates are in Talairach space (brainstem-normalized), not MNI — needs attention for cross-study comparison - Only one computational model tested (no formal model comparison among alternatives) - No non-social control condition — cannot formally dissociate social from non-social PE processing - The "social agent" is actually a computer algorithm — participants were deceived into thinking they played with humans - High-resolution slab acquisition means only midbrain/brainstem and limited cortical coverage — ventral striatum not imaged - 11 of 50 participants excluded (22% attrition) - No standardized effect sizes reported (only Z-statistics)
- **limitations_reported:** since we did not use a non-social control condition, our paradigm does not allow us to study if and how our norm PE signals are different/similar from/to reward PEs or to formally test if these signals would be present if the participants played with a non-human partner"; imaging procedure precluded acquiring data from the striatum, so could not check for possible activation in the striatum at the time of the response screen; the functional specificity of the cluster identified as tracking negPE (i.e. ONLY coding for negPE) is lower than the one for the clusters coding for posPE; tentative hypotheses about the functional role of PE signals at response time need future studies to uncover
- **limitations_categorized:** no non-social control condition; limited brain coverage (no striatum); limited specificity of negative PE clusters; correlational/tentative interpretation
