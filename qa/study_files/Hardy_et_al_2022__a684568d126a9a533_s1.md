## Quality
- **sample_size:** N=600 (3 networks x 10 generations x 20 participants); Amazon Mechanical Turk; US-based; approval rating >= 95.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Highly controlled laboratory analogue of multigenerational social transmission. Uniform random sampling of previous generation enforced by design, which limits ecological validity. Binary categorization task is simplified relative to real-world cultural transmission. Evidence was perfectly correlated across participants within a generation to reduce variance.
- **eligibility_flag:** Borderline -- the paper uses computational modeling (Bayesian social sampling) and human behavioral data in a social context with learning over time (multigenerational accumulation). However, learning is primarily about non-social content (gemstone categories) rather than social content, and the "computational model" is parameter-free and evaluated via simulation-and-compare rather than being fitted to individual-level data. The social context is the transmission structure rather than the learning target. Flagged as: social context is the transmission mechanism, not the learning content; model has no free parameters.
- **concerns:** - No free parameters in any model -- models are evaluated purely on MSE of predicted vs. observed aggregate popularity, with no individual-level fitting. - Social sampling proportion (19%) was empirically estimated from resampling behavior, not fitted. - Supplement referenced ("SEM") but not accessible -- proofs and additional model details may be there. - The task involves learning about non-social content (gemstone classifications) through a social transmission mechanism. - Evidence was perfectly correlated within generation, which is an artificial constraint.
- **limitations_reported:** We tested our model on a simple binary-choice decision-making task where the transmission structure was explicitly designed to match our model assumptions"; "These simplifications limit our ability to draw general conclusions about information accumulation in natural populations"; "While enforcing uniform sampling of the previous generation gave us tight control over the transmission dynamics, people's sampling, and exploration strategies may depend on the task and are likely considerably more complex in naturalistic domains and networks"; "People may not always transmit their true beliefs -- instead, they may give noisy, incomplete, or misleading accounts of their opinions and decisions to others.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; task simplicity; artificial transmission structure; limited generalizability; idealized assumptions about belief transmission.
