## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Stereotype-biased social learning; how societal stereotype knowledge shapes instrumental reinforcement learning from interactions with stereotyped group members, producing group-based preferences (prejudice).
- **study_focus_short:** Stereotype-biased social learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from reward feedback during interactions with members of stereotyped groups about the reward value of individual group members   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (group members / players from stereotyped groups)       - In Studies 5 & 8: Source type (social): other (demonstrator's interactions — observational learning)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (reward feedback; points shared or not shared)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (individual group members) / group (positively vs. negatively stereotyped groups)     - Target content (social): stimulus (reward value / preference for group members)
- **task_description:** Participants played a probabilistic reward learning (money sharing) game with avatar players from two groups described with positive or negative stereotypes. On each trial, participants chose between two players (one from each group) with fixed but individually varying sharing rates (equated across groups), receiving binary reward feedback. In Studies 5 and 8, participants instead observed a prior participant's choices and feedback before making their own test-phase choices.
- **task_paradigm:** Probabilistic reward learning
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (8 players; 4 per group, 2 groups). In Studies 5 and 8: single agent (observer), yoked to single demonstrator.
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Avatars with group color cues (blue/green clothing, hair shade), textual stereotype descriptions, binary reward feedback (+1 or 0 points)
- **method:** online / behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural / online

- **ecological_validity:** Uses abstract avatar-based sharing game rather than real intergroup interactions; stereotype descriptions are explicitly provided rather than naturally acquired; however, the design captures a realistic scenario of stereotype exposure followed by repeated social interactions. The observational learning studies (5, 8) add ecological relevance by showing bias transmission through observation. Limited by lab/online setting and artificial group distinctions.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Mean fitted parameter values for the winning model are not reported (only regression of rank-transformed parameters onto bias). - Bayesian models (9, 10) were only tested in Studies 1-3 and dropped from later studies due to poor fit — could be viewed as incomplete model space exploration. - High exclusion rates in several studies (e.g., Study 3: 71 of 158 excluded; Study 8: 146 of 300 excluded), which may bias the sample toward participants who learned well. - No parameter recovery or model recovery analyses reported. - No simulation of model predictions prior to data collection described.
- **limitations_reported:** Not explicitly discussed in a dedicated limitations section. The authors acknowledge: the task involves artificial groups rather than real social categories; the stereotype manipulation is explicit and may differ from implicit societal transmission; the sharing game is a simplified social interaction; individual differences in stereotype endorsement or egalitarian motivation were not measured as moderators.
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; task simplicity; no individual difference moderators; artificial groups; high exclusion rates
