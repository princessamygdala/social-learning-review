## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Trust learning; partner selection learning; belief updating about partner types (human vs. AI) through repeated social interaction with feedback
- **study_focus_short:** Trust learning · partner selection learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from partners' reciprocity outcomes about the trustworthiness/expected returns of different partner types (human vs. AI)   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (candidate partner — human or AI bot)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (points returned by selected candidate)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (candidate type — human candidates vs. bot candidates as population-level categories)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; expected trustworthiness/generosity of partner type)
- **task_description:** In a modified triadic trust game, a selector is endowed with 10 points and paired with two candidates (who may be humans or LLM-powered bots). After one round of text-based communication, the selector chooses to invest in one candidate (tripling the points) or keep the points; the selected candidate decides how many points (0–30) to return.
- **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Single agent (selector/participant), multi-target (2 candidates per round; across rounds, 5 human candidates + 5 bot candidates per group of 15)
- **n_players:** small group (3-4)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Text messages (free-form chat), monetary point outcomes, candidate identity labels (in transparent condition)
- **method:** online / behavioural
- **method_full:** Behavioural / online
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - In opaque conditions, selectors did not preferentially select bots over humans despite bots returning more (bot return = 19.1 vs. human = 11.38; d = 2.57)   - Selectors misattributed bot prosociality to humans: overestimated human returns (believed - actual = 1.43; d = 0.66) and underestimated bot returns (believed - actual = -5.43; d = -2.37)   - Transparency produced initial anti-bot bias (d = -0.59) but bots eventually outcompeted humans in second half (d = 0.57)   - Extended RW model with cross-type misattribution captured belief dynamics; full model (M0) outperformed reduced model (M1) only in transparent conditions   - Selectors with more accurate beliefs achieved higher payoffs (Extended Data Fig. 6b)   - In Study 3 opaque condition, bots also outcompeted humans in later rounds (d = 0.80) but selectors withdrew from investing rather than switching to bots

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 975 total (Study 1: 225 human-only + 150 hybrid opaque = 375; Study 2: 150 opaque + 150 transparent = 300; Study 3: 150 opaque + 150 transparent = 300). Demographics: 567 female, median age 33 (range 18–76).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–76
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. Uses real-time text communication with LLM-powered bots (GPT-4o) in an online trust game, which captures some naturalistic elements of human-AI interaction. However, the interaction is constrained to a single question-answer exchange per round, anonymous with no individual reputation building, and uses a stylized economic game rather than naturalistic cooperative tasks. Points-based incentive structure limits generalizability to real-world partnership decisions.
- **eligibility_flag:** FLAG — social agent is partially an automated system (LLM-powered bots as candidate partners). The learning modeled is human selectors learning about the trustworthiness of partner types, which qualifies as social learning. However, the "social" partners include AI agents, not solely human agents.
- **concerns:** - Bots were deliberately prevented from learning/adapting, limiting the ecological validity of the hybrid society dynamics - Model was fit at the group level (pooled by condition), not individual level — individual heterogeneity in learning rates not characterized - Only 2 models compared (full vs. reduced), limiting model comparison rigor - No parameter recovery or model recovery reported - No simulation of models before fitting - The model only captures belief updating when a candidate is selected (no update when selector keeps points), which may miss learning from non-investment decisions - Preprint status — not yet peer-reviewed
- **limitations_reported:** Our study does not aim to offer universal conclusions about partner selection in hybrid societies"; "it remains possible that future AI models or models guided by carefully tailored, task-specific instructions could become more adept at forming partnerships with humans"; "perceptions of AI agents are likely to evolve as people gain more experience interacting with them in everyday life"; absence of individual reputation mechanisms in experimental setting limited human candidates' ability to compete; results based on specific LLM (GPT-4o) though post-hoc checks with other LLMs showed consistency
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited generalizability; task simplicity; limited ecological validity; no individual reputation mechanism; model-specificity (single LLM); no parameter recovery; no model recovery
