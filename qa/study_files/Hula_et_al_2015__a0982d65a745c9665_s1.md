- **task_description:** In a multi-round trust game (10 rounds), an investor receives 20 monetary units per round and chooses how much to send to a trustee; the sent amount is tripled and the trustee decides how much to return. - **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Multi-agent (dyad), asymmetric (investor and trustee roles). Real subject data: N=195 dyads from Xiang et al. (2012), comprising healthy investors paired with various trustee groups.

- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Guilt parameter is recovered with high reliability from simulated data (confusion matrix shows strong diagonal for all 3 guilt types)   - Theory of mind level shows slight overestimation tendency in trustees   - Planning horizon P=7 investors are misclassified as P=2 when paired with impulsive (P=2) trustees   - Trustee negative log-likelihood significantly better than random for the new model (mean NLL = 11.7 vs. chance threshold 16.1; p < 0.05), unlike the earlier Xiang et al. ## Quality
- **sample_size:** Simulations: 8100 games (60 per parameter pairing) + 2025 validation games. Real data: N=195 dyads from Xiang et al. (2012) — 48 impersonal healthy trustees, 52 personal healthy trustees, 55 BPD trustees, 38 low-SES matched healthy trustees. Investors always healthy.
- **population_category:** clinical
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Low-moderate. Uses an established economic exchange game (multi-round trust task) that captures key features of reciprocal social interaction (cooperation, deception, coaxing). However, the game is a highly structured lab paradigm with discrete action choices and monetary incentives. Planning horizons and guilt parameters are discretized into a small number of types. The model assumes agents know the game rules and number of rounds.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - The paper is primarily a methods paper (labeled as "PLOS Computational Biology Methods paper") demonstrating a computational algorithm, with real data analysis as a secondary validation - Inverse temperature β = 1/3 is fixed rather than fitted, which may limit model flexibility - Only 3 discrete guilt types and 3 planning horizons — coarse discretization - The model assumes agents know their own ToM level and assume the partner is one level below, which is a strong assumption - Confusion between P=7 and P=2 investors when paired with impulsive trustees raises concerns about parameter identifiability in certain dyad configurations - No formal model comparison metrics (e.g., BIC, AIC) reported — comparisons are based on NLL thresholds and KS tests on parameter distributions across groups
- **limitations_reported:** One important lacuna is that although it is straightforward to use maximum likelihood to search over fixed parameters (such as ToM level, planning horizon or indeed temperature), it is radically harder to perform the computations that become necessary when these factors are incorporated into the structure of the intentional models"; subjects assumed to make inferences about opponent's guilt but not about their theory of mind level or planning horizon; the cognitive hierarchy theory assumes finite levels of mentalizing (Poisson with mean ~1.5); the planning horizon confusion when an impulsive trustee is paired with a far-sighted investor suggests difficulty distinguishing planning parameters in some dyad configurations
- **limitations_categorized:** parameter identifiability; model complexity constraints; limited parameter inference (guilt only, not ToM or planning); discretization of continuous parameters; fixed inverse temperature
