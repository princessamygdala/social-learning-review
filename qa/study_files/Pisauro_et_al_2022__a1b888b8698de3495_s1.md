## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Cooperation-competition trade-off learning; learning to adjust one's degree of cooperativeness based on social context and inferences about a co-player's intentions in a continuous economic game.
- **study_focus_short:** Cooperation-competition trade-off learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from observing a co-player's competitive/cooperative positioning to update one's own degree of cooperativeness along a continuous cooperation-competition spectrum.   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (co-player in dyad)       - Not joint     - Source content (social): action/policy (co-player's spatial positioning reflecting their cooperativeness)       - Not joint   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (co-player)       - Not joint     - Target content (social): state (mental state; intentions/cooperativeness level)       - Not joint
- **task_description:** Two players simultaneously position themselves in a continuous linear space representing a foraging territory; the player closest to a randomly appearing target wins a reward, with the payoff split between winner and loser determined by a social context parameter (cooperative, intermediate, competitive). Participants must infer the co-player's degree of cooperativeness and adjust their own positioning across 180 trials (60 per context).
- **task_paradigm:** Joint action / coordination
- **players:** Multi-agent (dyad), symmetric; one participant in fMRI scanner, one in adjacent room.
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** human (live)
- **stimuli:** Linear spatial territory (bar moving across screen), target position (randomly sampled), reward feedback dependent on distance to target and social context (α parameter).
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Social context significantly shifts cooperativeness: participants more competitive in competitive context, more cooperative in cooperative context (β = −0.12, fixed effect of condition on average cooperativeness)   - Distance between players decreased across contexts from cooperative to competitive (β = −0.25, fixed effect of condition)   - Tit-for-tat reciprocation: larger co-player deviations produced larger reciprocal changes (β = 0.03, fixed effect of bin)   - Bayesian model B6 best fit: observed vs predicted positions r = 0.91   - Strong negative correlation between Social Bias and TitxTat parameters (r = −0.62, β = −3.79)   - Precision parameter correlated with behavioural variance (r = 0.73, β = 1.09)   - Unsigned social prediction error (KLD magnitude) encoded in posterior rTPJ (Z = 4.40, MNI: 52, −58, 30)   - Signed social prediction error (KLD sign) encoded in anterior rTPJ (Z = −3.67, MNI: 50, −38, 32)   - Context-modulated cooperation encoding in pDMPFC (Z = −4.09, MNI: −8, 16, 52)   - Context-modulated signed PE in ACCg (Z = −3.13, MNI: 0, 34, 20) and PaCg (Z = −3.36, MNI: 2, 50, 12)   - arTPJ activity predicted future changes in cooperativeness in intermediate context

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 54 (27 same-sex pairs) for fMRI; 2 pairs removed (1 excessive head movement, 1 technical problem), leaving N = 50 individual participants (25 dyads) for analysis (7 male pairs, 18 female pairs). N = 81 participated in initial behavioural session.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** The Space Dilemma offers higher ecological validity than binary economic games by allowing continuous positioning along a cooperation-competition spectrum, mimicking real-world social orientation. However, the interaction is still constrained to a laboratory spatial task with abstract stimuli and limited to dyadic interaction within predefined payoff contexts.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Order of conditions was fixed (cooperative-competitive-intermediate) across all dyads, not counterbalanced, which may introduce order effects. The paper notes one pair had a scan interrupted after 30 trials and another was run with wrong TR (3s instead of 1.5s) — unclear how this affected results. The social bias and TitxTat parameters were strongly anticorrelated (r = −0.62), raising potential collinearity concerns in interpreting individual parameter contributions.
- **limitations_reported:** Future experiments using this paradigm might help to probe further hypotheses. For example, future studies might address questions relating to the impact of uncertainty, for instance varying the reward probability of each location (making the reward location — to an extent – predictable through uni/multimodal distribution instead of a uniform one), the difficulty of the task (speeding up or making it harder to make a certain choice) or the social dynamics (increasing the number of players or the distribution of the rewards among them).
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; fixed condition order; task simplicity; limited to dyadic interaction; no manipulation of uncertainty or difficulty

- **other_notes:** This paper introduces a novel continuous generalization of the Prisoner's Dilemma (the "Space Dilemma") that addresses a significant gap in the literature by studying cooperation-competition along a continuum rather than as a binary choice. The Bayesian model with social prediction errors (KLD) provides an elegant computational framework linking mentalizing to behavioral updating. The dissociation between signed PE (arTPJ) and unsigned PE (prTPJ) in adjacent TPJ subregions is a notable finding. The paper is published in Nature Communications (2022) and shares data/code openly.
