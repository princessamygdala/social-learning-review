## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 50 (26 females); mean age 18.9 years (SD = 1). After exclusions: N = 42 for T1 analyses, N = 45 for T2 analyses, N = 38 for joint T1/T2 analyses.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=18.9
- **ecological_validity:** Low-moderate. Social evaluative threat manipulation (video camera + experimenter observation) is a validated stress induction but occurs in a laboratory setting with abstract stimuli. The learning task itself has no social content. The Trier-like stress paradigm has some ecological validity for social evaluation contexts.
- **eligibility_flag:** FLAG — The learning task itself is non-social (probabilistic reinforcement learning with abstract stimuli and computer-generated feedback). The social component is limited to the stress manipulation context (social evaluative threat). This is borderline for "learning in a social context" — the social element modulates learning but the learning itself is not about social content and does not involve social agents as information sources.
- **concerns:** (1) Only one computational model tested — no model comparison. (2) Social component is contextual only (stress manipulation), not integral to the learning task. (3) Multiple exclusions reduce sample sizes for different analyses. (4) No formal model comparison metrics (BIC/AIC); only Pseudo-R² reported as goodness of fit. (5) Median split on BIS reduces statistical power. (6) No parameter recovery or model recovery reported. (7) Effect sizes (η², Cohen's d) not reported for key interactions.
- **limitations_reported:** Manipulations of stress controllability are difficult to manage with human participants, especially when the task requires veritable interaction"; "we regretfully did not measure an appraisal of perceived controllability"; "some aspects of hypothesized striatal and dopaminergic functions remain difficult or impossible to assess with human neuroimaging
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; missing manipulation check (controllability appraisal); methodological limitation (cannot assess subcortical dopamine with EEG)
