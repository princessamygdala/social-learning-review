## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Observational learning -- investigating whether human amygdala neurons encode reinforcement learning variables (expected values, outcomes, prediction errors) during learning through observation of another agent's experiences, and whether the neural code for observational learning overlaps with or is distinct from experiential learning.
- **study_focus_short:** Observational learning -- investigating whether human amygdala neurons encode
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning the value of stimuli by observing another agent's reward outcomes on those stimuli, and by directly experiencing one's own outcomes on the same stimuli   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (observed agent in pre-recorded video)     - Source content (social): outcome (reward feedback received by the other agent)     - *Also*: Source type (non-social): self (experiential condition)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (reward feedback received by self)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (stimulus/bandit reward contingencies)     - Target content (non-social): stimulus value (expected reward associated with each bandit)
- **task_description:** Participants performed a multi-armed bandit task with 288 trials across 4 interleaved experiential and observational blocks. In experiential no-choice trials, participants passively observed a single bandit's lever being pulled and received reward feedback; in observational no-choice trials, participants watched a pre-recorded video of another person experiencing the same trial structure. Choice trials tested learning by having participants select between two bandits with no outcome feedback.
- **task_paradigm:** Multi-armed bandit
- **players:** Single agent (participant), single observed agent (pre-recorded video of another person); "Single agent (participant), dyadic (observed stranger)
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** human (live)
- **stimuli:** Colored one-armed bandits (slot machines), reward outcomes in points drawn from normal distributions (truncated at -50 to +50), pre-recorded video of another player
- **method:** Single-neuron electrophysiology (depth electrodes in epilepsy patients)
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Amygdala neurons discriminated experiential vs. observational trial type with 86.1% decoding accuracy (p < 0.002, permutation test)   - 19 amygdala neurons (9.4%) were sensitive to experiential expected value (p < 0.006, permutation test)   - 18 amygdala neurons (8.9%) were sensitive to observational expected value (p < 0.002, permutation test)   - 22 amygdala neurons (10.8%) were sensitive to experiential outcomes (p < 0.002, permutation test)   - Observational outcomes were decodable from amygdala population (accuracy = 33.1%, p < 0.026, permutation test)   - Experiential outcomes were decodable from amygdala population (accuracy = 39.3%, p < 0.002, permutation test)   - Cross-condition decoding generalization failed: training on experiential and testing on observational (and vice versa) did not yield above-chance decoding, indicating distinct neural codes for experiential vs. observational learning   - Experiential and observational EV sensitivities across neurons were uncorrelated (rho = 0.10, p < 0.14); outcome sensitivities also uncorrelated (rho = 0.02, p < 0.77)   - Counterfactual RL model had highest protected exceedance probability among 4 models   - No evidence for lateralization or subnuclei specialization of value coding in amygdala
- **effect_size:** Decoding accuracies: trial type = 86.1%, experiential EV = 36.4%, observational outcome = 33.1%, experiential outcome = 39.3%. Proportion correct in choice trials: 0.776 +/- 0.038 overall. Correlation between experiential/observational EV sensitivities: rho = 0.10; outcome sensitivities: rho = 0.02. No conventional effect sizes (Cohen's d, etc.) reported.

- **concerns:** - The authors explicitly note (p. 96-98) that the design does not control for social vs. non-social observational learning -- the signals could reflect observation of causal relationships rather than specifically social observation - Clinical population (epilepsy patients with depth electrodes) -- unclear generalizability - Small sample (12 patients, though 202 neurons) - Proportions of sensitive neurons are lower than in monkey literature - No parameter recovery or model recovery analyses reported - The beta parameter has very high variability relative to its mean (0.17 +/- 0.35), raising concerns about parameter identifiability - Blocked design (experiential vs. observational) could confound trial type discrimination with visual/contextual differences
