## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social cue integration in learning; autistic trait-related differences in integrating implicit social gaze cues into probabilistic reward learning about nonsocial stimuli.
- **study_focus_short:** Social cue integration in learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from social gaze cues and nonsocial card outcomes about card reward probabilities, with social information modulating belief precision about the nonsocial cue.   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (virtual face/gaze agent)     - Source content (social): action/policy (gaze direction indicating card)     - Source type (non-social): world     - Source content (non-social): outcome (card reward feedback)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world     - Target content (non-social): state (card reward probabilities)     - Target type (social): other (virtual face/gaze agent)     - Target content (social): state (gaze accuracy/reliability)
- **task_description:** Participants played a probabilistic card game in which they chose between two cards (blue/green) to earn rewards, while a face cue in the center of the screen shifted its gaze toward one of the cards before each choice. Card reward probabilities and gaze accuracy (congruency with winning card) varied independently across stable and volatile phases over 120 trials.
- **task_paradigm:** Probabilistic reward learning
- **players:** Single agent (participant), single implicit social cue (virtual face with gaze)
- **n_players:** single agent (1)
- **partner_type:** none
- **stimuli:** Two colored cards (blue/green) with reward values (1-9), central face with directional gaze cue, binary feedback (green tick/red cross)
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Higher AQ scores associated with lower total task scores (r = -.39, 95% CI = -0.68 to -0.13)   - Significant group x condition interaction for card coupling parameter κ_c (F(1,35) = 10.74, p = .0025)   - Low AQ group showed larger κ_c in incongruent vs. congruent first condition (t(16) = -2.55, p = .04 Bonferroni corrected)   - Significant group x condition interaction for belief precision about card probabilities (F(1,35) = 10.82, p = .02)   - AQ scores correlated with taking misleading gaze advice in volatile low-accuracy phase (R² = 22.28%, F(35) = 9.74, p = .0037; r = .52, 95% CI = 0.29 to 0.75)   - Significant group x condition interaction for self-reported reliance on gaze (F(1,35) = 5.89, p = .021)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 36 (18 high AQ, 18 low AQ; 19 men, 17 women; age range 20-37 years, mean age 26.25 years); prescreened based on AQ scores up to 25; median split at AQ = 15.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 20–37
- **ecological_validity:** Low ecological validity. The social cue is an implicit gaze shift from a static face on a screen, which is a minimal and non-interactive proxy for real social interaction. No real social partner or live interaction. The gaze cue was presented without explicit instruction about its relevance, which adds some ecological validity in terms of implicit social processing, but the task remains a laboratory card-guessing paradigm.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Sample is subclinical (healthy participants with varying AQ scores, not diagnosed autism); cannot generalize findings to clinical ASD population (authors acknowledge this). Model comparison details and full model space are in the Supplement which was not accessible. Mean fitted parameter values not reported in main text. The study uses a median split on AQ scores for group analyses, which reduces statistical power compared with continuous analyses (though correlational analyses are also reported).
- **limitations_reported:** We cannot rule out intact precision-weighted PE processing in patients with autism, as our sample comprised healthy subjects"; "One can speculate that in a patient sample, impaired inference about the social cue in addition to the reduced integration of social information could be observed"; "Future research should include testing patients with a formal diagnosis of autism to explore whether the observed differences hold across the entire autistic spectrum"; the paradigm could be used with neuroimaging to investigate neural underpinnings.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited generalizability (subclinical sample only); no clinical validation; no neuroimaging; task simplicity (implicit gaze cue rather than real social interaction)
