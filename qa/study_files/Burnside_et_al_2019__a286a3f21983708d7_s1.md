## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 20 (16 female; mean age 22.3, SD = 3.4); 1 excluded from EEG analyses due to technical problem (N = 19 for EEG); recorded in 10 dyads
- **population_category:** healthy adults

- **ecological_validity:** Lab-based paradigm with abstract stimuli and binary outcomes; participants in separate shielded rooms with no face-to-face interaction; observer had no stakes in outcomes (no consequence), reducing ecological validity for real-world observational learning where observed outcomes may carry personal relevance.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Small sample size (N = 20, 19 for EEG) - Observer had no personal consequence for outcomes, which may explain the null oFRN-PE finding rather than a genuine absence of observational PE encoding - Model fit to acting trials only; observational learning rates derived from the same model but not directly fit to observation-only data - No parameter recovery or model recovery checks reported - No simulation of the model before fitting - BIC values divided by 100 for readability, making comparison less transparent
- **limitations_reported:** The observational PE signal may be weak because observed outcomes carried no personal consequence for the observer; the observer did not make a choice, which may reduce pMFC engagement; task or social context may determine involvement of structures in observational learning; observational tasks might instigate model-based learning strategies that differ from model-free computations; future research needed to identify how different aspects of an observational learning task impact the relationship between model variables and neural activity.
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; task simplicity; no personal stakes for observer; potential confound of action vs. observation; limited generalizability; small sample size

- **other_notes:** This is a hyperscanning EEG study (simultaneous recording from both participants in a dyad). The key finding is a dissociation: the FRN encodes prediction error in active learning but not during observation, while the P3a (attentional orienting) responds comparably to own and observed outcomes. The confirmation bias finding (preferential learning from confirmatory outcomes) replicates Palminteri et al. (2017). The social parameter of interest is the separate learning rate for observed outcomes, which was lower than for own outcomes, particularly for confirmatory trials.
