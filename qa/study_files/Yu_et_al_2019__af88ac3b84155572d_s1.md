## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Harm aversion across moral decision-making, moral judgment, and moral inference -- a unified computational framework for understanding how people trade off self-interest against harm to others across three dimensions of moral cognition.
- **study_focus_short:** Harm aversion across moral decision-making, moral judgment, and moral inference
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from observed moral decisions of others about their moral character (harm aversion), and using one's own harm aversion preferences to make moral decisions and judgments   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (observed agent)     - Source content (social): action/policy (moral choices trading money vs. shocks)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (observed agent)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; moral character / harm aversion trait)
- **task_description:** In the moral decision task, a "Decider" makes choices between amounts of money and painful electric shocks directed toward themselves or an anonymous "Receiver." In the moral inference task, participants predict decisions of observed agents who trade off money for themselves against shocks for a receiver, periodically rating the agent's moral character.
- **task_paradigm:** Punishment / third-party
- **players:** Single agent (participant), dyadic (anonymous receiver in decision task; observed agents in inference task)
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)

- **method:** fMRI / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI / behavioural (across the reviewed studies; the paper itself is a review)
- **main_result:** - Main Results (from reviewed studies, not new data):   - Harm aversion for others > harm aversion for self ("hyperaltruism") across multiple studies (Crockett et al., 2014, 2015, 2017)   - Reduced dorsal striatum response to ill-gotten gains correlated with hyperaltruism (Crockett et al., 2017)   - Bayesian HGF model captured 87% of variance in lab moral inference predictions (Siegel et al., in press)   - Beliefs about bad agents' harm aversion were more uncertain and volatile than beliefs about good agents (Siegel et al., in press)   - LPFC activity during moral decisions tracked blameworthiness as judged by independent raters (Crockett et al., 2017)   - Own harm aversion modulated weighting of profit and pain in blame judgments (Crockett et al., 2017)

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Three linked models: (1) Decision: Utility model V(harm) = (1-kappa)*Delta_m - kappa*Delta_s with softmax; (2) Judgment: Blame linear regression on Delta_m, Delta_s, kappa_o, kappa_s and interactions; (3) Inference: HGF + utility model where belief about agent's harm aversion (mu) replaces own kappa, updated via Bayesian prediction error weighted by belief uncertainty (sigma)
- **model_family:** Utility / EV
- **model_class:** Belief updating
