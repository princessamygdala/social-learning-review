## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Prosocial learning — whether model-based vs. model-free reinforcement learning strategies differ when learning on behalf of self vs. another person
- **study_focus_short:** Prosocial learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from one's own reward/punishment outcomes about which actions maximize value for self or another person   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self       - Participant makes all choices directly     - Source content (non-social): outcome       - Monetary reward/punishment feedback from chosen option   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (present confederate) — in other condition     - Target type (non-social): self — in self condition     - Target content (non-social): action/policy       - Which spaceship (action) leads to the more profitable planet
- **task_description:** In a two-step reinforcement learning task, participants chose between pairs of spaceships that deterministically traveled to one of two planets, receiving reward outcomes (integers from -4 to +5) generated by a Gaussian random walk. Participants completed the task in separate blocks for themselves (self condition) and for a present observer/confederate (other condition) who evaluated their performance.
- **task_paradigm:** Two-step task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), dyadic (stranger observer/confederate present, evaluates performance)
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** confederate
- **stimuli:** Abstract spaceships, colored planets, numerical monetary outcomes (-4 to +5)
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** Behavioural
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - No significant difference in stay probability between self and other conditions for reward outcomes (t = -1.31, df = 35, p = 0.17, CI = [-0.27, 0.06])   - No significant difference in MB index between self and other conditions (t = -0.09, df = 35, p = 0.93, CI = [-0.09, 0.08])   - Slight difference in MF control between self and other conditions (t = -2.61, df = 35, p = 0.01, CI = [-0.10, -0.01]), but MF was the less dominant strategy   - MB control positively correlated with accuracy (r = 0.63, p < .001, 95% CI = [0.46, 0.75])   - MF control negatively correlated with accuracy (r = -0.29, p = .013, 95% CI = [-0.49, -0.06])   - Weighting parameter (w) positively correlated with corrected reward rate: self (r = 0.66, p < .001, 95% CI = [0.43, 0.81]), other (r = 0.38, p = .02, 95% CI = [0.06, 0.63])   - No significant difference in weighting parameter between self and other conditions (t = -1.39, df = 35, p = 0.17, CI = [-0.18, 0.03])   - MB control more strongly affected performance in self than other conditions (F(3,68) = 14.49, p = 0.0001)   - No significant correlation between BFNE-II social anxiety score and MB control or w parameter in either condition

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 76 recruited (38 pairs); N = 36 analyzed after exclusions (18 female; age: 24.39 ± 2.61, mean ± SD; range not specified beyond inclusion criterion 18-35)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Low ecological validity — abstract spaceship/planet task with no real social interaction; the "prosocial" manipulation was limited to monetary beneficiary assignment with a present but silent observer. The observer's evaluation was not fed back to the participant during the task. Participants were strangers, limiting social motivation.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Only one model was fitted (the Kool et al. 2016 hybrid model); no competing models were tested or compared - No parameter recovery or model recovery reported - No simulation of the model prior to fitting - No posterior predictive checks - Mean fitted parameter values not reported in text (only priors given) - The MF difference between self/other (p = 0.01) was dismissed because "MF was less dominant" — this is a questionable analytical decision - Block-level condition assignment (self vs. other) may have weakened the manipulation - Null result may reflect insufficient statistical power or weak social manipulation rather than true absence of effect - Authors acknowledge the block design may have caused participants to forget the beneficiary
- **limitations_reported:** The presence of the partner might have counteracted the effect of making decisions for others by simultaneously increasing arousal/attention and cognitive load; the block-level beneficiary reminder (rather than trial-by-trial) may have imposed additional cognitive load causing participants to forget the beneficiary; the manipulation of effort may not have been adequately effective; participants did not know each other, unlike previous studies that recruited familiar pairs; the nature of the negative findings leaves open the possibility that the social manipulation was insufficient
- **limitations_categorized:** Weak social manipulation; limited ecological validity; potential insufficient statistical power; block design confound; unfamiliar dyads reducing social motivation; null result interpretation difficulty
