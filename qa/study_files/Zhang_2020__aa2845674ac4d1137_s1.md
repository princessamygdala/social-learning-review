## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social influence learning; integration of direct experiential learning and social observational learning during group decision-making under uncertainty
- **study_focus_short:** Social influence learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from own reward outcomes and observing group members' choices/outcomes about reward contingencies and others' performance   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self       - Source content (non-social): outcome (reward/punishment from chosen option)     - Source type (social): group (4 coplayers)       - Source content (social): action/policy (others' choices) and outcome (others' reward histories)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (reward contingencies of abstract fractals)       - Target content (non-social): stimulus-outcome associations (which fractal is rewarding)     - Target type (social): group (4 coplayers)       - Target content (social): state (others' performance/reliability, tracked via cumulative reward history)
- **task_description:** Groups of five participants performed a probabilistic reversal learning task connected via intranet in real time; on each trial, participants made an initial choice and confidence bet between two abstract fractals (70/30% reward), observed the four coplayers' choices, could adjust their choice and bet, then observed all outcomes. Reward contingencies reversed every 8-12 trials.
- **task_paradigm:** Reversal learning
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (4 same-gender coplayers in real-time; 185 total across 41 groups)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** unclear
- **stimuli:** Abstract fractals (two choice options), portrait photos of coplayers, binary monetary outcomes (20 cents win/loss), confidence bets (1-3)
- **method:** fMRI (39 scanned) + behavioural (146 behavioural-only)

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** M6b: Fictitious RL (direct learning; alpha) + preference-weighted cumulative reward history with decay (social learning; gamma) + instantaneous social influence (w.Nagainst) + Bet 1 on Choice 2 switching. 15 free parameters.Formula: V_t = beta_vself * V_self,t + beta_vother * V_other,t (Choice 1). V_self updated via fictitious RL (both chosen and unchosen). V_other = sum of preference-weighted, exponentially decayed cumulative reward histories of 4 coplayers over last 3 trials, normalized to [-1,1]. Choice 2 switching modeled by logistic regression with value difference, w.Nagainst, and Bet 1. Bets modeled via ordered logistic regression.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 185 (95 female; mean age 25.56 +/- 3.98; range 18-37); 39 fMRI (20 female; mean age 25.59 +/- 3.51; range 20-37), 146 behavioural only. 20 of original 205 excluded (switched once or never). - **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–37
- **ecological_validity:** High for a lab study -- real-time multiplayer interaction via intranet with actual coplayers (not confederates or computer agents); real photos of coplayers displayed; no deception; participants aware of real human interaction. Task uses abstract fractals rather than ecologically meaningful stimuli, and social interaction is limited to observing choices/outcomes without verbal communication. Control study with computer agents confirms social specificity.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Connectivity analyses (PPI, PhiPI) assess correlation, not directionality -- causal claims cannot be made - SPE effect sizes in putamen ROI analysis are small (beta ~ 0.04) and marginally significant (P = 0.04) - Vother was orthogonalized with respect to Vself in GLM 1, which means any shared variance was attributed to Vself -- this could underestimate Vother neural correlates - Model space does not include Bayesian models (acknowledged by authors) - No bet-related neural correlates found (likely due to temporal proximity of choice and bet cues) - Preference weights fixed rather than estimated as free parameters [HIGH]
- **limitations_reported:** Connectivity analyses assess correlation rather than directionality; establishing causal accounts using brain stimulation or pharmacological manipulations would be a promising future avenue; model space is not exhaustive, particularly did not test Bayesian models that track more complex task dynamics; no significant neural correlates for post-decision confidence (bets), possibly due to temporal proximity of choice and bet cues creating collinear regressors; response time not recorded, which might help dissociate confidence from valuation
- **limitations_categorized:** limited causal inference (correlational connectivity); non-exhaustive model space; collinearity in fMRI design; missing behavioral measure (response time); limited ecological validity (abstract stimuli)
