## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Multi-level EWA with time-varying parameters (monotonic effects variant): A_{i,j,t+1} = (1-ϕ_j)A_{i,j,t} + ϕ_j π_{i,j,t}; choice = (1-σ_j)P_A + σ_j[(1-κ_j)P_C + κ_j P_E]; conformity via f_j exponent; experience bias via β_j; time-varying σ, f, β, κ, ϕ, λ via monotonic effects or Gaussian processes.
- **model_family:** Rescorla-Wagner
- **model_class:** PE learning
- **all_models_tested:** - Baseline multi-level EWA (constant parameters): 6 participant-level params (σ, κ, f, β, λ, ϕ) + multi-level structure; metric: Bayesian posterior (MCMC/Stan) - Multi-level EWA with monotonic time-varying effects: same params with time-varying extensions; metric: Bayesian posterior - Multi-level EWA with Gaussian process time-varying effects: same params with GP extensions; metric: Bayesian posterior - Spatial vs temporal contrast model (indicator/dummy variables for first 5 rounds after each change type)  Note: No formal model comparison metric (BIC/AIC/WAIC) is reported between these model variants. The authors treat them as complementary analyses rather than competing models.
- **model_mb_mf:** MF (model-free reinforcement learning with social information weighting)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 237 (200 social learning experiment [127 women, 73 men; mean age 30.2, SD 11.8]; 37 individual learning control [18 women, 19 men; mean age 29.9, SD 13.8]); 25 sessions of 8 participants each for the social condition.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=30.2
- **ecological_validity:** Laboratory microsociety experiment with a stylised four-armed bandit task framed as farming. Migration and experience gradients add ecological realism beyond typical fixed-group designs. However, the social information was limited to choices and experience labels (no payoff information from others), interactions were anonymous, and the environment was far simpler than real-world social learning contexts. Authors explicitly note WEIRD sample concern.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** No formal model comparison metric (e.g., WAIC, LOO-CV) reported between model variants -- authors treat baseline, monotonic, and GP models as complementary rather than competing. Post-hoc simulations showed agents learned much slower than humans, indicating the model is "most definitely missing some important cognitive detail." The supplement file available is the peer review history, not the electronic supplementary material (hosted on Figshare), so supplementary figures S1-S7 could not be verified.
- **limitations_reported:** "Simulated agents showed similar overall patterns of behaviour but learned much slower than real human participants"; "our model is most definitely missing some important cognitive detail typical of real human participants"; "the way our model estimates time-varying learning parameters through monotonic effects and Gaussian processes is purely stochastic, i.e. relying on statistical associations, and not mechanistic, i.e. grounded in cognitive processing"; WEIRD sample -- "participants in our study were also predominantly Western, relatively educated, and from an industrialized, rich, and democratic country"; "Experiments are gross simplifications of reality.
- **limitations_categorized:** Model misspecification (slow simulated learning); limited ecological validity; stochastic rather than mechanistic time-varying parameters; limited generalizability (WEIRD sample); task simplicity
