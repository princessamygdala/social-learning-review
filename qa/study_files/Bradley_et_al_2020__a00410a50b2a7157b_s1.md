## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social reward learning in competitive bidding; oxytocin effects on sensitivity to social reward in schizophrenia
- **study_focus_short:** Social reward learning in competitive bidding
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from monetary and social outcomes in a competitive auction to update bidding strategy over trials   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (virtual competitors in auction) -- **joint** (outcome jointly determined by own bid and others' bids)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (monetary win/loss from auction)     - Source content (social): outcome (winning/losing the auction as social status signal)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): self (own bidding strategy)     - Target content (non-social): action/policy (optimal bid factor)     - Target content (social): state (subjective value of social reward from winning)
- **task_description:** Participants bid against five virtual players over 35 trials in a common-value auction, where overbidding increases the chance of winning (social reward) but leads to monetary losses. Before bidding, participants were taught the optimal risk-neutral strategy (RNNE), creating a measurable tension between monetary and social reward.

- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (5 virtual opponents)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Virtual suitcase items with estimated value ranges, photos of virtual competitors and participant, monetary outcomes, win/loss feedback sounds
- **method:** pharmacological / behavioural

- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Group x trial interaction on bid factor: controls decreased bids over time (b = -0.0075, t = -4.58, p = 4.7e-06), patients did not (b = -0.0026, t = -1.35, p = 0.18) (group x trial: b = 0.0053, t = 2.18)   - Drug x trial interaction on bid factor: placebo group decreased bids over time (b = -0.0074, t = -4.40), oxytocin group did not (b = -0.003, t = -1.62) (drug x trial: b = 0.0049, t = 2.01)   - Oxytocin increased social motivation to win (ρ_win) in schizophrenia (Mann-Whitney U = 30.00, p < 0.001) and trend-level in controls (U = 227.00, p = 0.10)   - Kruskal-Wallis across 4 groups for ρ_win: H(3) = 19.49, p < 0.001   - No group or drug differences for ρ_loss (H(3) = 0.62, p = 0.891), α (H(3) = 2.89, p = 0.442), or θ (H(3) = 0.51, p = 0.918)   - Trial-by-trial analysis: Group x Drug interaction on bid adjustment, F(1,75) = 162.35, p < 0.001, ηp² = 0.68   - Schizophrenia oxytocin vs placebo bid-outcome correlation: t(30) = 17.02, d > 1   - No group or drug effects on Devil's Task (risk-taking control)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 88 (51 healthy controls [26 PL, 25 OT], 37 schizophrenia patients [22 PL, 15 OT]); all male; after exclusions from original N = 93 (54 controls, 39 patients). Ages: controls PL M = 43.16 (SD = 13.69), controls OT M = 44.88 (SD = 12.21); patients PL M = 46.23 (SD = 13.6), patients OT M = 40.47 (SD = 12.23).
- **population_category:** mixed
- **population_age_range:** M=43.16 (SD=13.69)
- **ecological_validity:** The Auction Game creates a competitive social context with monetary stakes, but participants bid against virtual (not real) players. Steps were taken to enhance believability (photos, mock phone calls, exclusion of disbelievers). However, the task does not reflect the true complexity of real-world social interaction. No computer-only control condition to isolate social from non-social RL components.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) No computer control condition to dissociate social from non-social RL; (2) Groups not matched on education (significantly different, p = 0.01); (3) Male-only sample limits generalizability; (4) Mean fitted parameter values for ρ_win, ρ_loss, α, θ not reported (only group comparisons via non-parametric tests); (5) Exact RL model equations are presented only with placeholder symbols in the text (likely due to text extraction from PDF); full equations referenced to van den Bos et al. (2013b); (6) Pharmacodynamics of intranasal oxytocin poorly understood, timing effects possible.
- **limitations_reported:** Despite the interactive nature of the Auction Game and steps we took to maximize the feeling of being in a real social context, it does not reflect the true complexity of real-world interaction"; "we did not include a condition in which participants played against a computer to control for impairments in nonsocial reinforcement learning"; "we did not assess cognition, which is relevant given that cognitive limitations are a potential cause of overbidding in healthy people and cognitive impairment has been linked to deficits in reinforcement learning in schizophrenia"; "we enrolled only men in this study, and results are not generalizable to women given oxytocin's sexually dimorphic effects"; "the pharmacodynamics of intranasal oxytocin are poorly understood"; "our sample size is modest; replication and extension of our findings by others is critical
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; lack of non-social control condition; cognition not assessed; limited generalizability (male-only); pharmacological uncertainty; sample size
