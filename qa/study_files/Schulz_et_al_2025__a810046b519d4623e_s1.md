## Quality
- **sample_size:** Exp 1: N = 123 (58 female, 63 male, 2 other; mean age = 38.72, SD = 11.07); Exp 2: N = 111 (54 female, 55 male, 2 other; mean age = 36.77, SD = 13.27). Total N = 234. US adults recruited via Prolific.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=38.72
- **ecological_validity:** Low ecological validity — highly abstract task with alien planet framing, abstract symbols for news stations, and binary policy choices. No real news content, no real-world social identity of sources. Authors acknowledge this as a cost of tight experimental control. Questionnaire measures of real-world trust and media consumption did not correlate with task behavior.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) No formal model comparison metric (BIC/AIC/BMS) reported between Bayesian and RW models — only qualitative ANOVA-based comparison of simulated trajectories. (2) Medium-to-high number of free parameters (9) that are "partially intertwined" per authors' own admission. (3) No parameter recovery reported. (4) No model recovery reported. (5) Blocked source presentation means order effects possible (though randomized across participants). (6) The RW model comparison uses a different functional form (quadratic Q-values) which is not standard and may not provide the fairest comparison.
- **limitations_reported:** the purpose of our model fitting was to show general alignment between Bayesian-like learning patterns and participant behavior and not to make strong statements about a particular class of model or parameters"; "we rely on a medium to high number of parameters that are partially intertwined. Future modelling will have to disentangle these factors"; "our tightly controlled task came at the cost of external validity, and the statistical learning task we presented participants with was necessarily abstract"; "our pure Bayesian ability to learn the trustworthiness of information sources might only be a marginal factor driver behind our real-world attitudes and media choices"; "we did not set out this study to focus on individual differences, and larger samples are usually necessary to pin down individual differences in this field
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; model complexity / parameter identifiability; task simplicity; limited generalizability to real-world media behavior; sample size for individual differences
