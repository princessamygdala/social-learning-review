- **concerns:** (1) Hypothetical rewards and hypothetical social partners reduce ecological validity. (2) All participants are US undergraduate psychology students, limiting generalizability. (3) Between-subjects inequity manipulation means individual participants never experience both conditions, which strengthens causal inference but limits within-subject analyses. (4) Social perception ratings collected after the task, not before, so ratings may be influenced by the learning experience (though authors show no significant between-condition differences). (5) The "social" component modulates a fundamentally non-social learning task — the social context shapes the reward signal but the learning target is non-social (stimulus-action mappings). - Confidence: HIGH
- **limitations_reported:** One potential limitation of this study is the hypothetical nature of the monetary reward as well as the social partner. Participants were asked to imagine that the rewards were real and that a portion was actually given to another person"; "we also acknowledge that all participants are young adults in the United States, leaving open questions about the degree to which findings from our sample generalize to people situated within different socioeconomic systems" - Confidence: HIGH
