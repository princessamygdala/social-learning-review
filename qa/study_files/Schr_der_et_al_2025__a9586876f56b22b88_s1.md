## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Self-belief formation and revision; how initial expectations and confidence shape biased learning from performance feedback about self and others (confirmation bias in belief updating).
- **study_focus_short:** Self-belief formation and revision
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from manipulated performance feedback about one's own or another person's cognitive estimation ability, updating ability beliefs over time, and resisting revision when feedback reverses.   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (experimenter/reference group providing social comparison feedback)       - Social comparison feedback is delivered as percentile rank relative to 350 university students     - Source content (non-social): outcome (performance feedback percentile)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): self / other (another participant)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; ability belief about cognitive estimation performance)
- **task_description:** In the Learning of Own Performance (LOOP) task, participants estimate attributes of objects (heights, weights, quantities, distances) and receive manipulated percentile-rank feedback about their own or another person's performance relative to a reference group. In session T1, feedback is predominantly positive or negative by category to establish novel ability beliefs; in session T2, feedback contingencies reverse to probe belief revision.
- **task_paradigm:** Self-evaluation / self-esteem task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), dyadic (another participant or confederate observed but no direct interaction)
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** confederate
- **stimuli:** Estimation questions (heights, weights, quantities, distances), percentile-rank performance feedback, confidence rating scales
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural
- **main_result:** - Self-specific negativity bias during belief formation at T1: negative PEs led to greater updates than positive PEs for self (interaction Agent x PE-Valence x Session: β = 0.07, 95% CI [0.01; 0.13]) - Lower initial self-expectations linked to more negatively biased belief formation (ρ = 0.23, p_FDR = .035) - Confidence increased with learning during T1 (post T1 vs. pre T1: β = 11.15, 95% CI [8.08; 14.23]) - Learning rates at T2 greatly reduced vs. T1 (main effect Session: β = −0.16, 95% CI [−0.19, −0.12]) - Higher pre-T2 confidence linked to less belief revision (ρ = −0.27, p_FDR = .012) - Confirmatory bias in revision: lower initial expectations at T2 predicted more negatively biased updating for both self and other
- **effect_size:** - Interaction Agent x PE-Valence x Session: β = 0.07, 95% CI [0.01; 0.13], t(490) = 2.15 - Self negativity bias T1: α_Self/PE+ vs α_Self/PE−: t(490) = −6.08, p_Tukey < .001 - Main effect Agent on valence bias: β = 0.25, 95% CI [0.17; 0.33], t(98) = 5.96 - Main effect Session on learning rates: β = −0.16, 95% CI [−0.19, −0.12], t(358.23) = −8.23 - Initial expectation x self valence bias T1: ρ = 0.23, 95% CI [0.03; 0.41] - Confidence post T1 vs pre T1: β = 11.15, 95% CI [8.08; 14.23], t(1401) = 7.12 - Pre-T2 confidence x self learning rate T2: ρ = −0.27, 95% CI [−0.44; −0.07] - Pre-T2 confidence x learning rate change T1-T2 (self): ρ = 0.31, 95% CI [0.12; 0.48]

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 99 (from two pooled studies: n1 = 40, n2 = 62; 3 excluded from original 102; 79 females; ages 18–31, M = 22.49, SD = 2.82)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–31
- **ecological_validity:** Low-to-moderate. The task uses artificial cognitive estimation domains (heights, weights, quantities, distances) that lack everyday relevance, and feedback is fully manipulated. The authors explicitly acknowledge using "somewhat artificial belief domains" to avoid confounds from emotionally salient domains. No real social interaction occurs — participants do not interact directly. The other participant or confederate is passively observed. However, the two-session design with belief formation and reversal adds temporal ecological structure.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Individual fitted parameter means (α values, w values) are not reported — only valence bias scores and group-level statistics on learning rates are given. RStan scripts available only upon request, not publicly shared. - The pooled sample combines two studies with different inter-session intervals (up to 24h vs. ~12h; sleep vs. wake conditions) — although the authors controlled for this and found no Group effects, the heterogeneity is notable. - The "other" condition involves observing feedback about a co-participant/confederate without direct interaction, making the social component relatively thin. - No explicit limitations section in the paper.
- **limitations_reported:** Authors note the use of "somewhat artificial belief domains" to avoid confounds from emotionally or motivationally charged domains; they discuss that estimation abilities have "little impact on everyday life"; they note that RStan scripts are available only upon request rather than publicly shared; they acknowledge that the negativity bias during belief revision did not reach statistical significance for the self-other interaction at T2.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; task simplicity; code availability limitations; potential lack of statistical power for revision-phase effects
