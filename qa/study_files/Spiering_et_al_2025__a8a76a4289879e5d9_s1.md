## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social credit assignment / controllability inference in cooperative interactions. Participants learn how to attribute ambiguous joint outcomes to themselves versus another player, and learn their level of control over feedback through active disambiguation (deliberately performing badly to observe consequences).
- **study_focus_short:** Social credit assignment / controllability inference in cooperative interactions
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from joint performance feedback about self-performance, other-performance, and one's own controllability in a cooperative game   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): **joint** (self + other)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (parametric feedback reflecting weighted sum of self and other performance)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): self, other, and self-other control relationship       - If joint: marked as **joint** (control = relative weighting of self vs other)     - Target content (social): state (performance levels of self and other; controllability/agency)
- **task_description:** Participants played simple games alongside a fictive other player and received a single parametric feedback score reflecting the weighted sum of their own and the other's performance (weighted by a control parameter). They rated their beliefs about self-performance, other-performance, and control on each trial, and could deliberately perform badly ("active disambiguation") to observe how feedback changed and thereby infer their controllability.
- **task_paradigm:** Joint action / coordination
- **players:** Single agent (participant), dyadic (fictive other player; computer-generated based on real people's data). Multiple other players across task phases/blocks.
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Simple games (rockslide, bouncing ball, slingshot, light game), parametric feedback scores (0-100), neutral female face stimuli for other players (Chicago Face Database)
- **method:** fMRI / online / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI + behavioural (online replication sample)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N=67 (31 fMRI after exclusions [22 female, aged 18-33]; 36 online after exclusions [21 female, 1 diverse, aged 18-40]). Original: 36 fMRI recruited, 69 online recruited.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–33
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. The task uses a novel cooperative game paradigm with parametric feedback reflecting joint performance, which captures key features of real-world credit assignment (ambiguous shared outcomes, varying control). However, the "other player" is fictive (computer-generated), metacognitive introspection is deliberately disrupted via the logistic performance mapping, the games are abstract, and the social interaction is one-directional (no true dyadic interaction). The authors acknowledge that in more natural settings metacognitive assessment of performance might also contribute to control estimation.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - No formal quantitative model comparison metric (BIC, AIC, LOOIC) reported; models compared qualitatively via regression fits and rating errors - The "other player" is fictive, reducing social authenticity - Online sample had high exclusion rate (30/69 excluded for no AD behaviour), raising generalizability concerns - No parameter recovery or model recovery analyses reported - Sample size not pre-determined statistically ("No statistical methods were used to pre-determine sample sizes") - Mean fitted parameter values not reported for the Bayesian observer models
- **limitations_reported:** Metacognitive introspection disrupted by task design, which may differ from natural settings where metacognition contributes to control estimation; only half of online participants exhibited AD, pointing to differences between lab-based and online data collection or individual differences in strategy use; findings limited to dyadic interactions, unclear if generalizable to larger social groups; participants rewarded for rating accuracy not joint performance, which may alter AD efficiency; focus on healthy adults only.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; task simplicity; limited generalizability (dyadic only); sample differences (lab vs online); incentive structure; limited clinical generalizability
