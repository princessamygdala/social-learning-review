## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Confidence learning; learning others' ability to compute confidence in others' perceptual decisions (mentalizing learning / trait inference)
- **study_focus_short:** Confidence learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from feedback about another player's choice accuracy to update an estimate of the other player's sensory noise (ability), which is then combined with one's own sensory representation of decision difficulty to compute confidence in the other's choices.   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (three other players of varying ability)       - Not joint     - Source content (social): outcome (choice accuracy feedback on other's decisions)       - Not joint   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (each of three other players)       - Not joint     - Target content (social): state (mental state; sensory noise / ability — latent trait governing perceptual performance)       - Not joint
- **task_description:** Participants viewed random dot motion stimuli and placed post-decision wagers (bets) on perceptual decisions made by either themselves or one of three other players of varying ability (low, medium, high), choosing between a safe guaranteed reward and a risky option contingent on decision accuracy.
- **task_paradigm:** Social comparison task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (3 other players of varying ability: low ~0.55, medium ~0.75, high ~0.95 accuracy)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** unclear
- **stimuli:** Random dot kinematograms (varying coherence), monetary gamble options (safe vs. risky reward values)
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Participants tracked other players' ability and combined it with decision difficulty to guide post-decision wagers (ability on other-trials: t(20) = 4.52, p < .001; ability x type interaction: t(20) = -4.60, p < .001) - ToM-model (integrating belief state with learned other's sensory noise) outperformed S-model (self-projection) and Q-model (simple value tracking) based on PSIS-LOO and WAIC - LIP tracked motion coherence on both self- and other-trials but with opposite sign (interaction: t(20) = 3.07, p = .006) - TPJ encoded model-based confidence estimates selectively on other-trials (confidence on other-trials: t(20) = -2.17, p = .043; confidence on self-trials: t(20) = -0.60, p = .555) - dmPFC encoded confidence on both trial types in inverse manner (confidence on other-trials: t(20) = -2.67, p = .015; confidence on self-trials: t(20) = 1.79, p = .089) - Functional connectivity between LIP and TPJ/dmPFC increased on other- vs. self-trials (PPI: TPJ t(20) = 2.50, p = .021; dmPFC t(20) = 2.56, p = .019) - TPJ and dmPFC tracked social prediction errors (TPJ: t(20) = -3.18, p = .005; dmPFC: t(20) = -3.12, p = .005)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 21 (12 female; mean age = 22.6 +/- 4.4 years; 1 excluded from original 22 for poor performance in prescan)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=22.6
- **ecological_validity:** Low — abstract perceptual task (random dot motion) with pre-created "other players" who were not actually present during scanning. No real-time social interaction. The other players' data were taken from pilot participants, so no genuine social exchange occurred. However, the confidence computation studied (combining difficulty and ability estimates) is ecologically relevant to real-world social evaluation.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Other players were not real social agents present during the task; their choices were pre-recorded from pilot data, which limits social ecological validity - Small sample size (N = 21) - Mean fitted parameter values not reported (only priors and ranges) - The learning that occurs is specifically about a perceptual ability parameter (sensory noise), which is relatively narrow as a social learning construct - Only t-statistics reported; no standardized effect sizes (Cohen's d, etc.) for main effects
- **limitations_reported:** Our study only addresses one of the two sides of a social confidence computation" — the paper examines predicting others' accuracy but not inferring how confident others themselves feel; the authors note that reciprocal confidence inference awaits further study; the authors acknowledge that an analogous task with a non-human system might recruit similar mechanisms and that the study was not designed to address social uniqueness; the authors note that the ToM-model is one specific implementation and that a more model-free heuristic (combining S- and Q-models) could also account for the data, with future psychophysical studies needed to disambiguate these accounts.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; limited scope of social learning construct; social uniqueness not tested; model non-identifiability with alternative heuristic accounts; sample size
