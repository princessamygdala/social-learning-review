- **study_focus_short:** Prosocial learning / reward learning under mortality salience
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from monetary reward/no-reward feedback about stimulus-reward associations for different beneficiaries (self, stranger, no one) under mortality salience vs. negative affect priming   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): world       - Monetary reward feedback (100 points or 0 points)     - Source content (non-social): outcome       - Binary reward/no-reward feedback   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world       - Stimulus-reward probability associations (abstract visual patterns)     - Target content (non-social): stimulus       - Which of two abstract symbols is associated with higher reward probability  Note: The beneficiary manipulation (self/stranger/no one) adds a social dimension to the *goal* of learning, but the learning source and target themselves are non-social (feedback about abstract symbol-reward contingencies). Prior to the task, participants received either mortality salience (MS) or negative affect (NA) priming via 28 statements they judged agreement with.
- **task_paradigm:** Probabilistic reward learning
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (self, stranger, no one); between-groups design (MS group N=30, NA group N=30)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 60 (31 women; 30 MS group [17 women, ages 18–25, M = 20.0, SD = 1.35], 30 NA group [14 women, ages 18–24, M = 20.1, SD = 1.89]). 6 participants excluded from trial-by-trial FRN-PE correlation analysis due to null EEG codes.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–25
- **ecological_validity:** Low — laboratory task with abstract symbols and artificial beneficiary manipulation. Mortality salience induced via verbal statements rather than real threat. Probabilistic learning with binary outcomes is highly simplified relative to real-world learning under existential threat. All participants were Chinese undergraduates from a single university.
- **eligibility_flag:** The learning source and target are non-social (abstract symbol-reward associations). The social element is limited to the beneficiary manipulation (who receives reward: self/stranger/no one), which is a motivational context rather than social learning per se. The paper examines how mortality salience (a social/existential manipulation) affects reinforcement learning, but the learning itself is not fundamentally social — the agent learns stimulus-reward contingencies, not about social entities. FLAG: Borderline — social context manipulation on non-social learning. The "learning in a social context" criterion is only partially met (social motivational context, but the computational problem solved is non-social).
- **concerns:** - Only one computational model tested (basic RW) — no model comparison performed - No parameter recovery or model recovery reported - No simulation of the model before fitting - No posterior predictive checks - Exact fitted parameter values not reported in text (only visible in figures) - Temperature parameter $\beta$ values not reported at all - Between-subjects design (MS vs. NA) limits causal inference about within-person mortality salience effects - 75/25 reward probability may produce ceiling effects (acknowledged by authors) - Source localization of ERP components is inferred from prior literature, not directly assessed - Cultural generalizability limited (Chinese sample only)
- **limitations_reported:** the low task difficulty of reward learning resulted in a ceiling effect of learning performance, which might explain the null effect of MS priming on individual learning rates"; "the current work only tested a sample from a collectivistic society"; "it remains unclear whether MS-induced motivation for monetary reward is weaker in those with high-independence in a society in which independence is the locally prominent cultural orientation"; "a theoretical hypothesis regarding how death-related thoughts affect early perceptual/attentional processing and cognitive evaluation in general is lacking
- **limitations_categorized:** task simplicity; limited generalizability; limited ecological validity; sample homogeneity; theoretical framework gaps
