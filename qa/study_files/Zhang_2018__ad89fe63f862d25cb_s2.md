## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Expectation-biased social influence learning; how expectations about computer agents' expertise bias social influence in goal-directed learning.
- **study_focus_short:** Expectation-biased social influence learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from own reward outcomes and computer agents' choices about stimulus-reward contingencies, modulated by expectations about agents' expertise   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self       - Source content (non-social): outcome (reward/punishment)     - Source type (social -- flagged: automated system): group (4 computer agents replaying human choices)       - Source content (social): action/policy (agents' choices -- instantaneous only; no observational learning from outcomes)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (stimulus-reward contingencies)       - Target content (non-social): stimulus (which option is currently better)
- **task_description:** Participants performed a probabilistic reversal learning task with 4 computer agents (framed as either intelligent or unintelligent), making initial choices and bets, observing agents' choices simultaneously, then adjusting choice and bet before receiving outcome feedback.

- **concerns:** (1) Computer agents replay a subset of human choices from Study 1 -- agents are identical across conditions, only framing differs. (2) M6a/M6b did not converge, so cumulative reward history models could not be formally compared -- this is a limitation rather than definitive evidence against OL. (3) Unbalanced groups (N=36 vs N=29). (4) Semi-social nature limits generalizability to genuine social contexts. (5) Behavioral only -- no neural data.
- **limitations_reported:** Authors note: observational learning models did not converge for computer agent data so could not be formally tested; social information from even "intelligent" computers was weaker than from real humans, consistent with human vs computer partner differences in prior literature; the current study only examined behavioral patterns, no neural data.
