## Quality
- **sample_size:** N=102 (50 Social Group, 52 Non-social Group; 53 women; aged 18-28, mean 21.5 (SD 2.3)); double-blind crossover (MPH vs. placebo)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–28
- **ecological_validity:** Lab-based probabilistic learning task with cover story manipulation (social vs. non-social). Social information was not from a real-time social interaction but from purported previous participants. The social manipulation was a cover story only; identical task structure across groups. Pharmacological challenge (MPH) adds ecological constraint.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) The social manipulation is a cover story only — the indirect information is identical across Social and Non-social groups, so any social effect is purely belief-driven rather than involving actual social interaction. (2) Preprint (not peer-reviewed). (3) The indirect source is always indirect regardless of social/non-social framing, so the design specifically tests whether a social label modulates learning from an indirect source, not whether social learning per se differs from non-social learning. (4) The Non-social Group showed higher indirect learning rates than the Social Group, suggesting the cover story did modulate learning, but this was not drug-dependent.
- **limitations_reported:** It is possible that social and non-social learning are underpinned by networks that can be neurochemically dissociated, though by a pharmacological agent other than MPH (e.g. by a serotonergic agent)"; the study cannot provide evidence in support of the adaptive specialisation view but also cannot definitively rule it out; the social information in this paradigm is indirect — the design cannot test whether MPH would affect social learning when social information is the direct source; the pattern of results regarding MPH effects on volatility adaptation vs. lower-level learning rates is somewhat surprising given known dopaminergic effects
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; pharmacological specificity (single drug); task design confound (indirect = social); limited generalizability (healthy young adults only); no neuroimaging
