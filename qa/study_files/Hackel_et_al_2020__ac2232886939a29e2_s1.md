## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Cooperation learning / norm learning — How prosocial tendencies and descriptive social norms shape the neural value computations underlying cooperation in a public goods game.
- **study_focus_short:** Cooperation learning / norm learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from others' cooperation/defection feedback to update expectations about group cooperation norms, which guide own cooperative decisions.   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (group members from fictitious universities)       - Not joint     - Source content (social): action/policy (cooperation vs. defection decisions of other players)       - Not joint   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): group (university group norms of cooperation)       - Not joint     - Target content (social): state (descriptive norms; expected cooperation rate of each group)       - Not joint
- **task_description:** Participants played one-shot public goods games (PGGs) with students from two fictitious universities while undergoing fMRI. In each trial, participants chose to give $8 to a group pot (doubled and split 4 ways) or keep it, then received feedback about the other three players' decisions, with one university having 70% givers (prosocial norm) and the other 30% (antisocial norm).
- **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (3 other players per trial; 2 groups/universities)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** unclear
- **stimuli:** University emblems, binary give/keep decisions, monetary outcome feedback showing each player's contribution and payout
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Prosociality x Decision interaction in vmPFC: prosocial participants showed greater vmPFC for give vs keep, selfish participants the reverse (t(33) = 4.99, k = 53, p_cluster < .001)   - Keep > Give x Prosociality in right dlPFC: selfish participants showed greater dlPFC when cooperating, prosocial participants when free-riding (k = 13, p_cluster = .050)   - dlPFC-vmPFC connectivity moderated by prosociality: greater connectivity for non-dominant choices (r(33) = -0.23, bootstrapped 95% CI: [-0.56, -0.04])   - Norm detection moderated dlPFC-vmPFC connectivity during deviant decisions (r(33) = 0.50, bootstrapped 95% CI: [0.22, 0.69])   - dlPFC-vmPFC connectivity during deviant decisions predicted norm-compliant cooperation (OR = 1.97, 95% CI: [1.26, 3.10], Wald chi-squared(1) = 8.72)   - Descriptive norms impacted cooperation: more cooperation with prosocial school (OR = 2.15, Wald chi-squared(1) = 26.4)   - Norm detection effect on cooperation (d = 0.67)   - Expected number of givers (from computational model) associated with rTPJ activation (peak z = 4.59, k = 55)   - Emotional preference replication: vmPFC (t(33) = 5.33, k = 101); dlPFC (t(33) = 5.62, k = 36)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 47 recruited (32 female); N = 42 after exclusions; N = 35 for main fMRI prosociality analyses (7 excluded for invariant choices); N = 34 for norm analyses. Ages M = 20.85, SD = 2.60. University students from 16 northeastern colleges.
- **population_category:** undergraduates
- **population_age_range:** M=20.85 (SD=2.60)
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. The PGG is a well-established economic game but involves fictitious universities and computerized opponents rather than real social interaction. One-shot framing reduces repeated-game strategic considerations. Monetary stakes are modest ($8 per trial endowment). Norm manipulation is artificial (pre-set base rates).
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Only one computational model tested; no model comparison or recovery analyses - Model fit at group level (fixed effects) — individual-level parameter variability not captured - Learning rate alpha fit at ceiling (= 1), suggesting possible overfitting or that the decay parameter is doing all the work - Small sample for neuroimaging (N = 35 for main analyses, N = 34 for norm analyses) - ROI analyses based on prior work rather than independent localizers - No prediction error signal found in vmPFC/VS for feedback (authors attribute to task design limitations) - PPI connectivity findings have relatively wide bootstrapped confidence intervals
- **limitations_reported:** We found no clear evidence that decisions to conform were more valued than decisions to deviate"; "we did not observe any activation in vmPFC or the ventral striatum" for prediction errors — authors speculate "limitations in our task design contributed to this null result" noting "our task involves only four discrete feedback outcomes and may be insufficiently sensitive to detect this variability"; "cooperation is complex and draws on a widely distributed network of neural systems" beyond vmPFC and dlPFC; the computational model was fit at group level because "some subjects had no variability in their choices"; "small variations in learning rates do not generally change the conclusions of this sort of fMRI analysis
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; task simplicity (limited feedback outcomes); small sample size; group-level model fitting only; limited model comparison; narrow ROI focus
