## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social reward learning / social anhedonia -- investigating computational and neural mechanisms of social reward exploration (learning to maximize compliments from unknown partners) in trauma-exposed Veterans with varying anhedonia and anxiety.
- **study_focus_short:** Social reward learning / social anhedonia -- investigating computational and

- **task_description:** In the Flattery Learning Instrumental Reward Task (FLIRT), participants completed 20 two-armed bandit games (15 trials each) in which they chose between two unknown partners to maximize compliments; partners' compliment rates were drawn from a Beta distribution and unknown to participants, who were told partners selected compliments based on seeing their photograph.
- **task_paradigm:** Two-armed bandit
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (2 unknown partners per game, 40 total across 20 games)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** human (recorded)
- **stimuli:** Neutral male face photographs (partners), text compliments (e.g., "You are intelligent") or "Hello," token-based choice interface
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI (model-based fMRI with parametric RPE regressors)

- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Small sample size (N = 33) limits statistical power for detecting moderate effects - Predominantly male sample (78.5%) with only male partners limits generalizability to female participants and cross-sex interactions - Brain-behavior correlations extracted from same whole-brain clusters identified in prior analysis (circular analysis concern acknowledged by authors) - Transdiagnostic sample with heterogeneous diagnoses makes interpretation of specific disorder effects difficult - Partners were fictitious; no true social interaction
- **limitations_reported:** Sample size relatively small and transdiagnostic, limiting generalizability to specific diagnostic groups; statistical power limited for detecting small to moderate effects; vast majority male Veterans with only male partners limits generalization to same-sex interactions for male Veterans; self-report measures less optimal than real-life measurements; brain-behavior association assessed within same sample clusters; task had fixed bandit horizon and did not include social punishment learning; including patients with severe mental illness would help sample broader range of social anhedonia
- **limitations_categorized:** Sample size; limited generalizability; limited ecological validity; gender imbalance; circular analysis; task simplicity; no punishment condition; self-report reliance
