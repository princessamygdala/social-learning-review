## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Observational learning of empathy; social transmission of empathy through observational reinforcement learning
- **study_focus_short:** Observational learning of empathy
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from observing another person's empathic/non-empathic reactions to update one's own empathy ratings toward pain recipients   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (demonstrator)     - Source content (social): action/policy (empathy ratings displayed by demonstrator)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (pain recipient / third party)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; how much empathy to feel for others' pain)
- **task_description:** Participants observed video clips of recipients receiving painful/non-painful electrical stimulation and saw a demonstrator's empathy rating, then rated their own empathy. In the high empathy group, the demonstrator's ratings were consistently higher than baseline; in the low empathy group, consistently lower.
- **task_paradigm:** Observational learning task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (2 demonstrators [confederates]; 4 pain recipients across sessions)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** confederate
- **stimuli:** Video clips of females receiving electrical stimulation, numerical empathy ratings (0-100 scale), lightning bolt pain cues
- **method:** fMRI / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI (Study 1); behavioural (Studies 2-4)
- **main_result:** - Observing empathic (vs. non-empathic) demonstrators increased (vs. decreased) participants' own empathy ratings (group x trial: chi-squared(1) = 39.57, p < 0.001) - Observational prediction errors positively predicted trial-wise changes in empathy ratings (chi-squared(1) = 25.75, p < 0.001 in behavioral replication) - Weight parameters (W1) in the first half of learning correlated with overall empathy change (high empathy group: rho = 0.39, p = 0.047; low empathy group: rho = -0.50, p = 0.009) - Learning-related changes stronger for human vs. computer demonstrators (study x PE interaction: chi-squared(1) = 5.34, p = 0.021) - W1 modulated left TPJ-left AI connectivity (MNI: -38, 4, -10; Z = 4.25, p(cluster-FWE) = 0.024) and left TPJ-vmPFC connectivity (MNI: -8, 50, -4; Z = 4.63, p(cluster-FWE) < 0.001) - AI activation correlated with empathy ratings at baseline and changed after learning (high empathy group: increased, t(25) = 2.18, p = 0.039; low empathy group: decreased, t(25) = -2.52, p = 0.018)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** Study 1: N=52 (55 recruited, 3 excluded; 26 high empathy, 26 low empathy; all female, mean age 21 +/- 2.1y). Study 2: N=56 (57 recruited, 1 excluded; 26 high empathy). Study 3: N=52 (56 recruited, 4 excluded; 25 high empathy). Study 4: N=55 (56 recruited, 1 excluded; 27 high empathy). Total across studies: N=215. All healthy females. Studies 1-4: Asian samples; additional Caucasian replication in supplement (N=34).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=21
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. The demonstrator ratings were algorithmically generated (not real people's ratings), though participants believed they were from real people (Studies 1, 3, 4) or computers (Study 2). The empathy task uses video clips of real pain stimulation, which is more ecologically valid than abstract stimuli. However, the social interaction is limited to observing ratings (no real interaction). Generalization to new recipients was tested.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Demonstrator ratings were algorithm-generated, not actual empathy ratings from real people, which may limit ecological validity. All-female samples limit generalizability to males. Model fit (r-squared) is modest (0.19-0.24). The winning model has 6 free parameters (plus the prediction-stage alpha = 7 total) for 36 pain trials, which is a relatively high parameter-to-trial ratio. No parameter recovery or model recovery reported.
- **limitations_reported:** We report findings from four independent samples that all consisted of young female participants"; "future research should investigate observational learning of empathy in males and across the life span"; "individual differences in intelligence and cognitive abilities should be taken into account"; "it would be important to investigate changes in actual prosocial behavior in real life
- **limitations_categorized:** limited generalizability (gender); limited generalizability (age); limited ecological validity (no real prosocial behavior measured); individual differences not fully accounted for
