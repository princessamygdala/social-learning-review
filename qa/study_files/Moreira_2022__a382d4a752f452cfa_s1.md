## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social decision-making preferences involving close others (parent vs. friend) in risky contexts
- **study_focus_short:** Social decision-making preferences involving close others (parent vs. friend)
- **learning_mode_description:** N/A -- no learning occurs. This is a decision-making study. Participants make independent risky decisions on each round of the CCT; there is no trial-to-trial updating or learning.
- **task_description:** Participants completed a modified Columbia Card Task where risky card flips yielded gains for one close other (parent or friend) and losses for the other, with conditions counterbalanced; they also completed fMRI tasks (parent-friend representation task, coin flip reward task) and wrote memories about close others.
- **task_paradigm:** Risky decision-making
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (1 nominated parent, 1 nominated close friend)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** none
- **stimuli:** Overturned cards with gain/loss point values, headshot photos and names of close others, coin flip gambles, written memory prompts
- **method:** fMRI / behavioural
- **method_full:** Study 1: behavioural; Study 2: fMRI + behavioural; Study 3: behavioural + NLP
- **main_result:** - Study 1: Population-level parent-over-friend social decision preference (Condition b = 0.19, 89% CI [0.12, 0.27]; 20.92% increase in expected odds of risky choice in parent-gain condition); relationship quality moderated preferences (Condition x PRQ b = 0.22, 89% CI [0.11, 0.34]; Condition x FRQ b = -0.35, 89% CI [-0.51, -0.19]); real rewards attenuated parent preference (Condition x Reward Type b = -0.38, 89% CI [-0.54, -0.23]) - Study 2: No robust group-level difference in value-based pattern expression between parent and friend representations (equivocal evidence across all signatures); modest evidence that individual differences in pattern expression predicted social decision preferences - Study 3: Parent memories more strongly associated with value topic (WEAT d = 0.40, 89% CI [0.34, 0.45]); however, WEAT scores inversely predicted social decision preferences (Value WEAT x Condition b = -0.06, 89% CI [-0.11, -0.01])

## Quality
- **sample_size:** Study 1: N = 174 (after 6 exclusions from 180; mean age 20.72, SD 2.16, range 18.06-30.81; 54 males); Study 2: N = 45 (after 3 exclusions from 48; ages 18-19); Study 3: N = 468 (aggregated from 3 samples; 73% female)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–19
- **ecological_validity:** A key strength of the dissertation is its explicit focus on improving ecological validity of social decision-making research by involving close others (nominated parent and friend) rather than strangers/confederates, and by pitting outcomes for multiple close others against each other. However, the CCT is still a lab-based paradigm with artificial point-based outcomes; monetary operationalization of value may not capture the full psychology of social value.
- **eligibility_flag:** Does not meet inclusion criteria. This dissertation examines social decision-making preferences (parent vs. friend) using Bayesian multilevel logistic regression, pattern expression analysis, and NLP methods. There is no computational model of learning (no RL, Bayesian learning, or equivalent). Decisions do not involve learning over time -- each CCT round is independent with no trial-to-trial updating. This is a decision-making study, not a learning study. All three studies fail the "uses computational modeling" and "learning occurs over time" criteria.
- **concerns:** 1. No computational model of learning is used -- this is a decision-making study, not a learning study, making it ineligible for the review's primary focus. 2. Evidence in Studies 2 and 3 is described as "modest" by the author -- effects only strong enough to rule out one direction but not definitively establish an effect. 3. The neural value signature had modest correlation with the Neurosynth reward map (r = 0.306-0.505), and results depended on smoothing level. 4. Inconsistent direction of effects between neural (Study 2) and cognitive (Study 3) representations and decision preferences. 5. Social desirability could potentially confound results, though the author argues against this.
- **limitations_reported:** Generalizability limited to western United States sample; each participant nominated only one parent and one friend; use of monetary rewards may be subject to cohort effects; social desirability may partially drive parent-over-friend preference; analytic flexibility in Bayesian analyses not fully explored; unclear whether sufficient within- and between-subject data collected to properly estimate neural and cognitive representations; written memories may have been too short to stably estimate WEAT scores; modest evidence in Studies 2 and 3 may reflect insufficient sample size.
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited generalizability; task simplicity; potential demand characteristics; analytic flexibility; sample size (Studies 2, 3); measurement validity (representations); limited ecological validity (monetary rewards)
