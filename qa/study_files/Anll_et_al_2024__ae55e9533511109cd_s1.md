## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Learning from advice / information sharing during reinforcement learning; how accumulated experiential knowledge (via RL) shapes the decision to share choices as advice to others.
- **study_focus_short:** Learning from advice / information sharing during reinforcement learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from own reward outcomes about option values, then deciding whether to share choice as advice   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self     - Source content (non-social): outcome (fish yield / reward)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (option/lake values)     - Target content (non-social): stimulus (reward value of options)
- **task_description:** Participants completed a 2-armed bandit task (framed as a fishing trip) where they chose between two lakes to maximise fish yield over three expeditions of varying difficulty (Easy, Medium, Hard), and on each trial decided whether to broadcast their choice as advice to future participants.
- **task_paradigm:** Two-armed bandit
- **players:** Single agent (participant), unidirectional advice to unspecified future participants
- **n_players:** single agent (1)
- **partner_type:** none
- **stimuli:** Abstract lakes (2-armed bandit), numerical fish yield feedback (Gaussian-distributed rewards)
- **method:** online / behavioural
- **method_full:** Behavioural / online
- **main_result:** - Advice-giving increased with trial progression and was higher on easier blocks (Block main effect: χ²(2) = 28, p < .001; Trial number: OR = 1.22) - Choice probability (from Q-learning) strongly predicted advice-giving (P(choice) main effect: OR = 19.89, χ²(1) = 1206, p < .001) - Positive prediction errors predicted higher advice probability (PE: OR = 1.72, χ²(1) = 18, p < .001) - Social anxiety × P(choice) interaction: more socially anxious participants more likely to advise during exploitation (OR = 1.28, χ²(1) = 7, p = .008) - PTM altruism marginally predicted higher advice (OR = 1.34, χ²(1) = 4, p = .046) - 36.2% of participants were "over-advisers" (advised on every trial) - Advised trials were more accurate than non-advised trials

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 373 (187 male, 186 female); median age 35 (M = 36.8, SD = 11.7). After excluding over-advisers: N = 238.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=36.8 (SD=11.7)
- **ecological_validity:** Online task on Prolific; abstract 2-armed bandit framed as fishing; advice was unidirectional to hypothetical future participants — no real-time social interaction. Limited ecological validity: no real social consequences, no face-to-face interaction, no confidence communication.
- **eligibility_flag:** The Q-learning model is fitted to non-social choice data (2-armed bandit). The "social" component (advice-giving) is analysed via mixed-effects logistic regression, not a computational model of social learning per se. The paper models individual reward learning and then examines how learned values predict a social behavior (advice). FLAG: borderline — computational model is non-social RL; social component is in the regression analysis, not the computational model itself.
- **concerns:** - Only one computational model tested — no model comparison - The Q-learning model captures non-social learning only; advice-giving is modelled statistically, not computationally - 36.2% of participants excluded as "over-advisers" — large proportion - No formal model of the advice-giving decision process (e.g., no utility model for advising) - Authors acknowledge model comparison was "beyond the scope" - Effect sizes reported as odds ratios from logistic regression; some may be inflated by the logistic scale
- **limitations_reported:** "more ecological settings with actual exposure to real-world social consequence and real social expectations may result in a heightened importance of psychosocial individual differences like social anxiety and altruism"; "lack of second-order confidence measures"; "trial progression could affect our results in unexpected ways" (boredom, attention, confidence fluctuations, evidence accumulation thresholds); "focused here only on two individual traits, prosociality and social anxiety, and on a limited number of potential consequences of advice giving
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; no confidence measure; potential confounds (boredom/attention); limited trait measures; task simplicity
