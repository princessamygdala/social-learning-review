## Quality
- **sample_size:** Exp 1a: N=200 adults (50 per condition + 50 priors; 77 excluded); Exp 1b: N=150 adults (50 per condition; 89 excluded); Exp 2: N=90 preschoolers (M=58mo, 30 per condition; 37 excluded)
- **population_category:** mixed
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Online study with imagined demonstrations (adults); in-person with video-based teaching style manipulation and live demonstration (children). Toy exploration paradigm has been validated in prior work but manipulation was via brief videos, which may not solidify expectations.
- **eligibility_flag:** Borderline eligibility — model is normative Bayesian inference, not a process model of iterative learning. One-shot inference from a single demonstration rather than learning over time. Children's behavioral results were null for experimental manipulation (only age and SES predicted play patterns). Thesis chapter.
- **concerns:** (1) Only one model tested. (2) Adults' demonstration was imagined, not actual. (3) Children's main prediction was not confirmed -- null result for condition effects. (4) High exclusion rate for children (41% failed memory check). (5) Model captures adults' explicit numerical estimates but does not predict children's implicit play behavior. (6) Importance inference was only marginally significant (p = 0.059).
- **limitations_reported:** Authors' own: Children's exploratory play not predicted by experimental condition; age and SES were stronger predictors; high attrition in children's experiment due to memory check failures; videos may not have been sufficient to solidify teaching style expectations in children; participants may have attributed differences to learners rather than teaching styles; adult behavioral data modeled but predictions tested in children; ongoing replication with child sample
- **limitations_categorized:** null behavioral result; high attrition; limited ecological validity; limited generalizability; indirect testing (adults modeled, children tested); task demands

- **other_notes:** This is a doctoral dissertation with three empirical chapters. Each chapter contains a computational model (Bayesian for Chapters 2-3; causal graphical for Chapter 4) and multiple behavioral experiments with both adults and children. The dissertation extends the Shafto & Goodman (2008) pedagogical model to cases where default assumptions are violated (teacher omits information, has a particular teaching style, or misrepresents the learner's knowledge). The models are computational-level accounts (Marr Level 1) providing normative predictions for how learners should reason, not algorithmic-level process models. The child behavioral results were mixed across chapters (some null, some failed replications), while adult results were generally consistent with model predictions. No supplement file was found, but the dissertation includes appendices A-D with additional model and method details.
