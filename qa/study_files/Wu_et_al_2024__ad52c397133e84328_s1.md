## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Observational learning; emulation vs. imitation; autism-like traits and social goal inference
- **study_focus_short:** Observational learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from observing a partner's slot machine choices (without seeing outcomes) about which token is currently valuable, via either goal emulation (Bayesian inference of the partner's goal) or action imitation (repeating the partner's action).   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (partner/demonstrator)     - Source content (social): action/policy (partner's slot machine choices)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (which token is valuable)     - Target content (non-social): state (world state; identity of the valuable token)
- **task_description:** Participants observe a fully informed partner choosing between slot machines (without seeing generated tokens), then on 1/3 of trials choose a slot machine themselves to maximize reward. Slot machine uncertainty is manipulated across blocks to test arbitration between imitation and emulation strategies.
- **task_paradigm:** Observational learning task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), single partner (pre-recorded demonstrator agent with full information)
- **n_players:** single agent (1)
- **partner_type:** human (recorded)
- **stimuli:** Slot machines with colored token distributions (red, green, blue), video recordings of partner's hand pressing buttons, colored tokens as feedback
- **method:** online / behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural / online
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Emulation propensity negatively correlated with autism-like traits (F1) (r = -0.243, 95% CI [-0.302, -0.182])   - Emulation bias parameter (delta) negatively correlated with F1 (r = -0.124, 95% CI [-0.186, -0.061])   - Emulation group had lower F1 than non-learning group (Mean diff = 0.749, eta-squared = 0.0717) and imitation group (Mean diff = 0.451)   - Emulation propensity predicted F1 after controlling for age, gender, cognitive ability (beta = -0.264, eta-p-squared = 0.0696)   - Emulation bias predicted F1 after controlling for all model parameters (beta = -0.130, eta-p-squared = 0.0165)   - F1 predicted emulation propensity after controlling for social anxiety and other psychiatric dimensions (beta = -0.225, eta-p-squared = 0.0350)   - No significant association between arbitration and autism-like traits (r = -0.016 for arbitration propensity; r = -0.031 for eta)   - Dynamic arbitration model was the best-fitting model (highest model frequency, exceedance probability, lowest AIC)

Parameters: beta (inverse temperature), delta [S] (emulation bias), eta (uncertainty weight), alpha_lr, alpha_G, alpha_R-B, alpha_sa, alpha_sc; lambda fixed at 0.99.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** Study 1: N=943 (after exclusion from initial N=1197; 500 females, 435 males, 7 nonbinary; age = 37.20 +/- 12.19); recruited from MTurk (1028) and Prolific (169)
- **population_category:** clinical
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Online observational learning task (~15 min) with pre-recorded partner videos; reasonable ecological validity for observational learning but partner is not a real-time interactive agent; social interaction is limited to observing pre-recorded choices. COVID-era data collection may have influenced self-report measures.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) SRS total scores were inflated relative to normative samples, possibly due to COVID-19 pandemic effects and online sample characteristics -- authors addressed this with factor analysis but the resulting Factor 1 measure is an approximation of autism-like traits, not equivalent to validated clinical measures. (2) The partner is a pre-recorded agent, not a real interactive partner, limiting the social dimension. (3) Study 1 had asymmetric task design where emulation led to higher accuracy, confounding emulation propensity with general cognitive ability (addressed in Study 2). (4) No neuroimaging data.
- **limitations_reported:** We mainly relied on the SRS to measure autistic traits while several other widely applied measurements such as the 50-item Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ) and 36-item Broad Autism Phenotype Questionnaire (BAPQ) may provide more robust measures with greater specificity to autism"; "data in Study 1 was collected at the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic and during the height of lockdowns... may have caused a subset of items relating to direct social interactions to be overscored"; "both studies were conducted online, and it is possible that online participants have different personality and behavior profiles compared to people who come to the lab for in-person studies"; "it is still unclear from our data whether imitation is affected (either strengthened or impaired) by heightened autism-like traits"; "the current task was designed to separate imitation and emulation rather than optimize arbitration
- **limitations_categorized:** Measurement validity (self-report scale confounds); limited ecological validity (COVID-era data collection, online sample); task simplicity (not optimized for arbitration measurement); limited generalizability (general population, not clinical ASD sample)
