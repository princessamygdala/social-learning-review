## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social influence learning; emotional interference with associative learning in a volatile environment. Specifically, how emotionally incongruent social feedback (facial expressions) acts as "social noise" that disrupts probabilistic learning about cue-outcome associations.
- **study_focus_short:** Social influence learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning a probabilistic cue-outcome association under volatility, where social emotional signals (facial expressions) introduce perceptual noise that interferes with belief updating.   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): world       - Auditory cue (high/low tone) and binary outcome (correct/incorrect feedback)     - Source content (non-social): outcome       - Correctness feedback on cue-outcome prediction, blurred by emotional face     - Source type (social): other       - Emotional face (happy/sad) presented alongside feedback     - Source content (social): stimulus       - Emotional valence of face image (congruent or incongruent with feedback valence)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world     - Target content (non-social): state (world state)       - Probabilistic association between auditory cue and visual outcome; environmental volatility
- **task_description:** Participants heard a high or low tone on each trial and predicted whether a female or male face would follow (probabilistic associative learning). The association probability changed dynamically across 300 trials. Feedback on correctness was accompanied by a randomly assigned happy or sad emotional face, creating emotionally congruent or incongruent feedback conditions.
- **task_paradigm:** Probabilistic reward learning
- **players:** Single agent (participant), no interactive partner. Emotional faces are stimuli, not interactive agents.
- **n_players:** single agent (1)
- **partner_type:** human (recorded)
- **stimuli:** Auditory tones (high/low), face images (male/female; happy/sad/neutral emotional expressions), binary correctness feedback ("X" mark), monetary incentive.
- **method:** MEG / behavioural
- **method_full:** MEG (separate emotional face perception task) + behavioural (PAL-S task)
- **main_result:** - Social noise effect on decision change (SN_d) was significant under low irreducible uncertainty (t(33) = 2.221, p = 0.033) but not under high irreducible uncertainty (t(33) = -0.422, p = 0.676) - HGF-S was the best-fitting model (exceedance probability = 0.993, protected exceedance probability = 0.993) - Social noise parameter τ correlated with SN_d under low irreducible uncertainty (Spearman's ρ = 0.406, p = 0.018) - Simulation with increased τ confirmed decreased belief fluctuation at all hierarchy levels and increased SN_d (t(33) = 3.09, p = 0.004) - τ Cluster in MEG: individual differences in ERD difference (happy vs. sad faces) in left anterior insula and TPJ regions correlated with τ
- **effect_size:** - SN_d under low irreducible uncertainty: t(33) = 2.221 - Spearman's ρ = 0.406 (τ vs. SN_d) - HGF-S exceedance probability = 0.993 - Simulation SN_d increase: t(33) = 3.09 - Note: Cohen's d, r-squared, or standardized betas not explicitly reported for main behavioural effects

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 34 (from initial 40; 6 excluded due to MEG recording failures and digitizer failure); age mean = 23.94 +/- 3.37 years; male/female = 16/18; IQ mean = 121.18 +/- 10.45
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Low-moderate. Abstract auditory-visual associative learning task with emotional face stimuli. The "social noise" is operationalized as emotional face incongruency with feedback valence, which is a simplified proxy for real-world social interference. MEG and behavioural tasks were performed separately, limiting ecological validity of the brain-behaviour correlation. Participants were explicitly told face expressions might be irrelevant.
- **eligibility_flag:** The "social agent" is an emotional face stimulus (not an interactive social agent). The learning target is non-social (cue-outcome association in the world). Social information acts as a distractor/noise source rather than being the object of learning. Flag: borderline social learning -- social information modulates learning but the learned content is non-social. The emotional faces are automated stimuli, not social agents.
- **concerns:** - The social element is limited to emotional face interference; no actual social learning (learning about or from another agent's behavior/beliefs) - MEG and behavioral tasks were separate experiments, reducing the directness of brain-behavior inferences - Mean fitted parameter values for the HGF-S model not reported in accessible text (Supplementary Table S1 referenced but values not extractable) - ω₃ parameter recovery was poor (r = 0.190, p = 0.058), raising concerns about identifiability of meta-volatility - No model recovery analysis (confusion matrix) reported - Number of parameters for SK-1 and RW models not explicitly stated - τ-ERD correlation cluster did not survive Bonferroni correction across frequency bands (p = 0.0364 vs. threshold 0.025/6 = 0.0042)
- **limitations_reported:** Neural responses were not measured during the PAL-S task itself, preventing direct assessment of social noise processing during learning; the cross-regression of parameters from two independent experiments decreased expected effect size; the possible effect of gender mismatch between selected gender and feedback face gender was not considered as a major factor; time courses of correctness-gender association and button-gender association were not counterbalanced among subjects.
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; separate neural and behavioral paradigms; potential confound (gender mismatch not fully addressed); lack of counterbalancing; no direct neural measurement during task.
