- **study_focus:** Prosocial learning — whether model-based vs. model-free reinforcement learning strategies differ when learning on behalf of self vs. another person.
- **study_focus_short:** Prosocial learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from reward outcomes of one's own choices to make value-based decisions that benefit self vs. another person   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self     - Source content (non-social): outcome (monetary reward/loss from chosen option)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (reward structure of environment — which spaceship/planet yields higher payoff)       - Note: The learning target is the same environment structure in both self and other conditions; the social manipulation is who receives the monetary benefit, not what is learned about.
- **task_description:** In a two-step reinforcement learning task (Kool et al., 2016), participants chose between spaceships that deterministically traveled to one of two planets, with slowly drifting reward outcomes (Gaussian random walk, range -4 to +5). Participants completed the task in separate blocks for themselves and for a physically present observer/confederate who evaluated their performance.

- **players:** Single agent (participant), dyadic (unfamiliar observer/confederate present and evaluating)
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** confederate
- **stimuli:** Abstract spaceships, colored planets, integer reward feedback (-4 to +5)
- **method:** behavioural

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 76 recruited (38 pairs); N = 36 analyzed (18 female; age: 24.39 ± 2.61); 2 excluded for missing trials/not understanding task. Note: only 1 member of each pair was the "player" — the other was the observer.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Low-moderate. Abstract spaceship task with drifting rewards; social manipulation limited to physical presence of unfamiliar confederate who evaluated performance. No real social interaction or naturalistic social context. Strangers paired together which limits social motivation.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Only one computational model tested — no model comparison, limiting ability to evaluate model adequacy - No parameter recovery or model recovery analyses - No simulation studies reported - Mean fitted parameter values not explicitly reported in text - Social manipulation may be weak — block-level beneficiary cue (not trial-level), unfamiliar partners, no real interaction - MF difference between conditions (p = .01) was noted but dismissed because MF was "less dominant" — this seems like a potentially meaningful finding that was underexplored - Sample size determination based on "previous studies" without formal power analysis - Participants only from Tehran campus area — limited generalizability
- **limitations_reported:** The presence of the partner might counteract and cancel the effect of making decisions for others; participants were only informed about the beneficiary at the beginning of each block (not trial-by-trial), which may have imposed additional cognitive load; the block design of the repetition of instructions could have imposed additional cognitive load; the manipulation of effort may not have been adequately effective; the player and observer were seated next to each other which could have manipulated perceived similarity, vicarious anxiety, and empathy factors; participants did not know each other (unlike previous studies showing self-other differences with familiar pairs).
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; weak social manipulation; task design limitations (block vs. trial-level cue); potential ceiling/floor effects; limited generalizability (unfamiliar pairs only); no power analysis reported.
