## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Self-Other boundary learning; learning how agent-specific prediction error circuits adapt to social context (degree of shared experience between Self and Other).
- **study_focus_short:** Self-Other boundary learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from shared sensory prediction errors about the degree of Self-Other boundary distinction   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self + (social): other       - **joint** on shared trials     - Source content (non-social): outcome (Bernoulli sensory prediction errors)       - **joint** on shared trials   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (co-player's false belief about Bernoulli parameter)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; belief about environment)
- **task_description:** Participants tracked a drifting Bernoulli parameter while simultaneously estimating a co-player's false belief about the same parameter, given that the co-player received a partially corrupted information stream; trials were "privileged" (self only), "shared" (both see), or "decoy" (other sees misleading info). Training manipulated the proportion of shared trials (Hi-Share: 50% vs Lo-Share: 12.5%) across two different co-players, with fMRI testing 24 h later under matched conditions.
- **task_paradigm:** Probabilistic ToM task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (2 co-players represented by cartoon avatars; real participants who played a simplified version on day 1)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** human (live)
- **stimuli:** Bernoulli outcomes (pink/yellow), trial-type images (privileged/shared/decoy), cartoon avatars, visual perspective-taking scenes with patterns
- **method:** fMRI / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI (+ quantitative MRI for magnetisation transfer / myelin mapping) + behavioural
- **main_result:** - Hi-Share training impaired FBT performance at test relative to Lo-Share (main effect of context: F(1,39) = 6.76, p = 0.013; interaction context x probe: F(1,39), p = 0.023; Self-probe impairment: t(39) = 2.96, d not reported) - Self-Other belief correlation higher in Hi-Share than Lo-Share in both training (F(1,39) = 21.7) and testing (F(1,39) = 3.8) - Winning model in Hi-Share contained leak parameter (lambda); Lo-Share did not - alpha higher for Other than Self in Hi-Share test (t(39) = -2.27, p = 0.029) - lambda:alpha ratio negatively correlated with Self-Other performance difference (rho = -0.45, p = 0.004) - Visual perspective-taking transfer: main effect of avatar on corrected drift rate change (F(1,45) = 7.4, p = 0.009) - Self-Other PE classification: better in Lo-Share than Hi-Share (t(39) = 2.1, p = 0.041) - Cross-decoding PEself/PEother: higher in Hi-Share than Lo-Share (t(39) = 2.75, p = 0.009) - Cross-decoding difference correlated with lambda:alpha ratio (r = 0.41, p = 0.009) - vmPFC white matter MT correlated with cross-decoding adaptation effect (844 voxels, p < 0.001 FWE) - vmPFC BOLD tracked P(share) at eta = 0.01 (192 voxels, p = 0.008 SVC); left temporal pole tracked P(share) at eta = 0.025 (583 voxels, p = 0.006 FWE) - vmPFC P(share) contrast correlated with cross-decoding adaptation (r = 0.35, p = 0.032) - Leak parameter (lambda) predicted temporal discounting propensity (p = 0.005, permutation test); adding fMRI cross-decoding improved prediction (p = 0.016) - lambda:alpha negatively correlated with discount factor (rho = -0.32, p = 0.043)
- **effect_size:** See inline above. Many effects reported as F-statistics, t-statistics, and correlations; Cohen's d not explicitly reported for most contrasts.

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Rescorla-Wagner with parallel Self/Other belief updates. Winning models varied by condition: - Hi-Share training: M68 (4 alpha [alpha_s1, alpha_s2, alpha_o1, alpha_o2], 2 tau [tau_s, tau_o], 1 delta, 1 lambda; 8 params) - Lo-Share training: M4 (1 alpha, 1 tau, 1 delta, 0 lambda; 3 params) - Hi-Share testing: M68 (same as above; 8 params) - Lo-Share testing: M13 (1 alpha, 2 tau, 1 delta, 0 lambda; 4 params)  Key formula: B_{t+1} = B_t + alpha * PE_self + delta * (0.5 - B_t) + lambda * PE_other
- **model_family:** Bayesian
- **model_class:** PE learning / Belief updating
- **all_models_tested:** 72 RW models tested per dataset, varying combinations of: - alpha: 1 shared, 2 (self/other), 2* (shared vs privileged/decoy), or 4 (self-privileged, other-decoy, self-shared, other-shared) - tau: 1 or 2 (self/other) - delta: 0, 1 shared, or 2 (self/other) - lambda: 0, 1 shared, or 2 (self/other)  Full list in Supplementary Table 2. Additionally, a drift-diffusion model was fit to the perspective-taking task, and hyperbolic discounting models (1-param and 2-param) to the intertemporal choice task.
- **model_mb_mf:** MF
- **model_params:** - alpha (learning rate) — separate for Self/Other and shared/privileged trials in winning model. [S] alpha_o (learning rate for Other updates) - tau (decision temperature / choice noise) — separate for Self/Other in winning model - delta (memory decay toward 0.5) - lambda [S] (Self-Other leak parameter — updates one agent's belief with the other agent's PE) - For DDM: drift rate (12 means by trial type), non-decision time, drift starting point, boundary separation (each with mean and variance) - For discounting: k (discount rate), S (Stevens' power law time perception), beta (choice stochasticity)  Mean fitted values: not reported numerically in text; shown in Supplementary Fig. 1 graphically.
- **social_param:** lambda (Self-Other leak) — governs degree to which one agent's PE updates the other agent's belief; key parameter distinguishing Hi-Share from Lo-Share contexts. Higher lambda = more Self-Other mergence.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 47 recruited (26 female), ages 19–54, mean 26.5 (SD 7.8). 6 completed days 1–2 only; 1 excluded for misunderstanding tasks. Final: N = 46 (25 female) for perspective-taking; N = 40 (22 female, mean age 26.8, SD 8.1) for all other analyses including fMRI.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 19–54
- **ecological_validity:** Lab-based task with cartoon avatars and cover stories (shop assistant scenario). Real co-players provided experience streams, but interaction was indirect (no real-time social exchange). Bernoulli tracking task is abstract and not naturalistic. Transfer to visual perspective-taking task provides some evidence of generalizability, but task remains artificial.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Effect sizes (Cohen's d) not reported for most key comparisons; primarily F-statistics and t-values - The relationship between lambda:alpha and temporal discounting is described by authors as "speculative" - Whether the FBT truly engages social cognition per se is acknowledged as uncertain — it could be viewed as non-social dual-tracking - vmPFC contrast–cross-decoding correlation (r = 0.35) only significant after excluding 2 outliers - No direct manipulation of temporal contingency (shared trial proportion used as proxy) - Cross-sectional MT analysis (not longitudinal); cannot infer myelin changes from training
- **limitations_reported:** It is important for future work to further investigate the learning algorithm behind this adaptation of Self-Other boundaries"; "It is important to consider whether our FBT engages cognitive processes that are social per se"; "Despite the above considerations, there remains a possibility that neither the FBT nor the perspective-taking task engages social cognition"; "We cannot say that our subjects engaged in computations that are exclusive to socially interactive settings"; "The notion that future Self is represented like Other is, at this stage, speculative
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; ambiguous social nature of task; speculative interpretation of domain-generality; no direct manipulation of key variable (temporal contingency); cross-sectional structural analysis
