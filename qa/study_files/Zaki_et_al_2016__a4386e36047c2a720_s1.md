## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Emotion inference learning — learning to correctly infer others' emotional states through feedback-driven cue weighting.
- **study_focus_short:** Emotion inference learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from correctness feedback about emotion inferences to update the informational value of social cues (visual vs. verbal) for predicting specific targets' emotions.   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self (own inference outcome)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (correct/incorrect feedback on emotion inference)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (specific social targets)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; emotional state — which cue type reliably signals the target's emotion)
- **task_description:** Participants viewed three social targets who each produced conflicting visual (silent video) and verbal (caption) emotional cues, guessed whether each target felt positive or negative, and received correct/incorrect feedback. Feedback contingencies differed across targets: one target's emotions were reliably predicted by visual cues (~88%), another by verbal cues (~88%), and a third was unpredictable (50%).
- **task_paradigm:** Impression formation task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (3 social targets: visual-correct, caption-correct, unpredictable)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** none
- **stimuli:** Silent emotion videos, short text captions describing emotional events, binary feedback (correct/incorrect)
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Participants learned to use the correct cue type for visual-correct targets (accuracy across bins: F(5, 85) = 3.55, p < .01) [confidence: HIGH]   - Marginal learning for caption-correct targets (F(5, 85) = 2.12, p = .07) [confidence: HIGH]   - No learning for unpredictable targets [confidence: HIGH]   - Faster learning from visual-correct vs. unpredictable target (F(5, 85) = 4.49) [confidence: HIGH]   - Faster learning from caption-correct vs. unpredictable target (F(5, 85) = 3.78) [confidence: HIGH]   - No difference in learning speed between visual-correct and caption-correct targets (F(5, 85) = 0.49, p > .75) [confidence: HIGH]   - Standard and augmented Q-learning models produced similar fits; secondary learning rate (α2) did not differ from zero [confidence: HIGH]   - Conjunction: Prediction error tracked nucleus accumbens, precuneus, visual cortex; negative PE tracked dACC, anterior insula, TPJ [confidence: HIGH]   - Conjunction: Expected value tracked vMPFC, precuneus [confidence: HIGH]   - Visual-correct > caption-correct: Expected value in fusiform gyrus, supramarginal gyrus, inferior parietal lobe; PE in visual cortex [confidence: HIGH]

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 18 (11 women; mean age = 23.7 ± 7.9 years). Originally N = 25; 7 excluded (2 computer error, 1 excessive movement, 4 insufficient behavioral variability). [confidence: HIGH]
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=23.7
- **ecological_validity:** The task used naturalistic silent emotion videos from real targets and text captions, which is more ecologically valid than abstract stimuli. However, the forced binary positive/negative emotion judgment with artificial feedback contingencies (88% correct for one cue type) is a simplification of real-world social learning where cues are typically congruent rather than conflicting. The authors acknowledge this limitation explicitly. [confidence: HIGH]
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Small sample size (N = 18) severely limits power for between-participant analyses (authors acknowledge only r > .60 detectable). [confidence: HIGH] - Mean β (inverse temperature) not reported despite being a free parameter. [confidence: HIGH] - No standardized effect sizes reported (only F and t statistics). [confidence: HIGH] - Augmented model comparison is limited by the fact that chosen and nonchosen cues carried redundant (inverse) information, making the α2 parameter difficult to interpret. [confidence: HIGH] - SPM2 used (older software version). [confidence: HIGH] - No formal model comparison criterion (BIC/AIC) reported; only LRT for augmented vs. standard. [confidence: HIGH]
- **limitations_reported:** The current study used a relatively small sample size of 18 participants. This afforded us enough power to detect learning effects in our repeated-measures within-participant design but nonetheless constitutes an important limitation"; "our study was quite underpowered to detect between-participant effects (18 participants, for instance, would only allow us to reliably detect correlations with r values over .60)"; "chosen and unchosen options provided inverse—and therefore redundant—information about targets' states... low learning rates over non-chosen options in our paradigm might reflect an artifact of our design, rather than a general feature of social learning"; "although our paradigm allowed us to isolate brain systems involved in one form of social learning, it might fail to approximate learning in other social settings"; "the presence of activity in a given brain structure does not allow for strong inference about specific underlying psychological processing" [confidence: HIGH]
- **limitations_categorized:** sample size; limited generalizability; task simplicity (conflicting cues only); reverse inference limitations; limited ecological validity
