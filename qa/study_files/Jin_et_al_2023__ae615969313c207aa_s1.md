## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Trust learning — how MDD patients learn to trust newly-met partners through repeated social interactions, with manipulation of situational unpredictability (reputation disclosure vs. non-disclosure).
- **study_focus_short:** Trust learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from partner's reciprocity/betrayal outcomes about partner's trustworthiness to guide trust (investment) decisions   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (anonymous partner/trustee)     - Source content (social): outcome (reciprocation or betrayal — monetary return or no return)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (anonymous partner/trustee)     - Target content (social): state (trustworthiness — expected utility of trusting the partner)
- **task_description:** Participants played as investors in a repeated Trust Game (rTG), interacting with 10 computer-simulated partners (5 per block) over 20 rounds each. In each round, participants chose whether to invest 2 yuan (tripled to 6 for the partner) or keep it; partners reciprocated (returning 4 yuan) or kept the money, with reciprocity probabilities varying across partners (10%, 30%, 50%, 70%, 90%). Reputation information (star ratings) was disclosed in one block and hidden in the other.
- **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Single agent (participant as investor), multi-target (10 anonymous computer-simulated partners with varying trustworthiness levels; 5 per reputation condition)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Alphanumeric partner codes, star ratings (1–9 stars for reputation disclosure condition), binary monetary feedback (reciprocation: 4 yuan return vs. betrayal: 0 yuan return)
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural
- **main_result:** - MDD patients made fewer investments than controls overall (exp(B) = 0.68, z = −2.34, p = .019) - MDD patients had significantly higher learning rate for losses than controls (partial η² = 0.13, Cohen's d = 0.76) - No significant group difference in learning rate for gains - Group difference in α_L was larger in reputation non-disclosure (Cohen's d = 0.66) than disclosure condition (Cohen's d = 0.48) - MDD patients had higher inverse temperature τ than controls (partial η² = 0.04, Cohen's d = 0.40) - α_L in reputation non-disclosure condition positively correlated with PHQ-9 (r = 0.34), harm avoidance dimensions (r = 0.28–0.32), and negatively with TEPS-consummatory contextual (r = −0.26) - Cross-validation with Hierarchical Bayesian approach replicated main findings

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 120 (68 MDD patients, 52 healthy controls); MDD: mean age 27.88 (SD = 6.44), 63% female; HC: mean age 26.10 (SD = 5.10), 54% female. All unmedicated MDD patients (no antidepressants for 2 weeks prior).
- **population_category:** mixed
- **population_age_range:** M=27.88 (SD=6.44)
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. The repeated Trust Game captures key features of real social interactions (trust building, reciprocity learning) with manipulation of reputation information. However, partners were computer-simulated (not real people), and no manipulation check was conducted on whether participants believed they were playing with real partners. The authors acknowledge this limitation. Binary investment choice (all-or-nothing) is less naturalistic than graded trust decisions.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) Partners were computer-simulated, not real; no manipulation check on believability. (2) Moderate parameter recovery — limited valid trials (average 49–73 per condition due to non-investment trials yielding no feedback). (3) Learning rates for gains showed only moderate recovery, raising possibility of Type II error for gains findings. (4) Coding task was significantly different between groups, though other neurocognitive measures were not.
- **limitations_reported:** Parameter recovery tests suggested moderate level of correlation between original and recovered parameters. This could be attributed to the limited number of valid trials that involve learning from feedback"; "The limited number of valid trials also prevents us from performing reliable parameter estimation for candidate models which specify different learning rates for partners with different levels of repay"; "Partners in the rTG are not real persons. However, we did not collect information about the extent to which participants believe that they were playing with real persons in real time.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited parameter recovery; limited valid trials; no manipulation check for partner believability; task simplicity (binary choice); ecological validity (computer-simulated partners)
