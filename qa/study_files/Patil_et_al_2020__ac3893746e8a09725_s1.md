- **ecological_validity:** Low -- all studies use hypothetical moral dilemma vignettes with no real consequences. Two-step RL tasks use abstract space travel or hypothetical shock paradigms. No real social interaction.
- **eligibility_flag:** FLAGGED -- This paper does NOT meet inclusion criteria for the following reasons: 1. **No computational modeling of learning**: The paper uses reinforcement learning two-step tasks (Daw et al., 2011; Kool et al., 2016) only as a behavioral measure of individual differences in model-based vs. model-free control (the weighting parameter *w*). The RL model is not used to model social learning -- it is used as a cognitive style index correlated with moral judgment. The paper's primary analyses are correlational (mixed-effects regression, random-effects meta-analysis, cortical thickness GLM), not computational modeling of a learning process. 2. **No learning over time in a social context**: Participants make moral judgments on hypothetical vignettes (sacrificial dilemmas). There is no trial-by-trial learning, no feedback-driven updating, and no social learning process. The two-step RL tasks are used in a non-social (space travel) or pseudo-social (hypothetical shocks) context as a measure of cognitive style, not as the phenomenon under study. 3. **The study focus is moral judgment/decision-making, not learning**: The paper investigates whether individual differences in reasoning predict utilitarian moral judgments. This is a one-shot judgment paradigm, not a learning paradigm.  ---  ### COMPUTATIONAL LEVEL
