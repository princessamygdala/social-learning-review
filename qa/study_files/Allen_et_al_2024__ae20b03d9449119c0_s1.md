## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Cooperation learning; reciprocal cooperation; social learning of counterpart cooperation strategies; individual differences in callousness and exploitativeness as predictors of cooperation-related learning signals in the default network.
- **study_focus_short:** Cooperation learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from a social counterpart's cooperation/defection decisions to predict and adjust one's own cooperation policy   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (trustee counterpart)     - Source content (social): action/policy (trustee's cooperation or defection decisions)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (trustee counterpart)     - Target content (social): action/policy (counterpart's likely future cooperation behavior; expected return on one's own cooperation policy)
- **task_description:** Participants played a modified iterative trust game with three fictional trustees of varying reputations (good, bad, neutral), choosing to keep $1.00 or invest it with the trustee across 48 trials per trustee (144 total). If they invested and the trustee returned, they received $1.50; if the trustee kept, they received nothing; counterfactual feedback about the trustee's decision was provided even on keep trials.
- **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (3 fictional trustees of varying reputations: good, bad, neutral)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** human (recorded)
- **stimuli:** Neutral male face photographs (NimStim), biographical vignettes describing trustee reputation, monetary outcomes ($0, $1.00, $1.50)
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Policy model dominated all alternatives (Bayesian omnibus risk < 0.001; protected exceedance probability = 1.00)   - Reciprocity effect: trustee return on prior trial predicted participant investment on next trial (beta = 0.62, SE = 0.07)   - Participants invested less with bad trustee vs. neutral (beta = -0.24, SE = 0.05) and vs. good (beta = -0.26, SE = 0.05)   - Policy PEs in default network predicted greater reciprocity (beta = 0.25, 95% CI [0.09, 0.39])   - Callousness negatively associated with default network PE signaling (beta = -0.39, SE = 0.10)   - Exploitativeness positively associated with default network PE signaling (beta = 0.26, SE = 0.10)   - Indirect effect of callousness on reciprocity via PE-DN (b = -0.05, 95% CI [-0.10, -0.02])   - Indirect effect of exploitativeness on reciprocity via PE-DN (b = 0.03, 95% CI [0.004, 0.07])   - Callousness moderated effect of reputation on investing (beta = 0.26, 95% CI [0.04, 0.45])   - Medial temporal subsystem uniquely moderated reputation effect on investing (beta = -0.31, 95% CI [-0.53, -0.03])   - Callousness negatively related to PE in dorsal attention network (beta = -0.24) and striatum (beta = -0.25); exploitativeness unrelated to these regions   - Callousness related to lower task earnings (beta = -0.26, SE = 0.09); exploitativeness to higher earnings (beta = 0.22, SE = 0.09)
- **effect_size:** - Reciprocity effect: beta = 0.62, SE = 0.07 - Policy PE in DN on reciprocity: beta = 0.25, 95% CI [0.09, 0.39] - Callousness on DN PE: beta = -0.39, SE = 0.10 - Exploitativeness on DN PE: beta = 0.26, SE = 0.10 - Indirect callousness -> PE-DN -> reciprocity: b = -0.05, 95% CI [-0.10, -0.02] - Indirect exploitativeness -> PE-DN -> reciprocity: b = 0.03, 95% CI [0.004, 0.07] - Callousness on reputation sensitivity: beta = 0.26, 95% CI [0.04, 0.45] - MT subsystem on reputation: beta = -0.31, 95% CI [-0.53, -0.03] - Antagonism on learning rate: beta = -0.05, SE = 0.02

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 168 behavioral (113 BPD probable/definite, 55 healthy controls); N = 150 fMRI (after excluding 18 for missing data, poor quality, excessive motion); N = 161 with self-report data. Ages: M = 31.16, SD = 9.30; 78% female.
- **population_category:** mixed
- **population_age_range:** M=31.16 (SD=9.30)
- **ecological_validity:** Participants interacted with fictional trustees (not real people) via pre-recorded vignettes and programmed reinforcement schedules. Although robust reputation effects suggest task was perceived as social, authors acknowledge they cannot definitively rule out that findings capture a broader nonsocial learning process. Limited ecological validity due to lab-based, computer-mediated social interaction.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) The study uses fictional trustees with pre-programmed cooperation rates, limiting ecological validity for real social interaction. (2) Cannot definitively disentangle social-specific learning from general bandit-type learning. (3) Sample enriched for BPD, limiting generalizability to general population (though BPD status controlled in sensitivity analyses). (4) Correlational design; personality was naturally varying, precluding causal inferences. (5) Mean fitted parameter values are not reported in the paper or supplement.
- **limitations_reported:** Observations were contingent on natural variation in personality, precluding strong causal inferences; cannot definitively rule out that findings capture a broader learning process that might extend to other, nonsocial bandit tasks; sampling strategy recruited BPD and healthy controls, and while BPD status did not attenuate effects in sensitivity analyses, cannot definitively rule out that callousness and exploitativeness function differently in BPD than healthy participants due to interactions with other constructs elevated in BPD; generalizability limited to more transdiagnostic or general population samples pending future replication.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; limited generalizability; correlational design; clinical sample enrichment; task simplicity (fictional trustees, not live interaction)
