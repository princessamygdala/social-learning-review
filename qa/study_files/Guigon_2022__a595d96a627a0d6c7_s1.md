## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Inferring others' preferences for information; social inference about information-seeking preferences; mentalizing about others' beliefs regarding uncertain news
- **study_focus_short:** Inferring others' preferences for information · social inference about information-seeking preferences
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from feedback about receiver's information-seeking choices to infer receiver's preferences for additional information about news   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (receiver from prior experiment)       - Source content (social): action/policy (receiver's choice to accept or refuse extra information)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (receiver)       - Target content (social): state (mental state; preferences for information)
- **task_description:** Participants (senders) in the fMRI scanner viewed true or false news items and rated their truthfulness confidence, then decided whether to send extra information to a receiver drawn from a pool of 20 receivers from a prior behavioral experiment. Senders were rewarded for matching the receiver's previously recorded choice to accept or refuse extra information. In the Control condition, no information about receivers was provided; in the Cue condition, participants were shown the social distance between the receiver and a political organization congruent with the news topic.
- **task_paradigm:** Probabilistic ToM task
- **players:** Single agent (participant as sender), multi-target (20 receivers from prior experiment; drawn one per trial)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** none
- **stimuli:** Brief news items (true and false, 140 characters max), covering ecology, social justice, and democracy topics; social distance cues (0-100 scale) to political organizations
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Winning Bayesian model outperformed all alternatives (PEP = .99) - Average classification accuracy: .64 (Control), .71 (Cue condition) - Send probability decreased by 22.7% per SD increase in confidence (OR = .723, 95% CI [.634, .824]) - Send probability decreased by 44.2% per SD increase in social distance cue (OR = .558, 95% CI [.485, .641]) - When judging news as fake with low confidence, participants were more likely to send extra info (OR = 3.27 for fake vs. OR = 1.78 for true) - In Cue condition, weight on distance parameter increased ~3-fold (from 5.49 to 16.95), while truthfulness weight decreased (from 4.63 to 2.77) - Female subjects had higher send probability than males (OR = 1.55, 95% CI [1.18, 2.03]) - Beliefs about truthfulness probability correlated with increased BOLD in vmPFC, striatum, dlPFC - Beliefs about receiver's social distance correlated with increased BOLD in bilateral TPJ - Integration of self-oriented and other-oriented beliefs associated with right frontopolar cortex (rFPC)
- **effect_size:** - Confidence on send probability: OR = .723, 95% CI [.634, .824] - Social distance on send probability: OR = .558, 95% CI [.485, .641] - Mode of theta_truth on send (Control): OR = 3.22, 95% CI [2.37, 4.38] - Mode of theta_distance on send (Control): OR = 6.53, 95% CI [4.27, 10] - Mode of theta_distance on send (Cue): OR = 4.29, 95% CI [1.66, 11.08] - WTP to receive vs. not receive: Cohen's d = 0.39 - Learning rate decrease Control vs. Cue: eta = .72 vs .67, p = .016

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N=32 (from original 35; 3 excluded; F=16; mean age 24.64 ± 4.02, range 18-35). Receivers: pool of 20 from prior behavioral study.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–35
- **ecological_validity:** Task involves evaluating real/fake news items from French press (2017-2020), which has moderate ecological validity for online information-sharing context. However, the coordination game structure (matching receiver's choice for monetary reward) is artificial compared to natural social media sharing. No real-time social interaction; receivers' data were pre-collected. Non-ego-relevant stimuli deliberately limit generalizability to politically motivated sharing.
- **eligibility_flag:** Dissertation -- only Chapter II (Study 2) qualifies. Chapter I does not involve computational models of social learning over time (individual metacognition/information-seeking only). Chapter III does not involve social learning (testosterone effects on reward anticipation). Note: borderline on whether learning truly occurs "over time" as the Bayesian learning component (theta_learning) contributed minimally to decisions per the authors; however, the model does include dynamic belief updating across trials.
- **concerns:** - Number of free parameters for the winning Bayesian model is not explicitly stated in text - The learning component (theta_learning) had very low weight and was not significant in MLM, raising questions about whether meaningful learning over time actually occurs - Block design (Control always before Cue) confounds condition with order/practice effects - Receivers' choices were pre-collected, not real-time interaction - Supplement is embedded in thesis (no separate supplement file), which is unusual but all content is accessible - Small fMRI sample (N=32) - No parameter recovery or model recovery analyses reported - Some table values do not survive FWE cluster correction at p<.05 (e.g., vmPFC cluster in Table S7 at p(FWE)=.218)
- **limitations_reported:** The models of preference inference that we have tested do not represent the full range of existing models in theory of mind that offer alternative solutions to the inference problem" (from General Conclusion); fixed block order (Control then Cue) is acknowledged implicitly; participants may not have estimated similarity between receivers and themselves but used social distance directly
- **limitations_categorized:** limited model space; fixed condition order (confound); small neuroimaging sample; no real-time social interaction; no parameter recovery; no model recovery
