## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Advice taking from peers across development; how peer advice and own experience influence learning in children, adolescents, and adults.
- **study_focus_short:** Advice taking from peers across development
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from peer advice and own action-outcomes about option values in a reward-based environment.   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (peer advisor)     - Source content (social): action/policy (advice recommending a specific deck)     - Source type (non-social): self     - Source content (non-social): outcome (reward feedback from chosen deck)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (option values / deck expected values)     - Target content (non-social): outcome (expected reward values of card decks)
- **task_description:** Participants played a 4-armed bandit task (210 trials) where they selected card decks to maximize points. Two decks were "good" (higher expected positive values) and two were "bad," all with 50% loss probability. Before the task, participants received advice from a peer recommending one of the good decks.
- **task_paradigm:** Advice-taking task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), advisory (peer advisor, pre-task advice only). Three age groups tested: children (8--10 yrs), adolescents (13--15 yrs), young adults (18--22 yrs).
- **n_players:** single agent (1)
- **partner_type:** unclear
- **stimuli:** Card decks (4 options), monetary/point-based feedback (gains and losses).
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** Behavioural (with computational modeling).
- **main_result:** - Adolescents showed highest initial sensitivity to peer advice (chose advised deck most at beginning). - Adults most consistently followed the advice throughout the task. - Children and adolescents showed higher exploration rates (lower β) than adults. - The prior + bonus dual RL model was the winning model (Bayes factor for best vs. second-best model = 6494, indicating very strong preference). - Adolescents had higher prior expectations (β_p) based on advice than the other two age groups. - Children showed higher learning rates for losses (α_loss) compared to the other two age groups. - Adolescents selected choices with higher expected earnings compared to children and adults. - No age group differences in learning rate for gains (α_gain) or bonus parameter (β_b).

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Prior + Bonus dual RL: Q(t+1) = Q(t) + α[r(t) + g(i)μβ_b − Q(t)], with separate α_pos and α_neg, initial prior = g(i)μβ_p·N, softmax with β (temperature).
- **model_family:** Rescorla-Wagner
- **model_class:** PE learning

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 73 (25 adults, ages 18--22, 13 female; 24 adolescents, ages 13--15, 12 female; 24 children, ages 8--10, 10 female).
- **population_category:** mixed
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Low-moderate. Laboratory-based computer task. The "peer" advisor was described as a previous participant, but the actual social interaction was minimal (single pre-task advice). The card-deck task is abstract and does not reflect real-world social advice-taking contexts. Authors acknowledge the limitation regarding generalization to educational practice.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - The dissertation summarizes three published/under-revision studies; the full statistical details and effect sizes may only be available in the original publications (Rodriguez Buritica, Heekeren, & van den Bos, under revision at time of dissertation). - The supplement file found alongside this paper appears to belong to a different study entirely (a "Mushroom task" with fMRI). This mismatch means the supplement cannot be used to verify Study III details. - No specific effect sizes (d, η², etc.) for age group comparisons on parameter estimates are reported in the dissertation summary. - Mean fitted parameter values not reported in the dissertation text.
- **limitations_reported:** - EEG studies did not allow inferences about neural substrates underlying EL and OL; whether social and individual feedback-processing are linked to similar or two distinct parallel mechanisms is still a matter of research; laboratory setting where children learn indirectly from computer-generated information rather than real social interaction; generalization of findings for educational practice is not given yet; cannot fully distinguish exploration from random behavior in children; future studies are needed using fMRI, DTI, and computational modeling for a more mechanistic view.
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; task simplicity; no neuroimaging (for Study III); limited generalizability to educational settings; inability to distinguish directed vs. random exploration; cross-sectional design (not longitudinal).
