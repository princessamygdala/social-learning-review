## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social reinforcement learning in PTSD; trust learning and social prediction error encoding in assault-related PTSD
- **study_focus_short:** Social reinforcement learning in PTSD
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from social feedback (smiles/frowns from faces) about reward probabilities of social stimuli; and learning from partner reciprocity about trustworthiness in a trust game   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (face stimuli / partner)     - Source content (social): outcome (smile/frown; monetary reciprocity)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (face stimuli / partner)     - Target content (social): state (reward probability / trustworthiness)
- **task_description:** Study 1 (Trust Game): Participants played a 15-trial multi-round trust game as investor with a computerized partner whose reciprocity changed across three phases (high, low, high). Study 2 (fMRI Bandit): Participants completed a social two-arm bandit task (choosing between two faces to receive smiles) and a matched non-social bandit task (choosing between two houses to find one open) during fMRI, with reward probabilities changing across four 25-trial epochs.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 40 (25 PTSD, 15 control) for trust game; N = 29 (15 PTSD, 14 control) for fMRI; adult women aged 20–53
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 20–53
- **ecological_validity:** Limited — trust game used computerized (deceptive) partner, not genuine social interaction; fMRI social task used implied social interaction (choosing faces for smiles) rather than real social exchange; non-social control task well-matched
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** No trauma-exposed control group (controls had no trauma history), so cannot disentangle PTSD vs assault exposure effects; significant psychiatric comorbidity in PTSD group (52% GAD, 44% MDD, 16% alcohol dependence); small fMRI sample (n = 29); 11 participants (10 PTSD) did not return for fMRI scan, introducing potential attrition bias; only one computational model tested (no competing models compared); supplement referenced for model fitting details and fMRI preprocessing but supplement not accessible
- **limitations_reported:** No comparison groups of assaulted women without PTSD, non-assault traumatized women without PTSD, or women with PTSD related to non-assaultive traumas; relatively small sample size limited to women; trust game did not use genuine social interaction with a conspecific; fMRI social task did not involve unambiguous social interaction; used a previously established learning model without testing alternatives; PTSD group had significant comorbidity limiting specificity of inferences
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited control groups; sample size; limited ecological validity; no genuine social interaction; task simplicity; no model comparison; psychiatric comorbidity confounds; limited generalizability (women only)
