## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social feedback learning in borderline personality disorder -- examining how emotional state (neutral vs. negative) and feedback type (social facial expressions vs. nonsocial written text) influence reinforcement learning parameters and accuracy in individuals with varying BPD severity.
- **study_focus_short:** Social feedback learning in borderline personality disorder -- examining how
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from social or nonsocial correct/incorrect feedback about stimulus-reward associations   - Learning from:     - Source type (social or non-social, between-subjects): social condition -- other (stranger face displaying emotion); non-social condition -- world (written text feedback)       - Social condition source: facial expressions (happy = correct, angry = incorrect)       - Non-social condition source: written words ("correct" / "incorrect")     - Source content (non-social): outcome (correct/incorrect feedback on chosen stimulus)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (stimulus-reward contingencies)     - Target content (non-social): stimulus (value/reward probability of Japanese character stimuli)
- **task_description:** Participants completed a forced-choice probabilistic learning task (Frank et al., 2005) after neutral and negative mood inductions. They chose between pairs of Japanese characters with stochastic reward probabilities (80/20%, 70/30%, 60/40%), receiving either social feedback (happy/angry facial expressions) or nonsocial feedback (written "correct"/"incorrect"), then completed a test phase with all possible stimulus pairings and no feedback.
- **task_paradigm:** Probabilistic reward learning
- **players:** Single agent (participant), no interactive partner. Between-subjects assignment to social vs. nonsocial feedback condition. Within-subjects emotion manipulation (neutral then negative).
- **n_players:** single agent (1)
- **partner_type:** human (recorded)
- **stimuli:** Japanese characters (abstract stimuli), facial expressions from NimStim set (happy/angry, matched to participant gender and race) for social condition, written words "correct"/"incorrect" for nonsocial condition.
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - BPD x Emotion interaction on stochasticity (beta): negative emotion increased exploration, amplified by BPD severity (95% of posterior samples for Emotion > 0; 90% of BPD x Emotion samples > 0)   - BPD severity associated with poorer loss-learning accuracy (95% HDI [-0.212, 0.017], 95% probability coefficient < 0)   - BPD x Emotion interaction on loss-learning accuracy: improved loss accuracy post-negative induction for higher BPD (95% HDI [0.018, 0.336], 98% probability coefficient > 0)   - BPD x Emotion x Feedback (social) 3-way interaction on loss-learning accuracy: social feedback under negative emotion further impaired loss-learning for higher BPD (95% HDI [-0.436, 0.009], 97% probability coefficient < 0)   - No effects of any factor on gain learning rates, loss learning rates, gain-learning accuracy, or high-conflict gain-learning accuracy
- **effect_size:** - Stochasticity (beta) -- Emotion effect: Median = 0.24, 95% HDI [-0.029, 0.52], proportion > 0 = 0.957 - Stochasticity (beta) -- BPD x Emotion: Median = 0.071, 95% HDI [-0.029, 0.19], proportion > 0 = 0.898 - Loss-learning accuracy -- BPD: 95% HDI [-0.212, 0.017], 95% probability < 0 - Loss-learning accuracy -- BPD x Emotion: 95% HDI [0.018, 0.336], 98% probability > 0 - Loss-learning accuracy -- BPD x Emotion x Feedback: 95% HDI [-0.436, 0.009], 97% probability < 0 - BPD and suicide risk correlation: r = 0.65, p < .001

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 131 (81.7% female; M_age = 22.90, SD = 6.44); 66 no BPD, 28 subthreshold BPD, 37 BPD diagnosis. N = 21 excluded for not meeting learning threshold (>50% accuracy on A vs. B), leaving N = 110 for analyses.
- **population_category:** clinical
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Limited ecological validity. Social feedback was static facial expression images (NimStim), not live social interaction. Mood induction used personally-relevant social narratives but all learning occurred in a laboratory computerized task. The "social" condition involves faces as feedback stimuli rather than genuine social interaction. Fixed order of mood inductions (neutral always first) limits causal claims about emotion effects.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Only one computational model tested (Q-learning) -- no model comparison with alternative cognitive models - Social manipulation is between-subjects, reducing power to detect social-specific effects - The "social" feedback is static facial images, not interactive social feedback -- this is a weak social manipulation - Fixed order of emotion inductions (neutral always first) confounds emotion with practice/fatigue effects - N = 21 participants excluded for not meeting learning threshold, reducing effective sample - Data overlap: This study uses an overlapping sample with Dixon-Gordon et al. (2022), which examined suicide risk using the same data but different (less sophisticated) analytic methods; potential duplicate data concern - No parameter recovery or model recovery reported - No simulation of the model prior to fitting - BPD examined dimensionally (symptom count), not categorically -- limits comparison with prior categorical BPD studies  [HIGH]
- **limitations_reported:** Our sample was relatively homogeneous regarding race and ethnicity, limiting the generalizability of these results"; "given the high rates of co-occurrence of BPD with other psychological disorders, some of the findings associated with BPD may be due to co-occurring conditions"; "we examined BPD continuously... but in so doing we did not have a healthy control comparison per se, potentially reducing our ability to detect group differences"; "given that our neutral and stressful mood inductions involved a personally-relevant social interaction, it is possible that all participants, regardless of instruction condition, may have been primed to think about social feedback to some degree"; "we used a learning task with relatively few trials to reduce participant burden and careless responding, but it may also reduce the reliability of the learning parameter estimates and power in our study"; "although we intentionally used a fixed order of mood inductions to reduce carry over effects, it is still possible that we may see a fatigue or practice effect on the second administration of the learning task"; "there are other theories of dynamic decision-making processes... It is possible that different participants may rely on different processes of decision-making"
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited generalizability (sample homogeneity); comorbidity confounds; no healthy control group (dimensional approach); social priming confound; task simplicity (few trials); order effects (fixed induction order); model specificity (single model tested)
