## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social competitive behavior in depression; dissociating social valuation (dominance preference) from social preference (guilt/inequity aversion) as causes of altered competitiveness.
- **study_focus_short:** Social competitive behavior in depression
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from opponent's bidding behavior during repeated social contests about opponent's likely future actions to guide competitive decisions   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (opponent/computer partner playing as human)       - Content (social): action/policy (opponent's bid amounts)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (opponent)       - Content (social): action/policy (opponent's future bidding behavior)
- **task_description:** Participants played a 30-round resource contest game with an adaptive computer partner (believed to be human). In each round, the "alpha" player distributed $10 (keeping $6-$10, transferring $0-$4 to "beta"), and beta could challenge alpha for their position by bidding; both players privately bid amounts and whoever bid more won the alpha position.
- **task_paradigm:** Auction task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), dyadic (adaptive computer partner believed to be human)
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Monetary endowments, bid amounts, role positions (alpha/beta indicated by crown)
- **method:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Depressed participants made more high-value transfers vs. controls (z=2.04, p=.04) - Group parameter for guilt significantly >0 (95% HDI >0), indicating higher guilt in depression - D (dominance preference) did not differ between groups - Guilt positively correlated with MASQ-GD symptom severity in depression (r=0.26, p=.04) - Change in guilt T1-T2 correlated with change in symptoms (r=0.29, p=.04) - VS win vs. loss response: comparable between groups (t=0.85, p=.40) - Higher guilt associated with greater VMPFC activation during transfer (r=0.26, p=.01) - Higher guilt associated with greater ACC activation during challenge (r=0.26, p=.006) - Higher guilt associated with greater right AI activation during challenge (r=0.29, p=.002)
