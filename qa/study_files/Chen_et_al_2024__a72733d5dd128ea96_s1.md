## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Adaptive teaching and learning from feedback; reasoning about others' knowledge states (mentalizing learning)
- **study_focus_short:** Adaptive teaching and learning from feedback
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Teachers learn about learners' background knowledge from learner feedback and adjust teaching; learners reason about what teachers know about them and decide whether to provide feedback.   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (learner/teacher partner)     - Source content (social): action/policy (examples selected by teacher; feedback/estimates from learner)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (learner/teacher partner)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; background knowledge / beliefs)
- **task_description:** Participants interact with simulated agents in a proportion estimation task. In Experiment 1, participants (teachers/tour guides) select sets of colored turtles to teach a simulated learner the true island proportion, receiving optional learner feedback between two teaching rounds. In Experiment 2, participants (learners/tourists) receive turtle examples from a simulated teacher and decide whether to send costly feedback to help the teacher adapt.
- **task_paradigm:** Estimation / dot task
- **players:** - Experiment 1: Single agent (participant as teacher), dyadic (simulated learner partner) - Experiment 2: Single agent (participant as learner), dyadic (simulated teacher partner)
- **n_players:** dyadic (2)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Colored turtles (orange and purple), proportion estimates, binary feedback
- **method:** online / behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural / online
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Experiment 1: Teachers sensitive to learner priors — interaction effect of learner prior x knowledge condition on proportion of majority-color turtles sent (b = -0.27, z = -8.05, p < .001)   - Experiment 1: Teachers adjust to feedback — interaction of lesson number x feedback condition in "too high" condition (b = 0.04, z = 1.98) and "too low" condition (b = -0.11, z = -4.38)   - Experiment 2: Learners strategically send feedback — probability of sending guess decreases as teacher examples match learner prior (linear: b = -35.83, z = -5.18; quadratic: b = -14.33, z = -3.00)   - Experiment 2: Learners more accurate after second lesson (65.8% vs 49.7%; b = 0.35, z = 6.65)   - Experiment 2: Learners who provided feedback performed better (b = 0.71, z = 5.89)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 312 total (Experiment 1: N = 193 adults from MTurk, US; Experiment 2: N = 119 adults from MTurk, US)

- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Low ecological validity — participants interact with simulated (model-generated) agents rather than real humans; the proportion estimation task (colored turtles) is highly simplified relative to real-world teaching; interaction limited to two rounds of example selection and optional feedback; concepts are simple proportions rather than structured knowledge domains.
- **eligibility_flag:** MEDIUM confidence — borderline on temporal learning criterion (only 2 rounds); borderline on whether this constitutes "learning" vs. "communication/inference
- **concerns:** - No formal quantitative model comparison (no BIC, AIC, or Bayes factor comparing hierarchical vs. nonhierarchical models) - Model parameters were fixed a priori (not fitted to individual participant data); no individual-level parameter estimation - No parameter recovery or model recovery analyses - Participants interacted with simulated agents, not real humans — limits social validity - Only unstandardized regression coefficients reported; no standardized effect sizes - The "learning" in this paradigm is limited to two rounds of belief updating; does not demonstrate extended learning over time - Supplement referenced in text ("Supplementary Appendix") but no supplement file found
- **limitations_reported:** The proportion estimation task uses a simplified 2-component mixture that does not capture the complexity of real-world learner populations or concepts; real-world interactions are longer and richer than a single exchange of examples and feedback; the framework models knowledge states only as beliefs about the correct concept and immediate prior experience, not richer forms of background knowledge; participants showed evidence of heuristic strategies rather than exact Bayesian marginalization; doing exact Bayesian inference may be intractable for more complex concepts or multiple rounds of interaction; teachers' and students' goals may not be aligned in practice; socioeconomic status, gender, and race affect teacher-student interactions but are not modeled
- **limitations_categorized:** task simplicity; limited ecological validity; limited generalizability; computational tractability; goal alignment assumptions; demographic factors not modeled; heuristic deviations from rational model
