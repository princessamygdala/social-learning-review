## Quality
- **sample_size:** N=1,360 total (520 in decentralized networks across 13 trials, 520 in centralized networks across 13 trials, 320 in control groups across 8 groups); recruited from Amazon Mechanical Turk (World Wide Web).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate — uses a web-based platform with real monetary incentives for accuracy, which adds some ecological validity, but the social interaction is limited to observing a single average number from network neighbors (no face-to-face communication, no identity information, no discussion). Estimation tasks are stylized lab tasks, not naturalistic social decisions.
- **eligibility_flag:** The paper uses a computational model (DeGroot) and human behavioral data, and learning occurs over multiple rounds in a social context. However, the model parameters (self-weights) are not formally fitted via optimization — they are analytically derived from the DeGroot framework and empirically estimated from revision patterns. The "learning" is over 3 rounds only, which is minimal but qualifies as learning over time. FLAG: Borderline — model is more of a theoretical/analytical framework than a fitted computational model. The self-weight parameters are estimated from behavior but not via standard model fitting (MLE/Bayesian). The paper is better characterized as testing theoretical predictions of the DeGroot model rather than fitting a computational model to data.
- **concerns:** - The DeGroot model is used as a theoretical framework generating predictions, not as a model formally fitted to individual-level data. Self-weights are inferred from observed revision magnitudes rather than estimated via optimization. - Only 3 rounds of social influence — very limited temporal dynamics. - No formal model comparison (no alternative models tested with quantitative fit metrics). - Supplement (SI Appendix) referenced extensively throughout but not accessible here — simulation analyses, additional robustness checks, and network structure details are in SI Appendix only. - The "revision coefficient" (partial correlation between accuracy and revision magnitude) is the key empirical measure but is not a model parameter in the traditional sense.
- **limitations_reported:** Authors acknowledge that previous small-group studies (e.g., Lorenz et al., 2011) found social influence undermines wisdom of crowds, and show this was due to insufficient statistical power with small groups; effects of social influence on collective accuracy are significantly strengthened with larger population sizes; centralized networks can bias collective estimates toward beliefs of central individuals; results depend on the correlation between accuracy and self-weight, which may not hold in all contexts.
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; task simplicity; limited temporal dynamics (3 rounds only); no formal model fitting; no parameter recovery; centralization as confound; generalizability concerns (correlation between accuracy and self-weight may be context-dependent)
