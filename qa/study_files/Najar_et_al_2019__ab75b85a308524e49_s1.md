- **winning_model:** Value Shaping with symmetric update, VS2: Q(d) ← Q(d) + α_i × [1 − Q(d)]; Q(d̄) ← Q(d̄) + α_i × [−1 − Q(d̄)]. Private learning: Q(c) ← Q(c) + α_p × [r − Q(c)]. Action selection: softmax with β and κ. (4 params: α_p, α_i, β, κ)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** Exp.1: N = 24 (10 male, 14 female; mean age 24.5 ± 3.45). Exp.2: N = 44 (SD group: N = 22, 11 male/11 female, mean age 26.4 ± 3.9; UD group: N = 22, 9 male/13 female, mean age 24.9 ± 3.5). Total N = 68.
- **population_category:** healthy adults

- **ecological_validity:** Lab-based probabilistic learning task with abstract stimuli; demonstrator choices were computer-controlled (not real partner choices despite cover story); single-step binary choice with no real social interaction; limited ecological validity for real-world imitation.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) Preprint, not peer-reviewed at time of writing. (2) Demonstrator choices were computer-generated, not from a real social partner — participants were deceived about this. (3) Task is single-step, so value shaping, reward shaping, and policy shaping are mathematically equivalent and cannot be distinguished. (4) Mean fitted parameter values are not numerically reported — only shown in figure distributions. (5) No supplement was found to verify additional details.
- **limitations_reported:** Further research using multi-step reinforcement learning paradigms is needed to assess which of these three shaping methods best accounts for human behaviour"; "Whether or not the difference between private and imitation learning rates changes as a function of task related factors (e.g., task difficulty) or inter-individual differences (e.g., age of the participants, pathological states) remains to be established"; single-step task means value shaping, reward shaping, and policy shaping cannot be distinguished; effect of demonstrator skill on performance did not reach significance in Exp.1.
- **limitations_categorized:** task simplicity; limited generalizability; indistinguishable model variants in single-step design; small effect size in within-subject manipulation; limited ecological validity
