## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social influence learning; how observing group members' choices and outcomes during probabilistic reversal learning alters choice, confidence, and value updating via direct and observational learning.
- **study_focus_short:** Social influence learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from own reward outcomes and others' cumulative reward history about stimulus-reward contingencies in a group setting   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self       - Source content (non-social): outcome (reward/punishment from chosen option)     - Source type (social): group (4 co-players)       - Source content (social): action/policy (others' choices) and outcome (others' cumulative reward history over last 3 trials)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (stimulus-reward contingencies)       - Target content (non-social): stimulus (which abstract fractal is the more rewarding option)
- **task_description:** In a multi-player probabilistic reversal learning task, groups of 5 participants made initial choices between two abstract fractals and placed confidence bets, then observed their co-players' choices, could adjust their choice and bet, and received outcome feedback; reward contingencies reversed every 8-12 trials.
- **task_paradigm:** Reversal learning
- **players:** Multi-agent (group of 5, real participants), symmetric; 1 fMRI participant + 4 behavioral participants per group.
- **n_players:** small group (3-4)
- **partner_type:** human (live)
- **stimuli:** Abstract fractals (2 options), portrait photos of co-players, binary reward feedback (win/loss of 20 cents multiplied by bet), confidence bets (1, 2, or 3).
- **method:** fMRI / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI + behavioural
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Social influence on choice: direction x coherence interaction on choice switch probability (F(1,574) = 55.82, p < 0.001)   - Direction main effect on choice switch probability (F(1,228) = 299.63, p < 0.001)   - Coherence main effect on choice switch probability (F(2,574) = 131.48, p < 0.001)   - Social influence on bet: direction main effect on bet difference (F(1,734) = 50.95, p < 0.001)   - Bet difference direction x coherence interaction (F(1,734) = 4.67, p < 0.05)   - 2nd choice accuracy higher than 1st choice accuracy (F(1,1288) = 15.68, p < 0.001) -- social information facilitated learning   - Model comparison: M6b (cumulative reward history OL + instantaneous social influence + bet) decisively wins (BMA probability = 99.8%)   - V_self in vmPFC (TFCE SVC, p < 0.05 FWE): double dissociation with V_other   - V_other in ACCg (TFCE SVC, p < 0.05 FWE): double dissociation with V_self   - RPE in bilateral VS/NAcc (TFCE whole-brain, p < 0.05 FWE)   - Dissenting social info (w.N_against) scales parametrically with rTPJ, ACC/pMFC, bilateral aINS, FPC (all TFCE p < 0.05 FWE)   - Switch > Stay: bilateral dlPFC, ACC; Stay > Switch: vmPFC (all TFCE p < 0.05 FWE)   - PPI: rTPJ-putamen connectivity modulated by switch/stay; putamen encodes social prediction error   - PhiPI: vmPFC-ACCg interaction in mPFC; rTPJ-dlPFC interaction in vmPFC and pMFC   - Correlation beta(w.N_against) with model-free switch slope (R = 0.64, p < 0.001)   - Correlation beta(w.N_with) with model-free bet slope (R = 0.33, p < 0.001)

- **ecological_validity:** High for social influence studies: real-time interaction with real human co-players (no deception), multi-player group setting (5 players), actual monetary incentives. Uses abstract fractals (low ecological validity of stimuli) in a laboratory PRL task. Cover story framed as multi-player decision game.
- **eligibility_flag:** Dissertation; both Study 1 and Study 2 qualify. Study 2 flagged: social agent is a computer algorithm (replayed human choices), not a genuine social partner. No double-counting risk as datasets are independent (different participants).  ---  # STUDY 1 (Chapters 2-4): Social Influence in Goal-Directed Learning (Behavioral + fMRI)  ## COMPUTATIONAL LEVEL
- **concerns:** (1) Preference weights for co-players were fixed (0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0.25), not estimated as free parameters -- may not capture individual variability in credibility assignment. (2) Exclusion of 20 non-responders (those who switched 0 or 1 times) may bias toward conforming individuals. (3) V_other orthogonalized with respect to V_self, which assigns maximum variance to V_self -- this ordering choice could affect ACCg results. (4) Small fMRI sample (N=39) after exclusion. (5) SVC used for key value contrasts (vmPFC, ACCg) rather than whole-brain correction.
- **limitations_reported:** Authors note: limited ability to dissociate normative vs informational influence; the value difference and 1st bet create a dynamic competing process that is not fully resolved; the PRL task structure may not generalize to all social learning contexts; univariate fMRI analysis may be suboptimal (MVPA suggested as future direction); no causal evidence for rTPJ role (TMS/DCM suggested as future work); payoff structure independent of group coherence (no cooperation/competition tested).
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; limited generalizability; no causal manipulation; task simplicity; limited analysis methods (univariate only)
