## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 32 (16 female, 16 male); mean age 25.1 +/- 4 years, range 19-34; all from University of Hamburg student population
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 19–34
- **ecological_validity:** Uses face stimuli (ecological) but in a probabilistic reward learning paradigm with stable contingencies (lab-constrained). Opposite-gender faces as inherent value cues adds social relevance but the task is artificial (binary choice, monetary payoff). The concept of value congruence has real-world parallels (advertising, investing) but the lab implementation is simplified.
- **eligibility_flag:** MEDIUM confidence — borderline social learning; faces serve as cues with inherent social value but learning is about non-social reward contingencies
- **concerns:** - The social component is limited: faces serve as conditioned stimuli with inherent value, but participants are not learning anything social (e.g., about the person's traits, intentions, or social attributes) - Gender asymmetry in behavioral effects (men showed significant attractiveness effect, women did not) but no significant gender x attractiveness interaction - Small volume corrections based on prior coordinates (8mm spheres) for several key contrasts - Attractiveness contrast reported at p < 0.001 uncorrected (not FWE corrected) - No independent validation dataset - Supplement not available (no supplement found in papers folder)
- **limitations_reported:** Future research is needed to explore the effect of value congruence with other cues that are not so prone to elicit a gender-specific response"; "further research should widen the scope of value congruence beyond stable reward contingencies and explore the effects of changes in cue-outcome associations on dynamic LRs
- **limitations_categorized:** limited generalizability (gender-specific cues); task simplicity (stable contingencies only); limited ecological validity
