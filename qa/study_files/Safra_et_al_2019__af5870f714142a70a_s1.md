## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 100 total (Discovery sample: N = 50; Replication sample: N = 50). Mean age ~33 years; Discovery 28% women, Replication 42% women. Online participants via Amazon Mechanical Turk.
- **population_category:** clinical
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Online task (MTurk) provides more diverse population than lab-based studies; however, social interaction is with a simulated virtual demonstrator (not real person), binary reward structure is artificial, and the social context is minimal (observing avatar choices/outcomes rather than real social interaction). Moderate ecological validity.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Virtual demonstrator behavior was generated by a Q-learning algorithm, not a real person — participants were deceived about social partner being real - No clinical diagnosis; only self-reported HAD scores in general population - No control for medication status - Supplement files (S1-S10) not accessible for verification of additional model comparisons and parameter recovery details - The paper mentions additional models tested in supplementary analyses (S8-S9 Fig) but exact model specifications are not in the main text
- **limitations_reported:** Did not control for participants' actual diagnosis and treatment, which may be problematic since medication interacts with decision-making in depression; results would benefit from replication in carefully characterized clinical populations while controlling for medication status and medical history; only tested ability to detect depressive symptoms via self-rated scale (HAD), not clinically diagnosed depression; longitudinal designs needed to assess test-retest reliability and whether measures reflect states or traits
- **limitations_categorized:** No clinical diagnosis (self-report only); no medication control; limited generalizability (general population, not clinical); no longitudinal data; limited ecological validity (virtual demonstrator, not real social partner)
