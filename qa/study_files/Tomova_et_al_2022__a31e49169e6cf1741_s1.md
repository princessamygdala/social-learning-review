## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social isolation effects on reward seeking and reward learning; how isolation modulates learning from social vs. non-social feedback in adolescents.
- **study_focus_short:** Social isolation effects on reward seeking and reward learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from probabilistic social and non-social feedback about stimulus-reward contingencies under isolation vs. baseline   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (averaged face stimuli — smiling/neutral)     - Source type (non-social): world (abstract symbols +/0)     - Source content (social): outcome (positive/neutral facial feedback)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (symbolic win/no-win feedback)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (slot machine reward contingencies)     - Target content (non-social): stimulus (which slot machine is more rewarding)
- **task_description:** Participants completed a probabilistic reversal learning task in which they chose between two slot machines, receiving either social feedback (smiling/neutral faces) or non-social feedback (+/0 symbols), with contingencies reversing every 7 trials. Separately, they completed an effort-based decision-making task where they chose whether to exert effort for high/low rewards in social/non-social contexts.
- **task_paradigm:** Reversal learning
- **players:** Single agent (participant), no social partner (feedback from pre-programmed stimuli).
- **n_players:** single agent (1)
- **partner_type:** none
- **stimuli:** Slot machines with probabilistic outcomes; social feedback (averaged smiling/neutral faces); non-social feedback (plus/zero symbols); effort task with social pictures (people hugging/laughing) vs. nature pictures.
- **method:** fMRI / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI (baseline MID task only) + behavioural (reward learning and effort tasks across 3 sessions)
- **main_result:** - Isolation increased loneliness (iso total vs. start: d = 1.06; iso media vs. start: d = 0.68) - Isolation increased reward seeking: faster RTs for high rewards in iso total vs. baseline (d = 0.60-0.82) - Isolation increased learning from positive PEs, especially from social feedback (social: d = 12.38; non-social: d = 1.11) - Virtual interactions partially remediated effects but decreased social learning (iso media social learning_pos vs. baseline: d = 2.36 decrease) - Univariate neural reward sensitivity in OC predicted reward seeking after isolation (r = 0.53) - Higher chronic loneliness associated with stronger isolation effects on reward seeking in social contexts (b = -0.07)
- **effect_size:** MEDIUM confidence — implausibly large, may reflect hierarchical Bayesian estimation properties rather than meaningful behavioral difference
- **learning_from:** World; probabilistic feedback from slot machines delivered via social cues (faces) or non-social cues (symbols).
- **learning_about:** World; stimulus-reward contingencies (which slot machine pays more).  ---  ### ALGORITHMIC LEVEL

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 40 (42 recruited, 2 dropped; ages 16-19; within-subjects design with 3 sessions: baseline, iso total, iso media)
- **population_category:** adolescents
- **population_age_range:** 16–19
- **ecological_validity:** Experimental isolation paradigm (up to 4 hours) provides causal inference but is short-term and artificial. Participants knew isolation would end. Virtual interaction condition adds ecological relevance. Social feedback in RL task uses averaged faces rather than real social partners.
