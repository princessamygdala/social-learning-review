- **population_category:** mixed

- **ecological_validity:** Lab-based (children) and online (adults) using cartoon/video scenarios of teaching; third-party observer paradigm (participants not themselves the learner L0); limited ecological validity as a proxy for real teaching evaluation
- **eligibility_flag:** Borderline eligibility — computational models are normative (ideal observer Bayesian models fit at group level to condition means), not process models of trial-by-trial learning. Learning is primarily one-shot inference/evaluation from a single demonstration, not iterative learning over time. Publication type: thesis (flag which studies qualify). Informant evaluation (teacher quality ratings) may be better characterized as social judgment/inference rather than social learning per se.
- **concerns:** (1) Only one model tested per study -- no model comparison. (2) Models fit to condition means (5-15 conditions) with 2 free parameters, so perfect or near-perfect fits (R²=1.00 with 5 conditions, 2 parameters) are not strongly diagnostic. (3) High exclusion rates, especially for children (e.g., 44% in Exp 4). (4) Failed replication in Exp 4 raises concerns about robustness of preschooler findings. (5) Dissertation with multiple experiments; some experiments are exploratory. (6) Borderline whether this constitutes "learning over time" vs one-shot social inference.
- **limitations_reported:** Authors' own: Failure to replicate preschooler findings (Exp 4) raises possibility initial results were a false positive or inflated effect sizes; high attrition rates in child samples; within-subjects design may have been too demanding for preschoolers; value manipulation used different operationalizations across experiments (informational value in Exp 1 vs perceptual "coolness" in Exps 2-5); L0 learns deterministically in model (unrealistic); model assumes learner and teacher have identical utility functions; model does not capture repeated demonstrations; participants were third-party observers rather than learners themselves; unknown whether children detect these violations in naturalistic settings
- **limitations_categorized:** limited generalizability; failed replication; high attrition; task simplicity; limited ecological validity; sample size; inconsistent manipulation across experiments; model assumptions

- **other_notes:** This is a doctoral dissertation with three empirical chapters. Each chapter contains a computational model (Bayesian for Chapters 2-3; causal graphical for Chapter 4) and multiple behavioral experiments with both adults and children. The dissertation extends the Shafto & Goodman (2008) pedagogical model to cases where default assumptions are violated (teacher omits information, has a particular teaching style, or misrepresents the learner's knowledge). The models are computational-level accounts (Marr Level 1) providing normative predictions for how learners should reason, not algorithmic-level process models. The child behavioral results were mixed across chapters (some null, some failed replications), while adult results were generally consistent with model predictions. No supplement file was found, but the dissertation includes appendices A-D with additional model and method details.
