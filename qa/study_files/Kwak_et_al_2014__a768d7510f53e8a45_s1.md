## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 102 (44 men, 59 women; age range 18-36, M = 23, SD = 4); 1 excluded from original 103 for choosing same deck on all trials. Subgroups: Learning All (N=36), Learning Self (N=12), Learning Charity (N=7), Anti-Learning (N=11), Non-Learning (N=36).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–36
- **ecological_validity:** Modified IGT with charitable giving adds social dimension but remains a lab-based card task with abstract monetary outcomes; charity is a real foundation (Make-A-Wish) with real donations, enhancing ecological validity somewhat. However, learning about a charity is not the same as learning about a specific individual's welfare. Limited social interaction -- no direct social partner.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - The "social" component is charitable donation rather than interaction with another person, which is a weak form of social context - Non-learners (N=36, ~35% of sample) excluded from individual difference analyses, reducing effective sample - Only correlational evidence linking model parameters to self-reported altruism; causal direction unclear - No cross-validation or out-of-sample model validation reported - Charity preference vs. learning confound acknowledged by authors
- **limitations_reported:** Results only show association between altruistic tendencies and pro-social reward learning, cannot determine causal direction; correlation may be driven by mediating factors such as desire to appear altruistic in self-report; choices on decks may reflect preference rather than learning per se; some participants may have had accurate knowledge about deck contingencies but chose not to adjust behavior based on that information.
