## Quality
- **sample_size:** N=19 simulated agents (9 prisoners + 10 guards) based on archival data from 24 original Stanford Prison Experiment participants; 30 simulation runs per condition (3 conditions); 693 time steps per run
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Uses archival data from a classic social psychology experiment with real participants; personality profiles derived from actual pre-experiment inventories; but the simulation itself is agent-based, not direct human behavioral data. The Stanford Prison Experiment had notable ecological features (role-playing in a simulated prison) but also well-known ethical and methodological concerns.
- **eligibility_flag:** This paper uses computational modeling validated against archival behavioral data, but does NOT collect new human behavioral data. The "participants" are simulated cognitive agents, not human subjects generating new data. The learning occurs through agent-based simulation rather than direct observation of human learning. FLAG: No new human behavioral data — agent-based simulation only. Additionally, this is an agent-based cognitive architecture (PMFServ) rather than a computational model in the standard sense used in computational psychiatry/neuroscience (no parameter fitting to individual human choice data in the typical sense). The paper may not meet inclusion criterion "Uses human behavioral data" — the behavioral data is archival and used for validation rather than model fitting.
- **concerns:** - No new human behavioral data collected — entirely agent-based simulation validated against archival data - Hand-calibrated activations (not formal parameter fitting) - Attentional cue weights described as "best guess" estimates — not empirically derived - No formal model comparison (conditions differ in initial knowledge, not model structure) - Motivated attention exponent (0.25) calibrated ad hoc from the Stanford Prison scenario itself - Linear independence of attention cues assumed without empirical basis - The Stanford Prison Experiment itself has been heavily criticized methodologically (demand characteristics, small N, lack of control) - Action frequency validation showed poor match for Resistance (0.29–0.30 simulated vs 0.09 ground truth) - No parameter recovery or model recovery performed - No code or data shared
- **limitations_reported:** Attentional cue weights are not reliable estimates of relative importance of factors; no empirical minima or maxima for cues; interaction between cognitive effects not studied experimentally; conformity model adapted from confederate paradigm to natural environment; calibration based on sparse data requiring hand-tuning; motivated attention exponent calibrated from a single scenario with no standard units; linear combination of cues assumed due to lack of interaction data; production mechanisms not modeled in detail; retention model kept deliberately simple
- **limitations_categorized:** ad hoc parameter calibration; untested cue interaction assumptions; limited ecological validity of validation data; hand-tuning; no formal model comparison; no parameter recovery; sparse calibration data; model complexity (KIDS vs KISS tension)
