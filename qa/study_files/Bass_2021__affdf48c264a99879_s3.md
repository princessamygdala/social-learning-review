## Quality
- **sample_size:** Exp 1: N=60 adults (20 per condition; M age=22.16; 7 excluded); Exp 2: N=60 children ages 6-8 (20 per condition; M age=88.1mo; 3 excluded)
- **population_category:** mixed
- **population_age_range:** 6–8
- **ecological_validity:** Online via Zoom with pre-recorded video teacher; clever deception paradigm (teacher cannot hear participant due to "technical glitch"); but artificial setup with rigged matching game performance and point-based reward system. More ecologically valid than cartoon paradigms in that participants are themselves the learner.
- **eligibility_flag:** Borderline — meets criteria for computational modeling and human behavioral data in a social learning context, but learning is primarily one-shot social inference/evaluation rather than iterative learning over time. Models are normative Bayesian ideal observer models, not process models of trial-by-trial learning dynamics. Publication type: thesis. Flag all three chapters as borderline for "learning over time" criterion.
- **concerns:** (1) Model is qualitative only -- no quantitative specification or parameter fitting. (2) Strong first-choice bias for "just right" game across all conditions. (3) Difficulty score is a derived measure from rankings; first-choice results alone are less clear. (4) Small samples (N=20 per condition). (5) Adult regression analysis had reduced N=38 due to missing SES data. (6) Online testing via Zoom may introduce variability.
- **limitations_reported:** Authors' own: Model not yet mathematically specified with underlying distributions; unknown whether children interpreted teacher's judgment as task-specific or general competence evaluation; "just right" bias may reflect rational trust in teacher's recommendation; age effects in Underestimate condition could reflect developing Theory of Mind or confidence; critical next step is to quantitatively specify the causal model; future work should test role of false belief reasoning directly
- **limitations_categorized:** model underspecification; sample size; limited ecological validity; task simplicity; no parameter recovery; limited generalizability

- **other_notes:** This is a doctoral dissertation with three empirical chapters. Each chapter contains a computational model (Bayesian for Chapters 2-3; causal graphical for Chapter 4) and multiple behavioral experiments with both adults and children. The dissertation extends the Shafto & Goodman (2008) pedagogical model to cases where default assumptions are violated (teacher omits information, has a particular teaching style, or misrepresents the learner's knowledge). The models are computational-level accounts (Marr Level 1) providing normative predictions for how learners should reason, not algorithmic-level process models. The child behavioral results were mixed across chapters (some null, some failed replications), while adult results were generally consistent with model predictions. No supplement file was found, but the dissertation includes appendices A-D with additional model and method details.
