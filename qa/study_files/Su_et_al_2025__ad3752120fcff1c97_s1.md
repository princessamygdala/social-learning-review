## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Fehr-Schmidt inequality aversion utility model with either RW or Bayesian Observer norm learning (best-fitting model selected per subject per condition). Shared parameters: α (unfairness sensitivity, [0,20]), τ (inverse temperature, [0,10]). RW adds ε (learning rate, [0,1]); BO uses Bayesian updating of μ and σ².  Formula: U(x_t) = x_t − α·max{f_t − x_t, 0}; P(accept) = exp(τ·U(x_t)) / (1 + exp(τ·U(x_t)))  RW norm update: f_t = f_{t-1} + ε(x_t − f_{t-1}) BO norm update: Bayesian updating of μ_t and σ²_t
- **model_family:** Bayesian
- **model_class:** PE learning / Belief updating
- **all_models_tested:** - M0 (Null model): U(x_t) = x_t; n_params = 1 (τ only); family: utility; metric: LOOIC - Fehr-Schmidt + RW: U(x_t) = x_t − α·max{f_t − x_t, 0} with RW updating; n_params = 3 (α, τ, ε); family: RW; metric: LOOIC - Fehr-Schmidt + BO: U(x_t) = x_t − α·max{f_t − x_t, 0} with Bayesian observer updating; n_params = 2 (α, τ; BO uses fixed priors); family: Bayesian; metric: LOOIC

Limited ecological validity -- real social hierarchies are more complex than binary superior/inferior.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Best-fitting model selected per subject per condition (RW or BO), then shared parameters pooled for ANOVA -- this mixed-model approach could introduce confounds. - **flagged_fields:** - `model_family`: MEDIUM confidence -- the paper does not name a single "winning model"; instead the best-fitting model (RW or BO) is selected per subject per condition, and shared parameters are pooled. This is an unusual approach. - `coordinates_peak`: N/A -- EEG study with no source localization - `preregistered`: LOW -- not mentioned anywhere in the paper - `model_comparison`: MEDIUM -- LOOIC values are reported in supplementary figures (S2, S3) but specific numeric values are in figure form only, not tabulated
- **cannot_find:** - Exact LOOIC values (presented only in supplementary figures, not extractable from text) - Formal model recovery or parameter recovery - Posterior predictive checks - Code/data repository (data available "on request" only) - Preregistration status
- **other_notes:** The paper uses a within-subject design comparing competitive and cooperative contexts. The key computational contribution is showing that the Fehr-Schmidt unfairness sensitivity parameter (α) is modulated by social rank and, in Experiment 2, by the interaction of social rank and social context. The norm-learning component (RW or BO) captures how fairness expectations update over sequential offers, while the utility function captures sensitivity to deviations from those expectations. The "learning" in this paper is fairness norm updating -- whether this constitutes "learning over time" in the social context is borderline but defensible, since the RW/BO models explicitly capture trial-by-trial norm adaptation from offers made by socially ranked others.
