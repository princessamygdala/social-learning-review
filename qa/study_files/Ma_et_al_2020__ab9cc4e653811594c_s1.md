## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Trust learning — age-related changes in belief updating about peer trustworthiness during adolescence, driven by prior beliefs, prior uncertainty, and uncertainty tolerance.
- **study_focus_short:** Trust learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from sequential sampling of a trustee's reciprocation history about the trustee's trustworthiness   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (trustee)     - Source content (social): outcomes (reciprocation/defection history)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (trustee)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; trustworthiness/trait)
- **task_description:** Participants played the investor role in an Information Sampling Trust Game with 60 different computer-generated trustees. On each trial, they could sequentially sample tiles (up to 25) from a grid revealing the trustee's past reciprocation history (green = reciprocated, red = defected), then decided whether to invest (trust) or keep their endowment.
- **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (60 different trustees, computer-generated)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** 5x5 grid of coloured tiles (green/red), payoff matrix, binary investment decision
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural
- **main_result:** - Prior beliefs shifted toward expecting greater trustworthiness with age (β = 0.212, p = 0.009) - Prior uncertainty increased from early to mid-adolescence (age linear β = 0.027, p < 0.001; age quadratic β = 0.020, p = 0.005) - Uncertainty tolerance increased linearly with age (β = 0.012, p < 0.001) - Decision noise did not change with age (β = 0.11, p = 0.600) - Sampling sensitivity to information consistency interacted with age (linear β = 0.174, p < 0.001; quadratic β = -0.036, p = 0.002) - Investment decisions tracked reciprocation probability more strongly with age (age linear β = 2.123, p < 0.001; age quadratic β = -0.906, p < 0.001)
- **effect_size:** - Sampling model: Marginal R² = 0.137, Conditional R² = 0.268 - Investment model: Marginal R² = 0.753, Conditional R² = 0.797 - Age-expected reward correlation: r_s = 0.387, p < 0.001 - Model comparison (Uncertainty vs Sample Cost): summed ΔBIC = -1685, 95% CI [-3086, -527] - Model comparison (Uncertainty vs Threshold): summed ΔBIC = -1741, 95% CI [-3072, -636]

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Bayesian Uncertainty model (Beta-Binomial prior updating with uncertainty tolerance criterion; 4 params: α₀, β₀, k, τ)  Formula: p(r | n₊, n₋) = Beta(r; α, β) where α = α₀ + n₊, β = β₀ + n₋. Uncertainty = √(αβ / ((α+β)²(α+β+1))). Sampling probability: p(sample | α, β) = 1 / (1 + e^((Uncertainty(α,β) − k) / τ))
- **model_family:** Bayesian
- **model_class:** Belief updating
- **all_models_tested:** - {"name": "Uncertainty model (with priors)", "family": "Bayesian belief updating", "n_params": 4, "metric": "BIC"} - {"name": "Uncertainty model (basic, uniform prior)", "family": "Bayesian belief updating", "n_params": 2, "metric": "BIC"} - {"name": "Sample Cost model (with risk attitude + priors)", "family": "Bayesian expected utility / dynamic programming", "n_params": 6, "metric": "BIC"} - {"name": "Sample Cost model (with risk attitude)", "family": "Bayesian expected utility / dynamic programming", "n_params": 4, "metric": "BIC"} - {"name": "Sample Cost model (basic)", "family": "Bayesian expected utility / dynamic programming", "n_params": 3, "metric": "BIC"} - {"name": "Threshold model (asymmetric bounds)", "family": "Heuristic / threshold", "n_params": 3, "metric": "BIC"} - {"name": "Threshold model (basic)", "family": "Heuristic / threshold", "n_params": 1, "metric": "BIC"} - {"name": "Threshold model (collapsing bounds)", "family": "Heuristic / threshold", "n_params": 2, "metric": "BIC"}

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 157 (75 boys); ages 10–24 years (M = 17.50, SD = 4.34); uniformly sampled across 5 age bins (10–12, 13–15, 16–18, 19–21, 22–24)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 10–24
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate lab limitation — trustees were computer-generated (not real peers), no real-time social interaction, and investment outcomes were not shown during the task. The trust game framing provides some social context but lacks the richness of real peer interactions. No face-to-face or naturalistic social exchanges.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) This is a preprint (bioRxiv, May 2020) that was not certified by peer review at time of posting. Should check whether a peer-reviewed version has since been published — potential duplicate risk. (2) Trustees are computer-generated, not real social agents — social context is framed rather than genuine. (3) Cross-sectional design limits causal inference about developmental change. (4) Investment decisions (trust/not trust) are not modelled — only the sampling decisions are fitted by the computational model. (5) The paper mentions this "was part of a larger imaging study, not relevant to the current study" — there may be a related fMRI paper using the same sample.
- **limitations_reported:** The study does not examine effects of peer status on trustworthiness information sampling; cross-sectional design used rather than longitudinal; uncertainty tolerance in social contexts may follow a distinct developmental trajectory from non-social contexts and "should be confirmed in future studies"; the avoidance mechanism based on initial impressions could lead to biased beliefs about environment statistics; behavioural study only (part of larger imaging study not reported here).
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; cross-sectional design; task simplicity (computer-generated trustees); no neuroimaging analysis reported; generalizability to real social contexts uncertain; no longitudinal data
