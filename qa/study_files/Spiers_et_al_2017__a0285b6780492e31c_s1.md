## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Prejudice learning / illusory correlation learning — learning the valence of novel social groups (majority vs. minority) via associative learning of group-behavior pairings.
- **study_focus_short:** Prejudice learning / illusory correlation learning

- **task_description:** Participants read sentences describing valenced behaviors performed by members of four fictitious social groups (2 majority, 2 minority; 2 positive, 2 negative valence) and intermittently rated how positive or negative they perceived each group. ## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 18 (22 scanned, 4 excluded for excessive head motion; 11 men, age range 20-34, M = 24.1, SD = 4.3)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 20–34
- **ecological_validity:** Lab-based learning task using fictitious groups and pre-rated behavioral sentences. Low ecological validity — real-world prejudice formation involves richer, more varied social interactions. However, the paradigm captures core associative learning principles underlying prejudice.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Very small sample size (N = 18 after exclusions) - Model fit to group-level averaged data only, not individual-level fits - Only one model tested (no model comparison) - Learning rate for positive groups near zero (alpha = 0.002), raising question about whether learning truly occurred for positive groups - Valence confounded with commonness and imageability of sentences (acknowledged by authors) - No ventral striatal PE signal despite being a core prediction of associative learning models - Whole-brain analyses did not survive FWE correction; ROI analyses relied on anatomically predefined regions
- **limitations_reported:** Negative behaviors were less common and less imageable than positive behaviors, confounding valence with these features; cannot separate familiarity and imageability from valence in stimuli; cannot determine whether attitudes were affective vs. purely cognitive/conceptual; cannot dissociate responses involved in updating stored representations from those detecting attitude violations; future research needed to match absolute valence and familiarity; explicit report measure limits conclusions about affective commitment
- **limitations_categorized:** Stimulus confounds; limited ecological validity; inability to dissociate cognitive processes; reliance on explicit measures; small sample size
