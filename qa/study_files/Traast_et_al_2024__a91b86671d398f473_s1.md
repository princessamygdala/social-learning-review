## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Intergroup impression formation via instrumental learning; how race biases social reward learning through expectancies (priors) and updating (learning rates)
- **study_focus_short:** Intergroup impression formation via instrumental learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from interaction partners' sharing feedback about the reward value of Black vs. White partners   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (Black and White interaction partners)     - Source content (non-social): outcome (monetary sharing feedback: shared or not)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (Black and White interaction partners)     - Target content (social): state (mental state; reward value / cooperativeness)
- **task_description:** White American participants played a probabilistic money-sharing game with 8 fictitious players (4 Black, 4 White) presented in fixed Black-White pairs with varying reward rates (70/30, 60/40, 40/60, 30/70). In a training phase, participants chose between paired players and received binary sharing feedback; in a test phase, they chose between all possible Black-White pairings without feedback to assess learned reward preferences.
- **task_paradigm:** Impression formation task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (8 fictitious players; 4 Black, 4 White)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Face photographs from Chicago Face Database (smiling), binary monetary feedback (shared: +1 / shared: 0)
- **method:** online / behavioural
- **method_full:** behavioural / online
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Participants learned to choose higher-rewarding players (OR = 9.08, 95% CI [5.89, 13.99])   - White players chosen more than Black players despite equivalent reward rates (OR = 6.43, 95% CI [2.55, 16.19])   - Race effect moderated by IMS (Race x IMS interaction: OR = 0.79, 95% CI [0.70, 0.89])   - Low-IMS participants showed race effect on choice (β = .57), high-IMS did not (β = −.28, n.s.)   - Initial pro-White preference in first 50 training trials (OR = 2.78, 95% CI [1.44, 5.34])   - Race effect on choice independent of perceived reward rates (OR = 4.01, 95% CI [1.92, 8.38] after covariate adjustment)   - No race effect on self-reported perceived reward rates (β = 1.83, n.s.)   - Best-fitting computational model: group-based prior + learning (AIC = 101.17 vs. group-based prior AIC = 102.32, group-based learning AIC = 103.49, unbiased model worst)
