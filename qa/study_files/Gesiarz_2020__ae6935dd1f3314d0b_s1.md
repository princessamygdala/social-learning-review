## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Self-verifying information-seeking; computational mechanisms underlying preferences for confirmatory vs. disconfirmatory social feedback about personality traits.
- **study_focus_short:** Self-verifying information-seeking
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from others' evaluations of one's personality traits about how to minimize surprise in self-relevant information seeking   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (two group members who provided ratings)     - Source content (social): outcome (personality trait ratings / social feedback)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): self (own personality traits / self-evaluation)     - Target content (non-social): state (beliefs about oneself; self-evaluation confidence)
- **task_description:** Participants in groups of three got to know each other, rated themselves and others on 50 personality adjectives, then over 50 trials chose to reveal either the higher or lower rating others gave them on each trait, followed by happiness ratings. Ratings were actually computer-generated (M=6, SD=2).
- **task_paradigm:** Self-evaluation / self-esteem task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (2 other group members as raters)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Personality trait adjectives (50 positively valenced), numerical ratings (0-10 scale), happiness scale
- **method:** behavioural
- **method_full:** Behavioural (lab-based)
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Surprise negatively predicts momentary happiness (beta = -0.02, p < 0.001) and confidence in self-evaluations (beta = -0.24, p < 0.001)   - Positive prediction error predicts higher happiness (beta = 0.02, p < 0.01)   - Positive information value predicts higher happiness (beta = 0.05, p < 0.001)   - Reference-point heuristic (based on average self-evaluation) outperforms all alternatives (summed BIC comparison across models)   - BIC values for GLME heuristic comparison: self-rating heuristic = 3725.2; other-reference heuristic = 3721.6; self-reference heuristic = 3716.7   - 44.6% of participants best characterized by learning mechanisms; 29.2% by other-reference heuristic; 26.2% by self-reference heuristic   - Cognitive response to surprise correlates with confirmatory choices (partial R = -0.32, p = 0.01)   - Hedonic response to surprise not related to confirmatory choices (partial R = 0.04, p = 0.77)   - Positivity bias in belief updating: positive PE learning rate > negative PE learning rate (t(63) = 5.14, p < 0.001)   - Positive belief update increases confidence; negative decreases it (t(63) = 5.50, p < 0.001)   - Reliance on reference-point heuristic inversely related to mean RT (R = -0.25, p = 0.04)   - Participants using learning strategies had greater absolute deviation of self-esteem from mean expected rating (t(63) = 2.19, p = 0.03)

The deception limits ecological validity. Personality trait feedback is naturalistic but the forced binary choice (higher vs. lower rating) is artificial. Lab-based with undergraduate sample.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Deception: Participants believed ratings came from other group members but they were computer-generated. 29/94 participants (30.9%) excluded for not believing the manipulation -- a high exclusion rate that may bias results toward more gullible participants. - The winning model is a heuristic (not a learning algorithm), which somewhat limits the paper's contribution to understanding learning mechanisms specifically, though learning models were compared. - No parameter recovery or model recovery analyses reported. - Participants could assume trait evaluations were uncorrelated across trials (acknowledged by authors), which may have artificially boosted reliance on heuristics over learning strategies. - Small final sample (N=65) for individual differences analyses.
