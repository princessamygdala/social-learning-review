## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Observational learning — ingroup vs. outgroup action and outcome learning
- **study_focus_short:** Observational learning — ingroup vs. outgroup action and outcome learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from observing ingroup and outgroup demonstrators' actions and outcomes to optimize own choices   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (ingroup or outgroup demonstrator)     - Source content (social): action/policy (observed choices) and outcome (observed reward feedback)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (reward probabilities of fractal stimuli)     - Target content (non-social): stimulus (stimulus-reward contingencies)
- **task_description:** Participants observed an ingroup (same political leaning) or outgroup (opposite political leaning) demonstrator choose between two fractal images with different reward probabilities. In action_outcome blocks they saw both the demonstrator's choice and outcome; in action_only blocks they saw only the choice. Participants then chose between the same fractals to maximize their own rewards.
- **task_paradigm:** Observational learning task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (2 demonstrators: 1 ingroup, 1 outgroup; computer-generated choices presented as social agents)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Abstract fractal images, binary reward outcomes (0 or 10), abstract symbols representing demonstrators, political initiative rating scales for group induction
- **method:** fMRI / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI (main study); behavioural (control study)
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Outgroup deficit in observational learning: fewer correct choices after observing outgroup vs. ingroup demonstrator (F(1,28) = 7.839, η² = 0.219)   - Outgroup deficit driven by action-only condition (F(1,28) = 7.421, η² = 0.210), marginal in action-outcome condition (F(1,28) = 3.323, η² = 0.106)   - Outgroup vs. computer demonstrator: fewer correct choices (F(1,57) = 14.343, η² = 0.201), confirming outgroup deficit rather than ingroup enhancement   - Model comparison: best model has common outcome weight (ω) but separate action weights (κ) for ingroup vs. outgroup   - κ-ingroup > κ-outgroup (Wilcoxon z = 3.13)   - Left IFG activity correlated with ingroup vs. outgroup differential κ (MNI: -34, 0, 28; Z = 4.07, p_FWE = 0.041)
- **effect_size:** - η² = 0.219 (group effect on correct choices, fMRI study) - η² = 0.210 (group effect, action-only condition) - η² = 0.106 (group effect, action-outcome condition, marginal) - η² = 0.201 (outgroup vs. computer demonstrator) - z = 3.13 (κ-ingroup vs. κ-outgroup, Wilcoxon) - Z_stats = 4.07 (IFG correlation with differential κ, whole-brain FWE corrected p = 0.041)

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Observational RL with common outcome weight (ω) and separate action weights (κ_ingroup, κ_outgroup); softmax with perseverance parameter (range 0–3)  Equations: - sOPE = R^other_t - Q^other_outcome_(t-1) (simulated outcome prediction error) - APE = A^other_t - P^subject_(t-1) (action prediction error) - Q update (outcome): Q^subject_(t) = Q^subject_(t-1) + ω × sOPE - P update (action): P^subject_(t) = P^subject_(t-1) + κ × APE - ω common for ingroup/outgroup; κ separate for ingroup/outgroup
- **model_family:** Rescorla-Wagner
- **model_class:** PE learning
- **all_models_tested:** [   {"name": "Common ω, common κ", "family": "Observational RL", "n_params": 3, "metric": "AIC"},   {"name": "Separate ω, common κ", "family": "Observational RL", "n_params": 4, "metric": "AIC"},   {"name": "Common ω, separate κ", "family": "Observational RL", "n_params": 4, "metric": "AIC"},   {"name": "Separate ω, separate κ", "family": "Observational RL", "n_params": 5, "metric": "AIC"} ] Note: An alternative model family adding κ-weighted APEs to the action_outcome condition was also tested (4 additional models) but fit worse (mean AIC 18.56 vs. 17.58).

## Quality
- **sample_size:** fMRI study: N = 29 (of 32 recruited; 19 females; mean age 22.51 ± 0.54 years; 3 excluded for right-wing attitudes). Control behavioral study: N = 30 (of 33 recruited; 18 females; mean age 23.25 ± 3.34 years; 3 excluded for right-wing attitudes). All right-handed, Swiss, politically left-wing.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=22.51
- **ecological_validity:** Limited — political group membership manipulated via ratings of Swiss political initiatives; demonstrator choices were computer-generated (not actual human choices); abstract fractal stimuli with binary outcomes; no real social interaction between participant and demonstrators.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Demonstrator choices were computer-generated using a standard RL algorithm (α = 0.3, β = 0.4), not actual human choices — participants were deceived into believing they were from real ingroup/outgroup members. Only left-wing participants studied, limiting generalizability. Relatively small fMRI sample (N = 29). Mean fitted parameter values for ω, κ_ingroup, κ_outgroup, and perseverance not reported in paper. No parameter recovery or model recovery reported.
- **limitations_reported:** Used exclusively political attitude to manipulate group membership; studied left-wing participants only — future research should study right-wing individuals or other political groups; relatively small sample size — recent studies recommend larger samples for replicable fMRI findings; correlation findings should be reassessed with a larger sample; did not specify which outgroup attributes drive the group differences in observational learning mechanisms; did not investigate whether participants' trust of outgroup actions or individual impressions/emotions toward the outgroup modulate the deficit
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited generalizability (single group manipulation type); limited generalizability (single political group studied); sample size; sample size (fMRI replicability); task simplicity (outgroup attributes not specified); construct validity (trust/emotion mechanisms not tested)
