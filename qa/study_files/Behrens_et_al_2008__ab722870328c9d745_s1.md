## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Trust learning / social information valuation — learning the reliability of a social partner's advice via associative (reinforcement learning) mechanisms, in parallel with reward-based learning.
- **study_focus_short:** Trust learning / social information valuation
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from a confederate's advice accuracy and from reward outcomes about the trustworthiness of the confederate and the correct action   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (confederate)       - Source content (social): action/policy (advice accuracy — correct vs. incorrect)     - Source type (non-social): world       - Source content (non-social): outcome (reward — correct/incorrect action feedback)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (confederate)       - Target content (social): state (mental state; trustworthiness/fidelity)     - Target type (non-social): world       - Target content (non-social): state (probability of correct action)
- **task_description:** Participants chose between two options (blue/green) to accumulate points while undergoing fMRI. On each trial, they received advice from a supposed human confederate (actually computer-controlled) and then observed the outcome, allowing them to independently track the probability of the correct action and the trustworthiness of the confederate's advice, which changed in volatility over time.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 24 (14M/10F; mean age 29, SD 11.75 years, range 20–62; 4 left-handed). 1 excluded for head motion, leaving N = 23 for fMRI analyses.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 20–62
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. Participants believed they were interacting with a real confederate (actually computer-controlled), which enhances social realism. However, the advice was binary (correct/incorrect) and the task structure is highly controlled and abstracted from real social interactions. The wider-than-usual age range (recruited from general public, not students) is a strength for generalizability.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Only one computational model tested (no formal model comparison) - Confederate was actually computer-controlled (not a real social partner), though participants believed it was real - No parameter recovery or model recovery reported - Model was previously published (Behrens et al., 2007) and applied here without formal comparison to alternatives - Relatively wide age range (20–62) may introduce heterogeneity; some cross-subject correlations reduced when older subjects excluded (Table S1) - Free parameters (ω values) not reported as mean fitted values
- **limitations_reported:** Authors acknowledge that the wide age range might influence results, and one of the key three-way interaction effects did not survive exclusion of subjects over 30; authors note the confederate deception design may limit full ecological social interaction.
- **limitations_categorized:** sample heterogeneity (age range); limited ecological validity; no model comparison; no parameter recovery
