## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Bayesian belief updating model with discretized binomial belief distributions over harmful intent (HI) and self-interest (SI), with partner policy uncertainty. 6 parameters: pHI0, pSI0 (initial attribution modes), uHI0, uSI0 (initial attribution uncertainties), uP (partner policy uncertainty), eta (cross-dictator learning rate). Fitted via MAP estimation with grid-search + gradient ascent.
- **model_family:** Bayesian
- **model_class:** Belief updating
- **all_models_tested:** [{"name": "Bayesian belief updating (6 params)", "family": "Bayesian belief updating", "n_params": 6, "metric": "log-likelihood / MAP"}] Note: Only one computational model was tested. The paper also used information-theoretic model comparison (AICc) for the statistical regression models (not the computational model itself), and network modelling (EBIC for GGM selection).
- **model_mb_mf:** Bayesian
- **model_params:** - pHI0: initial mode of harmful intent prior distribution (central tendency of HI belief) [S] - pSI0: initial mode of self-interest prior distribution (central tendency of SI belief) [S] - uHI0: initial uncertainty (spread) of harmful intent prior distribution [S] - uSI0: initial uncertainty (spread) of self-interest prior distribution [S] - uP: partner policy uncertainty -- how informative partner's actions are about their true attributes [S] - eta (η): learning rate -- how much posterior from previous dictator influences prior for next dictator [S]  Mean fitted values not reported as single means (density distributions shown in Fig 7 but no numeric means provided in text).
- **social_param:** All parameters are social: pHI0 and uHI0 govern harmful intent attribution; pSI0 and uSI0 govern self-interest attribution; uP captures uncertainty about how partner attributes map to partner behaviour; eta captures cross-partner social learning rate.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 1754 (from two online studies combined; original recruitment N = 1998, reduced due to dropout/failed control questions); general population sample from Prolific Academic (UK, fluent English, no current/history of mental illness)
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Online study using a modified Dictator game with real human partners (ex-post matching), which improves ecological validity over purely simulated opponents. However, the interaction is economically constrained (binary take-half or take-all decisions), lacks real-time social cues (eye contact, facial expressions, verbal communication), and uses cartoon avatars rather than real faces. The task captures only a narrow slice of social attribution processes.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Only one computational model was tested -- no alternative model comparison (e.g., reinforcement learning, simpler heuristic models) - Emission noise and partner policy uncertainty are confounded (acknowledged by authors) - Computational analysis was not preregistered (noted by authors) - The paper reanalyses behavioural data from Barnby et al. (2020, Royal Society Open Science) -- potential overlap with that dataset if also included in the review; flag as possible duplicate data source - Attribution ratings discretized from 1-100 into 9 bins for modelling, losing resolution - No formal model comparison metric (BIC/AIC) used to compare alternative computational models since only one was tested
- **limitations_reported:** Cannot capture all social nuance present in real-world interaction; other social factors may influence learning rates and uncertainty over inferred social intentions (e.g., eye contact, multiple social actors); unable to separately fit participants' own noisy reporting (emission noise) vs. noisy aspects of their generative model of others (dictator policy uncertainty) due to limited data per dictator; computational analysis was not preregistered
- **limitations_categorized:** limited ecological validity; task simplicity; parameter identifiability; emission noise confound; no preregistration of computational analysis
