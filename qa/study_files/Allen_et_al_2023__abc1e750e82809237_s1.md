## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Cooperation learning; reciprocal cooperation; social exchange learning; mentalizing and individual differences in callousness and exploitativeness
- **study_focus_short:** Cooperation learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from counterfactual and actual outcomes of social exchange decisions to update the value of one's cooperation policy toward a social partner   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (trustee partner)     - Source content (social): outcomes (trustee's share/keep decisions, including counterfactual feedback)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (trustee partner)     - Target content (social): action/policy (value of keeping vs. sharing with partner; one's own cooperative policy)
- **task_description:** In a modified iterative trust game, participants received $1.00 per trial to keep or share with one of three trustees (good, bad, neutral reputation); if they shared, the trustee could reciprocate ($1.50) or defect ($0), and critically, participants saw the trustee's decision even on keep trials, enabling counterfactual learning.
- **task_paradigm:** Trust game
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (3 trustees of varying reputation: good, bad, neutral)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** human (recorded)
- **stimuli:** Neutral male face photographs (NimStim), text reputation descriptions, monetary outcomes ($0/$1.00/$1.50)
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Policy model dominated all alternatives in Bayesian model comparison (protected exceedance probability = 1.00, BOR < .001) - PE modulation of the default network predicted greater reciprocity (beta = .25, 95% CI [.09, .39], p < .001) - Callousness negatively associated with PE modulation of default network (beta = -.39, SE = .10, p < .001) - Exploitativeness positively associated with PE modulation of default network (beta = .26, SE = .10, p = .01) - Indirect effect of callousness on reciprocity via PE-DN (b = -.05, 95% CI [-.10, -.02], p < .001) - Indirect effect of exploitativeness on reciprocity via PE-DN (b = .03, 95% CI [.004, .07], p = .03) - Callousness moderated effect of reputation on sharing (beta = .26, 95% CI [.04, .45], p = .01) - PE signaling in medial temporal subsystem moderated reputation effect on sharing (beta = -.31, 95% CI [-.53, -.03], p = .04)
- **effect_size:** - PE-DN on reciprocity: beta = .25, 95% CI [.09, .39] - Callousness on PE-DN: beta = -.39, SE = .10 - Exploitativeness on PE-DN: beta = .26, SE = .10 - Indirect callousness -> PE-DN -> reciprocity: b = -.05, 95% CI [-.10, -.02] - Indirect exploitativeness -> PE-DN -> reciprocity: b = .03, 95% CI [.004, .07] - Callousness on reputation effect: beta = .26, 95% CI [.04, .45] - Medial temporal PE on reputation effect: beta = -.31, 95% CI [-.53, -.03] - Antagonism on learning rate: beta = -.05, SE = .02

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 168 (113 BPD, 55 healthy controls) for behavioral analyses; N = 150 for fMRI (17 excluded: 1 missing, 4 poor quality, 14 excessive motion); N = 161 for self-report; N = 144 for personality-neuroimaging models (listwise deletion). Mean age = 31.16 (SD = 9.30); 78% female.
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. Trust game is a well-established paradigm for studying social exchange, but interactions are with pre-programmed trustees (not real people), reducing ecological validity. Counterfactual feedback display is informative but somewhat artificial. Clinical sample (BPD + healthy controls) enhances individual difference variability but may limit generalizability to general population.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) Preprint, not peer-reviewed at time of posting. (2) Trustees are pre-programmed, not real interaction partners — participants may or may not have believed they were real. (3) Cross-sectional design precludes causal inference. (4) Clinical sample mixing BPD and healthy controls is treated as continuous, which is appropriate for dimensional analysis but may obscure group-level differences. (5) No mean fitted parameter values reported. (6) Coordinates are center-of-mass rather than peak voxel coordinates. (7) Parameter recovery and model recovery cited from prior work (Vanyukov et al., 2019) rather than conducted in the current study.
- **limitations_reported:** Our observations were cross-sectional in nature and contingent on the naturalistic manipulation of human individual differences, precluding strong causal inferences.
- **limitations_categorized:** Cross-sectional design; limited causal inference; correlational individual differences
