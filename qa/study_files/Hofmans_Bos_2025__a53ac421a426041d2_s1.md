- **n_players:** single agent (1)

- **stimuli:** Mushroom fields (5 or 45 mushrooms per field, blue/red), peer estimate (numeric), peer confidence rating (low/medium/high), slider response
- **method:** fMRI / online / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI (Experiment 2); behavioural/online (Experiment 1)
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Social information use was higher in the uncertain vs. certain condition (Exp 1: b = -0.05, P < 0.001; Exp 2: b = -0.16, P < 0.001)   - Social information use increased with peer confidence (Exp 1: b = 0.09, P < 0.001; Exp 2: b = 0.17, P < 0.001)   - Interaction of own certainty x peer confidence (Exp 1: b = -0.02, P = 0.012; Exp 2: b = -0.03, P = 0.002)   - Winning Bayesian model M3d with stay bias exponentially modulated by peer confidence best captured behavior across both experiments   - Neural: Own certainty correlated positively with bilateral dlPFC, dorsomedial PFC, bilateral anterior insula, left caudate; negatively with left premotor gyrus, left mid cingulate, medial PFC   - Neural: Peer confidence correlated positively with medial PFC, ACC, caudate nucleus   - Neural: KL divergence (belief update) correlated positively with ACC and ventral mPFC   - Partial overlap in ventral mPFC for own uncertainty, peer confidence, and KL divergence

## Quality
- **sample_size:** Experiment 1: N=101 (50 women; mean age 24.0, SD 5.7, range 18-45; online behavioral). Experiment 2: N=68 behavioral (40 women; mean age 24.8, SD 3.8, range 22-43), N=58 fMRI after quality exclusions (35 women; mean age 24.3, SD 3.5, range 22-43).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–45
- **ecological_validity:** Task is based on the validated BEAST paradigm (Molleman et al., 2019). Peer estimates came from real previous participants, enhancing ecological validity. However, the social component is constrained: participants received explicit confidence reports rather than engaging in interactive social exchanges or interpreting implicit social cues. The authors acknowledge the task structure is similar to nonsocial paradigms where participants update decisions based on feedback. The mushroom estimation context is somewhat abstract.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Coordinates could not be extracted from the supplement (.docx tables S3-S5 not available in .txt conversion) -- these should be re-extracted from the .docx or PDF supplement. - The paper only compares models within the Bayesian family; no comparison to alternative model families (e.g., RL, sequential sampling) was conducted. Authors acknowledge this limitation. - The "social" nature of the task is debatable -- peer information is presented as a number + confidence label, functionally similar to a second sample or advisory cue. No TPJ/dmPFC activation for peer confidence effects, which the authors note. - Brain mask restricted to frontal lobes, insula, TPJ, striatum -- not truly whole-brain. - The peer estimate was always in the direction of the true percentage and 15-18 percentage points from E1, which constrains the ecological representativeness.
- **limitations_reported:** Our current design makes it challenging to definitively tease apart the separate neural correlates of peer confidence and belief updating, as participants most likely start the integration of the personal and peer information immediately upon seeing the peer information"; "Another limitation lies in the way information was presented across own certainty conditions... This creates a potential confound between inferential certainty and perceptual certainty"; "The ecological validity of our task design... the social component was somewhat constrained, with participants receiving explicit confidence reports, rather than engaging in interactive social exchanges or interpreting implicit social cues"; "Our modeling approach was limited to a Bayesian framework... we did not directly compare Bayesian models to alternative model families, such as sequential sampling models"; "Our study design (e.g., trial number, sample size) was suboptimal to detect subtle trial-by-trial changes in connectivity
- **limitations_categorized:** confound between neural signals for peer confidence and belief updating; confound between inferential and perceptual certainty; limited ecological validity; limited model comparison (single model family); insufficient power for connectivity analyses

- **other_notes:** This paper presents a rigorous Bayesian modeling approach to social information use, satisfying 10/10 Wilson & Collins criteria. The key novel contribution is the identification of overlapping ventral mPFC activity for own uncertainty, peer confidence, and belief updating (KL divergence), suggesting this region serves as a convergence zone for integrating precision estimates from self and social sources. The stay bias parameter, exponentially modulated by peer confidence, is a notable modeling innovation capturing the heuristic decision of whether to integrate social information at all, separate from the Bayesian precision-weighting process.
