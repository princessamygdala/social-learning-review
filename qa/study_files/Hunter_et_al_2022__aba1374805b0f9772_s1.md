- **sample_size:** N = 966 total recruited (500 per experiment); N = 743 after exclusions (N1 = 412, Experiment 1; N2 = 331, Experiment 2); ages 18+; recruited via Amazon Mechanical Turk

- **ecological_validity:** Task uses a competitive economic game with social framing (opponent drawn from pool of previous human players), but interaction is with a computerized opponent, not live. Online administration via AMT increases sample diversity but reduces experimental control. The patent race game has abstract monetary framing rather than naturalistic social interaction.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - The "social agent" is a computerized opponent drawing from a pool of previous human choices, not a live interaction — this is worth flagging though the paper explicitly discusses this as a socially framed task. - No direct measure of rumination was collected; the link between upward counterfactual updating and rumination is theoretical. - Individual-level parameter estimates from the EM procedure rely on group-level priors, which may reduce individual variation. - The regression coefficients for social anxiety on model parameters are relatively small in absolute terms.
- **limitations_reported:** We do not assess rumination directly (and this is an important direction for future work)"; task framing effects — the association of social anxiety with model-basedness was stronger in this socially framed task than in Gillan et al.'s single-player Markov decision task, raising questions about specificity; no evidence that compulsivity is associated with reduced model-based learning in this task, in contrast to findings from two-step tasks; social anxiety may be distinct from other mood disorders in that overthinking is goal-directed rather than idle/distracting; future work comparing multiple tasks and manipulating framing while holding structure fixed is needed.
- **limitations_categorized:** no direct rumination measure; task specificity/generalizability; limited ecological validity; social framing confound; no live social interaction; online sample
