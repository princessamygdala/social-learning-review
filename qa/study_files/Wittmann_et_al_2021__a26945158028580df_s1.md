## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Self-other mergence — learning about one's own and others' abilities in cooperative and competitive social contexts, and the causal role of dmPFC in separating self and other performance representations.
- **study_focus_short:** Self-other mergence
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from one's own and another player's performance feedback about ability estimates for self and other, modulated by cooperative/competitive social context.   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self       - Source content (non-social): outcome (performance feedback)     - Source type (social): other (co-player)       - Source content (social): outcome (performance feedback)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): self       - Target content (non-social): state (ability estimate)     - Target type (social): other (co-player)       - Target content (social): state (ability estimate)
- **task_description:** Participants performed short reaction-time-based "minigames" and received predetermined performance feedback for themselves and two other players on each trial, while making engage/avoid decisions in cooperative or competitive contexts. Before each minigame, participants rated expected performance for self and the relevant other player, providing a behavioral readout of ability estimation.
- **task_paradigm:** Social comparison task
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (2 other players; cooperative or competitive pairing per trial)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Abstract minigames (reaction-time perceptual tasks), numerical performance feedback on 15-point scales, player initials, cooperative/competitive context cues
- **method:** fMRI / TMS
- **method_full:** fMRI, TMS (cTBS)
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Self-other mergence (SOM) present at baseline: O-ability influenced more positively by S-performance in cooperation than competition (SOM_int(S/O): t(55) = 3.233)   - S-ability similarly influenced by O-performance in a context-dependent manner (SOM_int(O/S): t(55) = 2.162)   - cTBS to dmPFC reduced neural SOM_int(S/O) signal in dmPFC (z = 3.94 at MNI 6/46/30)   - cTBS to dmPFC increased behavioral SOM_int(S/O) (group × stimulation interaction: F(1,54) = 6.681; within dmPFC group: t(27) = 2.578)   - S-performance signal during competition reduced after dmPFC cTBS vs. vertex (z = −4.27 at MNI −4/46/24; survived whole-brain correction)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 56 (28 dmPFC group, 28 vertex group); age range 18–39; 26 female. 65 recruited total, 9 excluded (2 premature cessation, 4 technical difficulties, 2 inability to follow procedure, 1 believed deception).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 18–39
- **ecological_validity:** Low-to-moderate. Task uses abstract minigames with predetermined feedback rather than real social interaction. Other players' performance was pre-recorded. Cooperation/competition context was experimenter-determined, not participant-chosen. Social framing via initials only (no faces or real-time interaction).
- **eligibility_flag:** The RL model serves as a preprocessing tool to derive performance estimates rather than as the primary model being tested. The paper's main contribution is about causal brain manipulation effects on self-other mergence (a neural/behavioral phenomenon), with the computational modeling playing a supporting rather than central role. The learning component (tracking ability estimates over time) meets inclusion criteria, but the primary SOM effects themselves are described as "decision-related rather than learning-related" by the authors (line 230). FLAG: Borderline — SOM is explicitly described as decision-related, not learning-related; the learning component (RL model) is a preprocessing step, not the main focus.
- **concerns:** - Only one RL model tested — no model comparison or competing models - RL model parameters (α, β) fitted at group level (all participants pooled within each group), not individually — this may obscure individual differences - Exact fitted parameter values for α and β not reported - No parameter recovery or model recovery reported - No simulation of model before fitting - SOM effects are explicitly characterized as decision-related (current context) rather than learning-related (accumulated history), raising questions about whether this qualifies as computational modeling of social learning per se - The correlation between neural and behavioral SOM within the dmPFC group was not significant (r = −0.205, p = 0.295)
- **limitations_reported:** Authors note that the within-participant dmPFC group correlation between neural and behavioral SOM was not significant; they acknowledge potential ambiguities in interpreting correlations between behavior and neural activity across participants; they note the need for causal methods beyond correlational fMRI to disambiguate interpretations; they acknowledge that the cTBS effect was particularly pronounced during competition and offer a post-hoc explanation for this asymmetry.
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; no model comparison; group-level parameter fitting; no parameter recovery; no model recovery; asymmetric TMS effects across conditions; non-significant brain-behavior correlation within group
