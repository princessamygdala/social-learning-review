## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 71 (40 female; age M = 19.8, SD = 1.34 years); anodal group n = 36, sham group n = 35. All right-handed university students.
- **population_category:** undergraduates
- **population_age_range:** M=19.8 (SD=1.34)
- **ecological_validity:** Low ecological validity — laboratory-based economic game (patent race) with abstract monetary outcomes; opponents randomly matched from pool (not face-to-face interaction); nonstrategic condition uses computer-generated hurdle. Single university student sample limits generalizability.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) No parameter recovery or model recovery analyses reported. (2) Number of free parameters for RL and BB models not specified, making model comparison less transparent. (3) The δ parameter (degree of belief learning) was not significantly different between anodal and sham groups (p = .476), despite the authors' claim that tDCS enhanced belief-based learning — the significant effect was on ρ (depreciation of prior beliefs). (4) Sample from single Chinese university limits generalizability. (5) No effect sizes (Cohen's d) reported for between-group parameter comparisons. (6) The softmax λ parameter values are not reported per group.
- **limitations_reported:** Population limited in diversity as recruitment was from a single university with participants of similar ages; mentalizing abilities and executive functions such as working memory may play a role in belief-based learning but were not measured to control for individual differences; the mechanism underlying the modulation effect of rTPJ on belief-based learning was not identified.
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited generalizability; lack of individual difference measures; mechanism unclear; sample homogeneity.
