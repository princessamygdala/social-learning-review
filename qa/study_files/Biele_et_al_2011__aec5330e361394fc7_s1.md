## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Learning from advice — how one-time advice modulates reinforcement learning through an outcome-bonus mechanism
- **study_focus_short:** Learning from advice
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from advisor's recommendation about which option to choose, combined with individual reward feedback, to learn option values   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (advisor)       - Source content (social): action/policy (recommendation of which deck to choose)     - Source type (non-social): self       - Source content (non-social): outcome (monetary gains/losses from chosen deck)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world       - Target content (non-social): state (expected reward values of card decks)
- **task_description:** Participants performed a 4-armed bandit task (168 trials) choosing among four card decks to maximize cumulative rewards, after receiving one-time advice from a prior participant indicating which deck to choose. Each trial provided binary gain/loss feedback of varying magnitudes.

- **n_players:** single agent (1)
- **partner_type:** human (live)
- **stimuli:** Four card decks (abstract), monetary gains and losses as feedback, written advice form
- **method:** fMRI
- **method_full:** fMRI
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Sustained preference for recommended deck over non-recommended deck with identical payoff distribution (41.5% vs 21.5% of choices; p < .0001)   - Outcome-bonus model best explains sustained advice-following (Bayes factor ~30-40x over individual learning model)   - Effect of good vs bad advice on choosing good decks greater in first half (r = .39) than second half (r = .25)   - Neural outcome-bonus signal in septal area and left caudate head (max z = 3.49; MNI: x = 4, y = 2, z = 4)   - Correlation between individual outcome-bonus parameter and amygdala gain-signal difference after following vs not following advice (max z = 3.02)   - Attenuated standard reward signal in VMPFC and VST when following advice

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Prior+Outcome-Bonus RW model (3 free params: $\alpha$ learning rate, $\tau$ choice sensitivity, $b_b$ outcome-bonus; prior $b_p$ also free)  Formally: - Softmax choice: $p_i(t) = \exp[\tau \cdot q_i(t)] / \sum_j \exp[\tau \cdot q_j(t)]$ - Update with outcome-bonus: $q_i(t+1) = q_i(t) + \alpha[r_i(t) + g(i) \cdot m \cdot b_b - q_i(t)]$ - Prior: $q_i(1) = g(i) \cdot m \cdot b_p \cdot N$ - Where $g(i)$ = 1 if option $i$ is recommended, 0 otherwise; $m$ = expected payoff from random choice; $N$ = number of trials
- **model_family:** Rescorla-Wagner
- **model_class:** PE learning
- **all_models_tested:** - {"name": "Individual learning (standard RL)", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 2, "metric": "BIC/AIC"} - {"name": "Prior model", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 3, "metric": "BIC/AIC"} - {"name": "Outcome-bonus model", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 3, "metric": "BIC/AIC"} - {"name": "Prior+outcome-bonus model", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 4, "metric": "BIC/AIC"} - {"name": "Dynamic outcome-bonus", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 4, "metric": "BIC/AIC"} - {"name": "Dynamic outcome-bonus + prior", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 5, "metric": "BIC/AIC"} - {"name": "Outcome-bonus gains only", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 3, "metric": "BIC/AIC"} - {"name": "Outcome-bonus losses only", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 3, "metric": "BIC/AIC"} - {"name": "Loss-as-zero model", "family": "Rescorla-Wagner", "n_params": 3, "metric": "BIC/AIC"}  (Note: Text S1 and Tables S1-S3 contain full model details but supplement was not accessible as a separate file.)

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 21 (fMRI participants; 16 received good advice, 5 received bad advice); ages not reported in main text. Additional 10 advisors (behavioral only).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate — advice was given once before scanning by a real human advisor who was motivated to give good advice (payment partially dependent on receiver's earnings), mimicking naturalistic advice-taking. However, advice was one-time only and the task was a lab-based 4-armed bandit, which limits generalizability to more complex real-world advice-following scenarios.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Small sample size (N = 21, only 5 received bad advice making bad-advice subgroup analyses underpowered); advice-following trials were not randomized (participants chose when to follow advice), introducing potential confounds; mean fitted parameter values referenced in supplement Table S3 which was not separately accessible; the paper acknowledges the non-randomized design as a limitation.
- **limitations_reported:** Participants controlled when to follow advice, so that advice-following trials were not randomized across the experiment. Hence, additional factors might have influenced the observed differences between following and not following advice"; "Future experiments that randomly interleave trials of tasks with and without advice should further investigate this issue"; "future research is needed to show whether the influence of advice is stable or dynamic when the task is relatively easy or the competence of the advisor is less uncertain.
- **limitations_categorized:** non-randomized design; limited ecological validity; task simplicity; limited generalizability
