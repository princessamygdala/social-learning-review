- **study_focus_short:** Mentalizing learning · recursive belief updating in competitive social interactions

- **task_description:** Participants played a repeated competitive "hide and seek" game (60 trials per game) against four artificial agents of varying Theory of Mind sophistication (random biased, 0-ToM, 1-ToM, 2-ToM), choosing between two options and receiving correct/incorrect feedback. The task was framed either as playing against other human players (social framing) or as a casino gambling task (non-social framing), with identical feedback structure in both conditions.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 29 recruited (15 female, mean age = 22.5, SD = 3.8); 3 excluded for low 3-back performance; N = 26 analysed
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=22.5
- **ecological_validity:** Laboratory-based competitive game with artificial opponents; social framing created through instructions rather than actual human interaction (deception by omission — participants believed they were playing against other humans). Limited ecological validity due to abstract binary choice task and artificial agents, though the framing manipulation itself is informative about the role of social context.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Order effect: social framing always performed before non-social framing (not counterbalanced), creating a potential confound - Participants played against artificial agents, not real humans — the social manipulation relies on belief, not actual social interaction - Confusion between WSLS and Bayesian sequence learning models in the non-social framing (partial model non-identifiability within T− family) - No correction for multiple comparisons on condition-specific tests (though FWER thresholds are reported) - Relatively small sample (N = 26)
- **limitations_reported:** order effect" — social framing always preceded non-social, which could introduce training, priming, or cognitive fatigue effects; potential motivational/attentional confounds from social vs. non-social framing; potential confound from difference in understanding of task structure between framings; model confusion between WSLS and Bayesian sequence learning in non-social condition; k-ToM model does not capture mentalizing from low-level social signals (eye gaze, facial expression, body posture); unclear how results generalize to contexts with different incentives for mentalizing; effort cost of mentalizing in complex settings might overcome expected gains
- **limitations_categorized:** order effects; limited ecological validity; potential motivational confounds; model non-identifiability; model scope limitations; limited generalizability; task simplicity
