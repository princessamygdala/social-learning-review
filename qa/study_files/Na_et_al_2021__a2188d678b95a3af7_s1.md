## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social controllability learning — how humans estimate and exploit the controllability of their social environment via forward thinking / mental simulation of future social interactions.
- **study_focus_short:** Social controllability learning
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from one's own action outcomes (accept/reject) about the controllability of social partners' future offers   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self       - Participants' own accept/reject decisions and subsequent changes in offers     - Source content (non-social): outcomes       - Whether offers increase or decrease after one's actions   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): other (multiple partners from a team)     - Target content (social): action/policy       - Partners' future offer behavior; the degree to which one's actions influence others' proposals (social controllability / expected influence, δ)
- **task_description:** Participants played a social exchange game adapted from the ultimatum game with virtual members of two teams (40 trials per team for fMRI; 30 for online). In each trial, participants decided to accept or reject a split of $20 proposed by a partner; in the Controllable condition, rejecting increased and accepting decreased the next partner's offer, while in the Uncontrollable condition offers were random.
- **task_paradigm:** Ultimatum game
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (40 different virtual partners per condition, organized into 2 teams)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Monetary offer splits ($20), team labels, avatars (online version), emotion rating scales
- **method:** fMRI / online / behavioural
- **method_full:** fMRI, behavioural (online replication)
- **main_result:** - Participants raised offers over time in Controllable vs. Uncontrollable condition (mean_C = 5.9 vs. mean_U = 4.8, t(47.45) = 4.33) - 2-step forward thinking (FT) model best explained behavior in both conditions (DIC comparison: 2-step vs. 0-step, Controllable t(47) = -4.45; Uncontrollable t(47) = -4.21) - Expected influence (δ) higher for Controllable than Uncontrollable (mean_C = 1.33 vs. mean_U = 0.98, t(47) = 2.90) - 2-step FT model prediction accuracy: 83.7% Controllable, 90.1% Uncontrollable - vmPFC parametrically tracked total value (current + future) from 2-step FT model (P_FDR < 0.05, k > 50) - vmPFC ROI coefficients for 2-step FT values > 0: Controllable mean = 0.29, t(47) = 1.96; Uncontrollable mean = 0.24, t(47) = 2.14 - 0-step model values not tracked by vmPFC at even liberal threshold (p < 0.005 uncorrected) - Behavioral replication in online sample (n = 1342): expected influence C > U (t(1341) = 12.97) - Expected influence correlated with task performance (r = 0.78, Controllable condition) - Norm prediction errors tracked in VS (4, 14, -14) and rAI (32, 16, -14) for Controllable; ACC (2, 46, 16) for Uncontrollable

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 48 fMRI (33 female, mean age 27.6 ± 9.1, from original 56 minus 8 exclusions); N = 1342 online replication (649 female, mean age 34.5 ± 12.8, from original 1499 minus 157 exclusions); N = 27 non-social control task
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=27.6
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. Uses an economic exchange game framed as social interaction with virtual team members, which captures aspects of reputation and social influence. However, partners are computer-controlled (not real humans), the ultimatum game is a simplified interaction, and the controllability structure is binary (fully controllable vs. fully uncontrollable), limiting ecological generalizability to real-world graded social controllability.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Partners are computer algorithms, not real humans, though framed socially - Offer distributions not perfectly matched between conditions (Controllable range $1-9, Uncontrollable range $2-8 for fMRI) - Temporal discounting parameter γ fixed at 0.8 rather than fitted per participant, which could affect δ estimates - One-tailed tests used for vmPFC ROI analyses - Supplementary files (Supplementary file 1, containing supplementary tables with whole-brain coordinates) not accessible in this extraction
- **limitations_reported:** Offer distributions not completely matched between conditions, and reduction in uncertainty may be inherent to controllability; lack of clear instruction about controllability conditions may have affected exploitation of controllability and development of illusion of control; future designs should dissociate uncertainty from controllability; explicit instructions might better isolate controllability-specific behaviors and neural substrates
- **limitations_categorized:** stimulus confounds; limited ecological validity; task design limitations; instruction ambiguity
