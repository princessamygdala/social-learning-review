## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 72 (36 adults: 20 females, M = 21.86 years, SD = 2.09, range 19-29; 36 adolescents: 19 females, M = 15.75 years, SD = 1.14, range 14-18). Two additional participants excluded (1 interrupted, 1 misunderstood task).
- **population_category:** mixed
- **population_age_range:** 19–29
- **ecological_validity:** Low-moderate. Uses a repeated trust game with anonymous fictional partners and preprogrammed return rates, which captures dynamic trust learning but lacks genuine social interaction. Online administration via E-Prime. Binary forced-choice (invest all or keep all) is less naturalistic than graded investment decisions.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** (1) Partners are fictional/preprogrammed, so there is no genuine social interaction — participants believed they were interacting with real people from prior sessions. (2) The table (Table 1) in the PDF appears garbled/reversed text, making it difficult to verify exact parameter values — values were extracted from the text and figure descriptions. (3) No parameter recovery or model recovery reported. (4) No simulation reported before fitting. (5) Softmax β is bounded 0-1 which is unusual (typically unbounded or bounded differently); the paper describes β close to 1 as explorative and close to 0 as exploitative, which is the inverse of standard convention. (6) The RW_P and RW_LG models both have 3 free parameters, so AIC comparison between them is essentially a log-likelihood comparison.
- **limitations_reported:** Authors do not have a dedicated limitations section. From discussion: the study uses a cooperative paradigm (trust game) which may bias toward forgiveness strategies; the asymmetric pattern may be task-specific rather than general; the developmental comparison is cross-sectional rather than longitudinal; only two age groups examined (adolescents and adults), limiting developmental trajectory conclusions.
- **limitations_categorized:** task specificity; limited ecological validity; cross-sectional design; limited age range; no genuine social interaction (preprogrammed partners)
