- **task_description:** Children (ages 3-9) played a card-matching game on tablets, believing they were interacting with a same-gender peer. They chose one of two colored cards, scoring points if their card matched the card "chosen" by the other player. Unbeknownst to participants, they played against an artificial agent that alternated between cooperative blocks (20 trials, matching the child's likely choice) and competitive blocks (10 trials, opposing the child's likely choice) without warning.

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** Age-dependent: BSL0 (Bayesian Sequence Learner, depth 0; 0 free parameters beyond Bayesian updating) for KG (3-5 yo) and EPS (6-7 yo); MIIL (Mixed-Intentions Influence Learning; 7 parameters: η_coop, η_comp, λ_coop, λ_comp, β_other_coop, β_other_comp, softmax β) for MPS (8-9 yo). The MIIL model arbitrates between cooperative and competitive influence learning experts using a reliability-weighted controller.
- **model_family:** Q-learning
- **model_class:** PE learning / Belief updating
- **all_models_tested:** 1. {"name": "BSL0", "family": "Bayesian Sequence Learner (depth 0)", "n_params": 0, "metric": "Free Energy (BMS)"} 2. {"name": "BSL1", "family": "Bayesian Sequence Learner (depth 1)", "n_params": 0, "metric": "Free Energy (BMS)"} 3. {"name": "WSLS", "family": "Heuristic (Win-Stay/Lose-Switch)", "n_params": 0, "metric": "Free Energy (BMS)"} 4. {"name": "QL", "family": "Q-learning (reinforcement learning)", "n_params": 3, "metric": "Free Energy (BMS)"} 5. {"name": "MIIL", "family": "Mixed-Intentions Influence Learning", "n_params": 7, "metric": "Free Energy (BMS)"}  Note: Competitive and cooperative influence models were tested in supplementary analyses but failed the confusion analysis and were excluded from the main comparison.

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 192 (100 females; ages 3.4-9, M = 6.5, SD = 1.5). Subgroups: KG (n = 78, ages 3.4-6, M = 5.0, SD = 0.8, 40 females); EPS (n = 58, ages 6-7, M = 6.5, SD = 0.3, 30 females); MPS (n = 56, ages 8-9, M = 8.5, SD = 0.3, 28 females). Recruited from schools in Nanjing and Guangzhou, China.
- **population_category:** children
- **population_age_range:** 6–7
- **ecological_validity:** Moderate. The task is a controlled lab-based card game on tablets, which is somewhat ecologically valid for children given familiarity with tablet games. However, children were deceived into believing they played a peer (actually an artificial agent), and the alternating cooperative/competitive blocks are artificial. No real social interaction occurred.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** - Children were deceived about playing a peer; actual interaction was with an artificial agent — this is flagged but common in the literature - BSL0 and WSLS models have 0 free parameters, making direct comparison with the 7-parameter MIIL model via BMS potentially biased (though BMS penalizes complexity via Free Energy) - No explicit parameter recovery for the full MIIL model — the simulation-recovery analysis shown in Supp. Fig. 2b only recovered the two arbitration weight parameters, not all 7 parameters - Parameters were "underestimated" in recovery analysis (acknowledged by authors) - Cross-cultural generalizability unclear (all Chinese sample)
- **limitations_reported:** Not explicitly listed in a dedicated limitations section. However, discussion notes: competitive bias may have been fostered by the experimental setup placing children facing each other; the overrepresentation of BSL0 in younger groups might hide cooperative/competitive influence models; parameter recovery showed underestimation of parameters.
- **limitations_categorized:** Limited ecological validity; potential model confusability; parameter recovery limitations; possible demand characteristics from experimental setup; limited generalizability (single cultural sample)
