- **concerns:** High exclusion rates in online studies; different testing environments for clinical vs. control groups in Ch.6; small clinical samples in Ch.6; no physiological measures to test arousal hypothesis; same model/task used across all chapters with same priors — limited methodological diversity.  ## Other Notes  This is a doctoral dissertation from Oxford (supervisor: Molly Crockett) that consolidates ~3 years of work into a unified framework. The HGF model (from TAPAS toolbox by Mathys et al.) is consistently the winning model across all studies. The key social parameter is omega (tonic volatility), which is higher for bad agents, reflecting more uncertain/flexible beliefs about potentially threatening others. The dissertation makes a strong theoretical contribution linking Bayesian belief updating to forgiveness and adaptive social functioning, and extends this to clinical populations (BPD, violence exposure). No neuroimaging is included in the dissertation itself, though the General Discussion (Ch.7) discusses neural mechanisms (amygdala, mPFC) speculatively.
- **limitations_reported:** Many DTC patients taking psychotropic medication; did not assess childhood trauma; unclear if differences in prior expectations attributable to childhood experiences; DTC environment biased toward personality-disordered individuals; unclear if patients exposed to positive interaction partners in DTC; cross-sectional design for treatment effects.
- **limitations_categorized:** Small sample size; medication confound; no pre-treatment baseline; cross-sectional design; limited ecological validity; different testing environments; no childhood trauma assessment
