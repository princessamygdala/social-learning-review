## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 17 (all male; mean age 25.4 ± 3.7 years; 2 left-handed; within-subject crossover design OT vs. PL). N = 16 for neuroimaging (1 excluded for head motion). N = 15 for RL learning rate comparisons (2 excluded for α > 1).
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** M=25.4
- **ecological_validity:** The task uses an iterative Trust Game (20 rounds) which is more ecologically valid than single-shot trust games, as it models evolving social relationships. However, the "partner" is a computer algorithm (not a real human), limiting true social interaction. Monetary unit-based incentives are abstract. All male sample limits generalizability. The pharmacological manipulation (intranasal OT, 40 IU) is artificial.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Very small sample size (N = 17, reduced to N = 15 for key RL analyses). Within-subject crossover design helps power but days between sessions varied widely (1–71 days, median 7, mean 14). Two subjects excluded from RL analysis due to learning rates > 1, suggesting model fit issues. No formal model comparison between Bayesian and RL models — both used in parallel without arbitrating between them. The "Trustee" was a computer algorithm, not a real human — although subjects were deceived into believing it was human. Correction for multiple comparisons used 3DClustSim (p = 0.005, alpha = 0.05), which has known inflated false positive rates. All-male sample. No parameter recovery or model recovery reported.
- **limitations_reported:** Our findings are limited to men in a specific context in which cooperation is incentivized"; "Given the likelihood of sex-differences (Rilling et al., 2014)... two important future directions will be to see if our effects replicate for women, as well as for adversarial contexts"; "this study was not designed to compare social versus non-social learning. Thus, future studies are needed to delineate the effect, to determine if attenuation of learning persists in non-social contexts"; "this hypothesis... deserves further independent testing with larger sample sizes and both sexes
- **limitations_categorized:** limited generalizability (male-only sample); limited ecological validity (cooperation context only); task specificity (social vs. non-social learning not compared); sample size; no sex comparison

The key finding is that OT attenuates general reinforcement learning (reduced α) rather than specifically affecting trust or pro-social behavior. The supplement confirms DCM was also used for effective connectivity (BMS selected different winning models for PL vs. OT conditions), adding a third modeling layer. The supplement also confirms the study was a registered clinical trial (NCT01834261). The Trustee was a computer algorithm mimicking human behavior based on parameters from King-Casas et al.
