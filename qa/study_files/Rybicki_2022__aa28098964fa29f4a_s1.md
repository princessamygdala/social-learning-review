## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social reward learning in autism; comparing social vs. individual learning and adaptation to environmental volatility in autistic vs. non-autistic adults.
- **study_focus_short:** Social reward learning in autism
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from own reward outcomes and from others' choices about reward contingencies in a volatile environment   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self       - Source content (non-social): outcome (reward feedback -- blue/green indicator)     - Source type (social): group (previous participants' choices)       - Source content (social): action/policy (group's most popular choice represented by red frame)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world       - Target content (non-social): state (reward probability associated with blue/green shapes)     - Target type (social): group       - Target content (social): state (accuracy/reliability of group advice)
- **task_description:** Participants chose between a blue and green shape to gain points, receiving reward feedback indicating which shape was correct; a red frame representing the most popular choice of a group of previous participants provided additional social information whose accuracy also varied across stable and volatile phases.
- **task_paradigm:** Volatility task (Behrens)
- **players:** Single agent (participant), multi-target (group of previous participants, simulated)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** human (live)
- **stimuli:** Abstract shapes (blue/green boxes), red frame (social cue), blue/green feedback indicator
- **method:** behavioural
- **main_result:** - No group (ASD vs. CTRL) differences in learning rates for social or individual information (group x information: F(1,62) = 0.798, eta_p^2 = 0.013, BF_excl = 2.549) - No group differences in adaptation to volatility (group x volatility: F(1,62) = 0.867, eta_p^2 = 0.014, BF_excl = 3.989) - Significant main effect of information source on learning rates: alpha_individual significantly higher than alpha_social (F(1,62) = 67.111, eta_p^2 = 0.520) - No group difference in social weighting parameter zeta (F(1,62) = 1.302, eta_p^2 = 0.021, BF_excl = 2.252) - Significant group x index x volatility interaction for WSLS scores (F(1,62) = 4.936, eta_p^2 = 0.074), though Bayesian evidence was weak (BF_excl = 1.386) - Learning rates significantly differed from optimal for alpha_individual_volatile (d not reported for this), alpha_individual_stable, and alpha_social_volatile
- **effect_size:** eta_p^2 = 0.520 (information source main effect on alpha); eta_p^2 = 0.013 (group x information interaction); eta_p^2 = 0.074 (group x index x volatility WSLS interaction)

- **ecological_validity:** Low -- social information represented by an abstract red frame rather than faces or biological stimuli, deliberately avoiding face-processing confounds but reducing ecological validity; lab-based task with probabilistic reward structure.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** Very high male-to-female ratio (57 males, 7 females out of 64); groups not matched on age (significant difference); learning rates significantly deviated from optimal estimates, raising questions about whether participants were genuinely using RL strategy; the winning model (Model 4) showed more heterogeneity in the ASD group, where a simpler model (Model 1) was actually the best-fitting model within the ASD group, potentially obscuring group differences; WSLS interaction effect not supported by Bayesian analysis.
