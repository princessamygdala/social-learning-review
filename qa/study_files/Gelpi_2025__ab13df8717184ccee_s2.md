## Computational level
- **study_focus:** Social coordination learning; stereotype formation through behavioral confirmation; learning social conventions in multi-agent environments
- **study_focus_short:** Social coordination learning · stereotype formation through behavioral confirmation
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning to coordinate with a market decider whose predictions are shaped by group-level stereotypes; learning about social group structure from coordination outcomes   - Learning from:     - Source type (social): other (market decider) -- **joint** (coordination outcome depends on both agent and market)     - Source content (social): outcome -- **joint** (success/failure of coordination)   - Learning about:     - Target type (social): group (social category/group conventions)     - Target content (social): action/policy (what resource group members bring to market)
- **task_description:** Participants played as either an agent (collecting and selling resources to a market decider) or a market decider (predicting what resources agents would bring), interacting with pre-trained MARL agents/market from Experiment 2. Agent participants had skill specializations but faced market predictions that could match or conflict with their true skill; market participants predicted resources brought by agents with visible group-identity cues.
- **task_paradigm:** Coordination game
- **players:** Single agent (participant) interacting with automated market decider or automated agents; multi-target (300 agents with group-identity cues in market task)
- **n_players:** multi-target (3+)
- **partner_type:** computer (algorithmic)
- **stimuli:** Web-based game interface with colored aliens (purple, yellow, cyan body colors as group labels), 12-pixel unique nametags, resource trading (wood/stone)
- **method:** behavioural / online
- **main_result:** - Main Results:   - Agent task: Participants predicted to behave against-skill by market were less likely to bring skilled resources (B = -2.41, SE = 0.171, z = -14.12, OR = 0.09)   - Agent task: Against-skill behavior increased over trials (B = -1.22, z = -15.84)   - Market task: Participants made more skill-consistent predictions for majority than minority agents (B = 1.97, SE = 0.03, z = -63.25, OR = 7.17)   - Market task: Stereotypic predictions strengthened over trials (B = 0.23, SE = 0.03, z = 7.47, OR = 1.26)   - Participants overestimated group homogeneity (M = 67.1% believed shared skill, vs true ~50% after replacement)   - At Replacement 5: overestimation of shared skill M = 19.3% greater than truth (SE = 1.77, t(210) = 10.91)   - SJS and SDO did not predict stereotype-consistent behavior (SJS: b = -0.12, 100% in ROPE; SDO: b = 0.04, 100% in ROPE)   - BSJO reduced majority-minority distinction (b = 0.27, 0% in ROPE) but via higher majority error rates, not minority improvement
- **effect_size:** Agent task: OR = 0.09 (against-skill market prediction effect); Market task: OR = 7.17 (majority vs minority skill-consistent predictions); η²_p = 0.08 (BSJO on perceived fairness)
- **learning_from:** Joint (market decider and agent coordination outcomes); other (market decider predictions)
