- **study_focus:** Collective foraging; adaptive integration of asocial foraging (area-restricted search) and selective social learning (success-biased imitation)
- **study_focus_short:** Collective foraging
- **learning_mode_description:** - Learning mode: Learning from own foraging outcomes and observed success of others about where to forage next (spatial reward prediction + social imitation)   - Learning from:     - Source type (non-social): self       - Source content (non-social): outcome (individual foraging reward)     - Source type (social): other (visible co-foragers)       - Source content (social): outcome (observed reward splash)   - Learning about:     - Target type (non-social): world (spatial reward distribution)       - Target content (non-social): state (world state; spatial reward structure)     - Target type (social): other (successful co-foragers)       - Target content (social): action/policy (where successful others forage)

## Algorithmic level
- **winning_model:** ARS+Cond (Area-Restricted Search + Conditional Social Learning): P(Choice_{k+1}) proportional to exp(f . w), where w_tilde = w + alpha * Delta_t. Locality, Block Visibility, and Reward Prediction (Gaussian Process) as asocial features; Successful Proximity and Unsuccessful Proximity as social features. Dynamic weights: locality adapts by time since last individual reward (ARS); successful/unsuccessful proximity adapt by time since last individual reward (Conditional learning).
- **model_family:** Heuristic
- **model_class:** PE learning

## Quality
- **sample_size:** N = 128 (82 female; M_age = 27.4, SD = 5.0); foraged in groups of 4 (32 groups); within-subject design (solo vs. group x random vs. smooth); 16 rounds per participant
- **population_category:** healthy adults
- **population_age_range:** 
- **ecological_validity:** High relative to typical social learning tasks. Immersive Minecraft virtual environment with naturalistic spatial foraging, limited field of view creating trade-off between individual search and social observation, competitive resource depletion, high-resolution spatial trajectories and automated visual field transcription. Authors note computer-based modality was used instead of VR due to issues with teleportation locomotion and VR treadmills; future studies should strive for greater ecological validity with VR advances.
- **eligibility_flag:** 
- **concerns:** No standard sample size determination reported ("No statistical method was used to predetermine sample size"). Feature weights in dynamic models are z-standardized within each choice, making cross-study comparability of parameter magnitudes difficult. GP lengthscale was fixed (not estimated from data), though it matches the environment-generating process. Success-biased social learning persisted even in random environments where it was non-adaptive, suggesting potential limitations in adaptability. No explicit parameter recovery or model recovery analyses reported.
- **limitations_reported:** Success-biased social learning persisted in random environments where it was non-adaptive, suggesting limits to human adaptability or a lingering bias towards social learning; alternatively social imitation may serve as a computationally cheap exploration device; the asymmetry of social attention may serve as a safeguard against maladaptive herding; the study used a depleting reward environment and future work could use non-depleting environments; the task was originally designed for VR but switched to computer-based modality due to issues with teleportation locomotion and VR treadmill individual differences; the study focused on short timescale temporal dynamics and connecting to long cultural timescales remains future work
- **limitations_categorized:** limited generalizability (random environments); task simplicity (single depleting resource); limited ecological validity (computer-based instead of VR); short timescale only; potential floor/ceiling effects on adaptivity
